# Development Political Economy — Discipline, Capture, Exit, and the Playing Field

## Purpose

This note extends the gated-development mechanism without changing the minimal graduation theorem.

The core development sequence remains:

`activation -> capability accumulation -> graduation -> generativity -> accretion -> closure -> robustness`

The present note asks why the same temporary support can generate very different trajectories across firms, sectors, and countries.

---

## 1. Anticipated withdrawal and the incentive to learn

Temporary support changes capability only if firms have reason to expect that support will eventually be withdrawn or conditioned on performance.

The key distinction is therefore not simply

`support vs no support`,

but

`support with credible graduation discipline vs support with open-ended political continuation`.

If support is expected to end, a firm has an incentive to use the protected interval to change its unsupported viability conditions:

- raise productivity;
- improve quality;
- build supplier depth;
- acquire technology;
- enter external markets;
- accumulate capabilities;
- reduce dependence on the initial policy wedge.

In the language of the capability model, anticipated withdrawal should affect the effective conversion rate

`Lambda_e(Omega_t, expectations_t)`

and possibly the allocation of rents toward capability-producing investment.

A stylized distinction is:

`credible withdrawal -> rents used to raise future unsupported viability`

versus

`expected permanence -> rents can be used to preserve political protection itself`.

This is a proposed political-economy mechanism, not yet a theorem.

---

## 2. Protection creates rents; rents can finance capability or capture

Protection can create a wedge between protected domestic returns and unsupported competitive returns.

That wedge generates rents.

Those rents can be allocated approximately as:

`protection -> rents -> {productive capability investment, political capture}`.

This creates two possible feedback loops.

### Developmental loop

`support -> rent -> productive investment -> q_e rises / q*_e falls -> graduation becomes easier -> support can be withdrawn`.

### Capture loop

`support -> rent -> political influence -> support becomes harder to withdraw -> weak pressure to graduate -> dependence on support persists`.

Thus protection is not merely a technological bridge. It creates a political constituency whose incentives depend on whether the value of future unsupported viability exceeds the value of preserving the rent-producing policy.

The central policy question becomes:

> Does support finance the conditions of its own disappearance, or the political machinery of its continuation?

---

## 3. Credible withdrawal as a discipline mechanism

A graduation regime requires more than a formal sunset clause.

Withdrawal must be sufficiently credible that firms cannot rationally base strategy on indefinite continuation of support.

External performance tests can strengthen this credibility by providing observables that are difficult to satisfy through domestic political bargaining alone.

Examples of possible tests include:

- export performance;
- productivity growth;
- quality standards;
- cost convergence;
- domestic supplier development;
- external customer acquisition;
- technology upgrading.

The conceptual mechanism is:

`temporary support + credible withdrawal + observable performance test -> stronger incentive to convert current rents into future capability`.

South Korea is a candidate empirical case for this mechanism, not proof of it.

---

## 4. Stability of the playing field is not constancy of policy

Development requires a stable rule governing change, not an unchanging policy environment.

A playing field can remain stable while:

- tariffs decline;
- firms enter and exit;
- sectors lose support;
- new technologies displace old ones;
- labor and capital move;
- industries shrink or disappear.

The relevant distinction is:

`policy stability != policy constancy`.

A stable developmental field is one in which actors can form expectations about how rules respond to performance, time, failure, and technological change.

Thus discipline and predictability can coexist with substantial structural transformation.

This is especially important for the capability model because expected policy reversals can raise effective capability decay, while credible transition rules can lower uncertainty around investment in graduation.

---

## 5. Positive and negative linkage dynamics

The baseline monotone model uses nonnegative complementarity because it isolates the formation mechanism.

A complete developmental model must eventually permit negative propagation.

A relation can improve another relation's viability:

`e active -> supplier demand rises -> f viability rises`,

but it can also reduce another relation's viability through:

- competition for labor;
- competition for capital;
- foreign-exchange scarcity;
- demand displacement;
- import competition;
- exchange-rate effects;
- supplier collapse;
- congestion;
- de-accretion.

A concrete negative propagation mechanism is:

`e exits -> supplier/customer viability falls -> f exits -> further linked exits`.

This is the negative counterpart to the green-wave mechanism.

The core monotone theorem should not absorb arbitrary signed interactions prematurely. Instead:

1. retain a positive-complementarity baseline for the formation/graduation result;
2. state the scope restriction explicitly;
3. introduce signed net effects or a separate competitive/resource layer in an extension.

---

## 6. Exit is not a single developmental event

The framework should not equate survival with success or exit with failure.

At minimum, distinguish four exit types.

### Failed graduation

A supported activity exits because it never accumulated enough unsupported capability.

`support removed -> q_e < q*_e -> collapse`.

### Destructive de-accretion

A previously viable relation exits because key complements, markets, infrastructure, or supporting relations disappear.

`network deterioration -> viability falls -> exit`.

### Competitive displacement

A relation exits because another activity or technology outcompetes it, potentially with ambiguous developmental consequences.

### Successful supersession

An activity exits because labor, capital, skills, and complementary capability have migrated into higher-value or more generative uses.

In this case:

`industry exit != capability destruction`.

The relevant diagnostic is therefore not merely whether a firm or sector survives, but what happens to the accumulated capability stock and the surrounding opportunity field after exit.

---

## 7. Firm discipline and human transition

A developmental regime may require hard performance discipline for firms while preserving real transition opportunities for workers and communities.

This is conceptually distinct from permanently protecting incumbent firms.

The desired structure is approximately:

`firm can fail / sector can shrink / technology can be superseded`

while

`workers retain agency, retraining options, mobility, social protection, and access to new productive relations`.

This belongs primarily in the policy/welfare and normative layer rather than the core graduation theorem.

It provides a potential bridge to FTLtauA: discipline can be applied to organizations without treating people as disposable inputs.

---

## 8. Revised Paper F political-economy mechanism

The fuller mechanism is now:

`stable rule of change`

`+ temporary support`

`+ credible withdrawal`

`+ external or otherwise hard performance test`

`+ productive use of rents`

`+ capture resistance`

`+ capability accumulation`

`+ graduation`

`+ positive and negative linkage dynamics`

`+ differentiated exit`

`+ generativity / reallocation`

This is not intended to become one giant theorem.

The preferred division of labor is:

- **core formal result:** network-conditioned graduation under capability accumulation;
- **political-economy extension:** expectations, credible withdrawal, rent allocation, capture, and discipline;
- **dynamic-network extension:** positive and negative linkage propagation, de-accretion, and exit cascades;
- **policy/welfare extension:** hard discipline for firms plus real transition opportunities for people;
- **empirical section:** South Korea, Argentina, and other cases used to discriminate mechanisms rather than prove them.

---

## 9. New research questions

1. How should expected support duration enter `Lambda_e` or the firm's investment problem?
2. Under what conditions are protection rents allocated to capability accumulation rather than capture?
3. Can a credible external performance test reduce the political value of preserving support?
4. What rule of policy change is stable enough to induce investment while flexible enough to permit structural transformation?
5. How should capability persistence after firm exit be represented?
6. When is exit evidence of failed graduation versus successful supersession?
7. How can negative linkage propagation be added without losing all tractability from the monotone baseline?
8. Can a de-accretion operator provide the dynamic bridge from Paper F into Paper E's robustness framework?

The architecture remains defeasible. Historical country cases are tests of these mechanisms, not premises of the theory.
