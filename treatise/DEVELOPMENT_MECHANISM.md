# Development as a Gated Propagation Process

## Working synthesis

Development is provisionally defined here as the process by which an economy becomes able to activate, learn from, graduate, and reproduce an expanding range of productive relations.

The sequence is:

`possibility -> effective connectivity -> activation -> capability accumulation -> graduation -> generativity -> accretion -> reproductive closure -> robustness -> adaptive embeddedness`

Each arrow is a gate. Passing one gate does not imply passage through the next.

---

## 1. Possibility and activation

A factory, technology, skill, or piece of infrastructure is not by itself a productive relation.

A relation must couple capability to productive use:

`supplier <-> buyer`

`technology <-> adopter`

`worker <-> productive organization`

`specialized input <-> user`

Activation may require contemporaneous coordination among complements. Higher feasible configurations can exist without being reachable from bottom-up activation alone.

---

## 2. Capability conversion

Let `q_e,t` denote durable productive capability accumulated around relation `e`.

A minimal dynamic is

`q_(e,t+1) = (1-delta_e) q_(e,t) + x_(e,t) Lambda_e(Omega_t)`.

`Lambda_e(Omega_t)` is the rate at which operating the relation today creates durable capability for tomorrow.

`delta_e` is the rate at which accumulated capability is lost.

The same physical activity can therefore have very different developmental consequences in different productive environments.

Potential determinants of `Lambda` include:

- learning-by-doing and repetition;
- absorptive capacity;
- customer feedback and standards;
- supplier sophistication;
- access to machinery and technology;
- export-market discipline and scale;
- finance and organizational continuity;
- infrastructure and logistics;
- external productive embeddedness.

Potential determinants of `delta` include:

- firm destruction and repeated crises;
- brain drain;
- supplier-network collapse;
- technological obsolescence;
- organizational discontinuity;
- unstable institutions or policy regimes.

---

## 3. Graduation

Suppose unsupported viability requires capability threshold `q*_e`.

A useful normalized graduation margin is

`gamma_e = Lambda_e / (delta_e q*_e)`.

In the simplest constant-parameter model:

- if `gamma_e < 1`, even indefinite supported operation cannot reach unsupported viability;
- if `gamma_e > 1`, finite-time graduation is possible.

Therefore:

> **Time under protection is not learning.**

The relevant question is how effectively supported operation converts into durable unsupported capability.

Infrastructure, market access, and global productive-network access can affect graduation by raising `Lambda`, lowering `q*`, lowering `delta`, or several at once.

---

## 4. Network-conditioned graduation

Graduation is not purely a property of a sector or technology.

The same prospective industry can face very different graduation conditions depending on surrounding productive structure:

`gamma_e = gamma_e(G_t, omega_t, q_t, institutions, logistics, demand, technology access, ...)`.

A productive network can therefore shorten required support duration or make graduation possible where it otherwise would not be.

This suggests a **graduation frontier**:

`G_t = {e : gamma_(e,t) > 1}`.

This is the set of productive relations that, if successfully activated and operated, can in principle become self-sustaining under the current productive environment.

A possible development-level interpretation is:

- **low-development regime:** small graduation frontier; many activities can be activated only under continuing exceptional support;
- **middle-development regime:** islands of graduation exist, but successful sectors weakly expand the frontier around them;
- **high-development regime:** graduated activities systematically improve the graduation conditions of further activities, expanding the frontier endogenously.

This is a hypothesis, not yet an established classification.

---

## 5. Generativity and the green wave

A graduated relation is generative when it improves the graduation conditions of another relation `f`, for example by

`Lambda_f up`,

`q*_f down`,

`delta_f down`,

or reducing activation barriers.

A developmental green wave therefore has the form

`e graduates -> gamma_f rises -> f becomes graduable -> f graduates -> gamma_g rises -> ...`

The propagated object is not merely output. It is improved conditions for the creation and graduation of further productive relations.

---

## 6. Global productive embeddedness

The productive boundary is relational rather than political.

An economy can access external demand, suppliers, capital goods, finance, standards, technology, and knowledge. External access can increase learning rates and lower unsupported viability thresholds.

Therefore:

> **Development may require access to completeness, not domestic completeness.**

A small country can participate in an enormous effective productive field if its accessibility to dense external networks is high.

This motivates the distinction:

> **Market access supplies demand; productive-network access supplies possibilities.**

And:

`trade exposure != productive embeddedness`.

---

## 7. Effective productive distance

Physical geography matters through the productive relations it enables or obstructs, not by distance alone.

A location may be physically remote but productively close if transport, ports, standards, customs, finance, information, and buyer/supplier networks collapse effective distance.

Conversely, a geographically nearer location can be productively distant if those channels are unreliable.

This is why Australia and New Zealand reject a crude `distance -> underdevelopment` hypothesis.

A development strategy should ask not only **what industry?** but

> **what industry, where, connected to which suppliers, serving which users, through which logistics and knowledge network, and what becomes easier to build next?**

---

## 8. Industrial-policy diagnostic

For any proposed developmental intervention ask:

1. **Activation:** What productive relation is being made viable?
2. **Capability conversion:** What will operating it teach, build, retain, or connect?
3. **Graduation:** Why should it eventually survive without extraordinary support?
4. **Generativity:** What further productive relations become easier to activate or graduate because it succeeds?
5. **Closure and robustness:** What dependence remains on thin anchors, suppliers, markets, or policy channels?

A policy can succeed at the gate it targets while damaging later gates.

Protection can support activation while reducing access to imported technology or external markets, thereby slowing capability accumulation and graduation.

Liberalization can remove support before accumulated capability and network access are sufficient for survival.

The relevant distinction is therefore not `protection vs openness`, but whether the intervention advances a viable staged sequence.

---

## 9. Paper F / Paper E handoff

Paper F asks how productive organization forms and propagates:

`activation -> capability accumulation -> graduation -> generativity`.

Paper E asks whether the resulting network closes and survives:

`accretion -> reproductive closure -> robustness`.

Adaptive embeddedness links the two dynamically by asking whether productive closure can be preserved or reconstructed as external networks change.

---

## Open formal questions

1. Define the smallest defensible `Lambda_e(Omega_t)` structure.
2. Derive the minimum-support-duration theorem under network complementarity.
3. Define unsupported dependency closure to prevent false graduation through permanently supported neighbors.
4. Determine how network structure changes `q*_e`, `Lambda_e`, and `delta_e`.
5. Formalize the graduation frontier and conditions under which it expands endogenously.
6. Map graduated/generative relations into Paper E's weighted network object.
7. Add scarcity/competition without destroying all useful tractability, or state the positive-complementarity baseline explicitly as a restricted formation model.

The architecture remains defeasible. Country cases such as Argentina, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Chile, Saudi Arabia, and DRC are diagnostics and candidate empirical tests, not proof of the mechanism.
