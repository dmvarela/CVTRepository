# Treatise Architecture — Working Map

## 0. Governing question

How do relations create, preserve, regenerate, consume, damage, repair, and reproduce the conditions of their own future viability?

The treatise is organized around transitions in relational viability rather than around disciplinary boundaries.

---

## I. Relations and viability

**Question:** What is the basic object?

A relation is not treated as a passive connection between already-complete nodes. Relations can alter the constituents, opportunity sets, constraints, and future viability of the systems participating in them.

Key distinctions:

- visible persistence vs. self-maintaining viability;
- present output vs. future relational capacity;
- relation maintained by external support vs. relation maintaining itself;
- constituent preservation vs. constituent consumption.

**Status:** foundational conceptual layer; overlaps with existing relational-viability traditions and must remain literature-disciplined.

---

## II. Formation and coupled activation

**Question:** How does a latent relation become viable?

A productive or cooperative relation may require complementary sides to become viable together. Feasibility of a higher configuration does not imply decentralized realization.

Core objects:

- supply/readiness constraints;
- demand/adoption constraints;
- contemporaneous complementarity;
- minimal/maximal self-consistent activation configurations;
- coordination gap.

**Status:** proposed reduced-form formation mechanism; Tarski/monotone-operator mathematics is inherited.

---

## III. Capability accumulation

**Question:** What changes while an activated relation operates?

Let `q_e,t` denote a reduced-form stock of durable relation-specific productive capability. A minimal dynamic is

`q_(e,t+1) = (1-delta_e) q_(e,t) + x_(e,t) Lambda_e(Omega_t)`.

`Lambda_e` is the conversion rate from operating today into durable capability tomorrow. `delta_e` is capability loss/decay.

Possible determinants of `Lambda` include:

- repetition and learning-by-doing;
- absorptive capacity;
- demanding users and feedback;
- supplier sophistication;
- technology and capital-good access;
- external productive embeddedness;
- finance and organizational continuity;
- recombination of accumulated capabilities.

**Status:** central proposed mechanism; requires formal and empirical discipline.

---

## IV. Graduation

**Question:** When does an initially supported relation become viable without extraordinary support?

If unsupported viability requires `q_e >= q*_e`, then a useful reduced-form graduation margin is

`gamma_e = Lambda_e / (delta_e q*_e)`.

Under the simplest constant-parameter accumulation model:

- `gamma_e < 1`: indefinite supported operation cannot reach unsupported viability;
- `gamma_e > 1`: finite-time graduation is possible.

Important diagnostic:

> Time under protection is not learning.

Support succeeds developmentally only if operating under support changes the productive state enough to create a path out of support.

Graduation must not be falsely inherited from neighbors that remain permanently supported. Unsupported viability should be tested against an unsupported supporting configuration or dependency closure.

**Status:** strongest current candidate for a nontrivial Paper F formal result: network-conditioned graduation / minimum support duration under complementarity.

---

## V. Generativity

**Question:** What becomes easier to create because a graduated relation now exists?

A relation is generative when its operation or accumulated capability improves the graduation conditions of other latent relations, for example by

- raising `Lambda_f`;
- lowering `q*_f`;
- lowering activation thresholds;
- lowering capability decay;
- creating new accessible complements.

The core developmental transition is not merely output reproduction but reproduction of productive possibility.

**Status:** proposed mechanism; overlaps with Hirschman linkages, product-space/economic-complexity adjacency, spillovers, and endogenous network formation.

---

## VI. Accretion

**Question:** How do graduated/generative relations become part of a higher-order organized productive system?

Distinguish:

- attachment/transmission;
- topological accretion;
- reproductive accretion;
- robust consolidation.

Nucleation differs from accretion:

- **nucleation:** subcritical pieces jointly cross a reproductive threshold;
- **accretion:** a newcomer joins an already reproductive core.

**Status:** Paper E / reproductive-accretion layer.

---

## VII. Reproductive closure

**Question:** When does reciprocal coupling create a higher-order reproductive system?

For two subcritical blocks

`B(alpha,beta) = [[A, alpha P], [beta Q, D]]`

with `rho(A)<1`, `rho(D)<1`, define

`T = (I-A)^(-1) P (I-D)^(-1) Q`

and `Gamma = rho(T)`.

The established threshold is

`rho(B) <,=,> 1  iff  alpha beta Gamma <,=,> 1`.

Interpretive distinction:

> A chain transmits. Reciprocal coupling closes. Sufficient round-trip gain reproduces.

**Status:** mathematically mature core of Paper E; underlying positive-systems / Perron-complement machinery is inherited.

---

## VIII. Robustness

**Question:** If the system reproduces, how dependent is that reproduction on a thin set of relations?

Key distinctions:

- reproductive strength vs. robustness;
- edge-count/cardinality robustness vs. weighted perturbation robustness;
- local Perron sensitivity vs. finite deletion robustness;
- many cycles vs. many independent closure paths.

The bounded-degree diamond construction shows that bounded degree, fixed spectral margin, nonvanishing edge weights, and exponentially many cycles can coexist with one-edge failure of reproduction.

**Status:** Paper E; explicit construction is a modest technical contribution around a known phenomenon.

---

## IX. Adaptive embeddedness

**Question:** Can a viable system preserve or reconstruct productive closure when its external network changes?

Political boundaries need not coincide with productive boundaries. An economy may borrow productive thickness from external networks through demand, suppliers, finance, technology, standards, logistics, and knowledge.

Key distinctions:

- trade exposure vs. productive embeddedness;
- domestic completeness vs. access to completeness;
- embeddedness vs. redundant embeddedness;
- static embeddedness vs. adaptive re-embedding.

A useful notion is effective productive distance: physical distance filtered through logistics, reliability, standards, institutions, finance, and network accessibility.

**Status:** emerging bridge between Papers F and E; requires substantial literature and empirical review.

---

## X. Applications

Applications should be treated as tests or instantiations of the architecture, not as proof of the general theory.

Candidate application domains include:

- development economics and industrial policy;
- institutions, authority, and cooperation;
- robotics and agency-bearing systems;
- membranes/boundaries and recoverability;
- AI continuity and relational design;
- biological/ecological analogies only where mechanisms transfer cleanly.

Country comparisons (Argentina, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, DRC, Chile, Saudi Arabia, etc.) belong here as empirical diagnostics, not as premises.

---

## Current paper handoff

**Paper F:** formation -> activation -> capability accumulation -> graduation -> generativity.

**Paper E:** accretion -> reproductive closure -> robustness.

The open mathematical bridge is a map from a graduated/generative linkage state in F into the weighted network object analyzed in E.

---

## Research discipline

For every new result or analogy ask:

1. Which transition does it bear on?
2. Is it a theorem, inherited result, mechanism, empirical hypothesis, analogy, or open question?
3. Does it change the architecture, or merely instantiate it?
4. What observation or counterexample would make us revise it?

The architecture does not certify itself.
