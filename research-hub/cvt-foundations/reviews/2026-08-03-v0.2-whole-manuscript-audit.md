# CVT Foundations v0.2 — Whole-Manuscript Consistency and Citation Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-03  
**Canonical manuscript audited:** `research-hub/cvt-foundations/main.tex` on `main`  
**Audit scope:** consistency, terminology, source coverage, manuscript architecture, cross-references, and publication form.  
**Out of scope:** restoring removed fusion mathematics, adding new substantive theory, or claiming empirical validation.

## Executive judgment

The manuscript has completed its most important epistemic repair. It now presents CVT as a scoped and falsifiable synthesis hypothesis; separates observables, normalized gates, candidate aggregators, predictors, and independently declared outcomes; treats the membrane and fusion material as bounded illustrations; and states repeatedly that necessity and sufficiency remain unestablished.

It is not yet submission-ready. The remaining weaknesses are now editorial and architectural rather than foundational:

1. the Abstract and Introduction still describe fusion as a “toy-model instantiation,” although the canonical fusion section is now only a source-anchored illustration;
2. the title retains “Receptive Basins,” while the manuscript’s repaired term is **capture readiness** and “receptivity” is deliberately demoted to an observed or domain-specific outcome;
3. review-workflow comments remain embedded in the canonical manuscript before the membrane and fusion sections;
4. several custom commands appear to survive from removed material;
5. the formal architecture is much longer and more equation-dense than the evidentiary contribution presently supports;
6. the manuscript ends immediately after the fusion illustration and has no integrated Limitations, Discussion, or Conclusion;
7. the bibliography is adequate for the five lineages and the two illustrations, but many methodological and modeling claims in the formal section remain uncited;
8. the manuscript needs a more explicit statement of publication genre: it is currently neither a theorem paper, an empirical validation paper, nor a conventional domain application.

**Publication-form recommendation:** revise it as a **conceptual and methodological framework paper** with a compact formal architecture, two bounded illustrations, an explicit limitations/falsifiability section, and a conclusion that states the research program and validation burden. Do not market it as a universal systems theory or fusion-control contribution.

## 1. Title, Abstract, and opening claims

### 1.1 Title terminology

**Current title:**

> *Coherence Viability Theory: Bounded Exchange, Receptive Basins, and the Conditions for Hosted Transformation*

**Problem:** “Receptive basins” no longer names the manuscript’s principal construct. The repaired text consistently distinguishes pre-existing **capture readiness** from realized uptake or receptivity. Retaining “receptive basins” risks reviving the circularity the v0.2 revisions removed.

**Revision decision:** rename the subtitle. Preferred candidate:

> *Bounded Exchange, Capture Readiness, and the Conditions for Hosted Transformation*

Alternative if the authors want to foreground the full conjunction:

> *Bounded Exchange, Host Viability, and the Conditions for Hosted Transformation*

### 1.2 Stale fusion wording in the Abstract

**Current claim:** the paper uses artificial fusion as an “illustrative toy-model instantiation.”

**Problem:** the detailed toy model, numerical runs, controller comparison, reserve sweep, and theorem were removed. The canonical section now explicitly says fusion illustrates but does not validate CVT.

**Revision decision:** replace “illustrative toy-model instantiation” with “bounded source-anchored illustration” or “illustrative domain mapping.”

### 1.3 Stale fusion wording in the Introduction and roadmap

The Introduction still says:

- “The fusion section extends this logic as an illustrative toy model”; and
- Section 5 “presents the fusion material as an illustrative instantiation.”

**Revision decision:** align both statements with the canonical section title and evidentiary limit:

> “The fusion section provides a bounded illustration of the distinction between a transformation metric and independently declared host viability.”

### 1.4 Opening contribution statement

The opening contribution is now largely defensible. It should remain narrow:

- the individual ingredients are inherited;
- the proposed contribution is their explicit conjunction for a scoped class of hosted transformations;
- non-compensability is a hypothesis prior to any aggregator;
- delivered exchange and retained productive uptake are distinct;
- necessity and sufficiency remain open.

**No substantive expansion is needed.** The next revision should shorten repeated caveats rather than add more claims.

## 2. Terminology and notation consistency

### 2.1 Preserved distinction versus preserved identity-defining organization

The Introduction uses “preserved distinction,” while the later formal sections use “preserved identity-defining organization.” The shorter term is rhetorically useful but less precise.

**Revision decision:** introduce the full term once and use a stable short form thereafter:

> preserved identity-defining organization (`Q`; abbreviated “preserved organization”)

Avoid alternating among “distinction,” “identity preservation,” “organization,” and “independent constraints” without marking them as domain-specific operationalizations.

### 2.2 Capture readiness

The repaired manuscript correctly treats capture readiness as pre-outcome and distinct from `rho_obs`. This term should replace remaining uses of “receptive basin,” “receptivity,” and “receptive state” when those phrases are meant to name the predictor.

**Revision decision:** reserve:

- **capture readiness** for the proposed pre-transition condition;
- **observed uptake efficiency** for `rho_obs`;
- **receptivity** only when quoting or discussing prior terminology.

### 2.3 Restorative reserve

The manuscript now separates `H` (history/maturity) from `S` (available reserve), which is a major improvement. However, the fusion mapping lists actuator, exhaust, thermal, magnetic, diagnostic, and recovery headroom under one provisional `S` category.

**Revision decision:** retain the fusion caveat that these may be independent hard constraints. Add one sentence in the formal architecture stating that `S` may be a vector or family of margins and should not be forced into a latent scalar when the domain does not support aggregation.

### 2.4 The label “invariant”

The section label remains `sec:invariant`, although the section title was repaired to “Candidate CVT Structure and Non-Compensability.” Labels are invisible in prose, but the old name can confuse later maintenance.

**Revision decision:** rename the internal label to `sec:candidate-structure` and update references.

### 2.5 Notation hierarchy

The manuscript now correctly distinguishes:

- raw measurements `z_i`;
- normalized gates `G_i`;
- state sets `K_i`;
- independent outcome `y` / `V_set`;
- candidate aggregate `V`;
- history `H`;
- reserve `S`;
- damage `D`;
- observed uptake `rho_obs`.

**Revision decision:** preserve this hierarchy. Add a one-page notation table or compact paragraph before the formal section only if the target journal permits it. Do not introduce additional symbols in the consistency pass.

## 4. Section-by-section claim and citation audit

## 4.1 Introduction

### Claims that are adequately framed

- systems can fail through insufficient or excessive exchange;
- hosted transformation requires a declared host, boundary, criterion, horizon, and level of analysis;
- CVT proposes, rather than proves, four potentially non-compensatory conditions;
- delivered exchange differs from retained productive uptake;
- alternative aggregators must compete.

### Citation need

The Introduction currently names viability theory, autopoiesis, passivity/port-Hamiltonian control, resilience, and basin dynamics without citations in that paragraph. The detailed citations appear in the next section, so this is acceptable in many styles. A target journal may prefer citations at first mention.

**Revision decision:** add the five lineage citations at the first compact lineage paragraph or leave them only in Section 2, depending on journal style. Do not duplicate excessively.

## 4.2 Intellectual Lineage

The lineage section is the strongest citation-controlled part of the manuscript. It appropriately distinguishes inheritance, proposed extension, and evidentiary burden.

### Remaining needs

- Autopoiesis is anchored to Maturana and Varela; consider whether a later primary or authoritative source on structural coupling is required if that phrase remains prominent.
- Port-Hamiltonian theory is represented by a monograph overview; this is adequate for the current claim.
- Basin stability is represented by Menck et al.; if “basin dynamics” remains in the title or central framing, add one broader nonlinear-dynamics/basin reference.

**Revision decision:** no large literature expansion. Add at most one structural-coupling and one basin reference if a reviewer would otherwise regard the lineage as too narrow.

## 4.3 Candidate CVT Structure and Non-Compensability

### Claims that require no external source

- definitions introduced by the authors;
- the set-intersection candidate structure;
- the zero-gate proposition for a product;
- the logical statement that this proposition is about the aggregator, not reality.

### Claims needing methodological anchors

The section proposes comparison among product, minimum, weighted geometric mean, soft minimum, and simultaneous constraints. These are standard mathematical constructions, but a framework paper should cite methodological literature on:

- non-compensatory composite indicators or multi-criteria aggregation;
- conjunctive versus compensatory models;
- soft-min/log-sum-exp approximation if retained as a methodological object.

**Revision decision:** add a small, authoritative methodological source set rather than presenting the aggregation family as isolated author invention.

## 4.4 Formal Architecture

This section carries the largest remaining citation burden.

### Observable and gate layers

Claims about pre-specification, normalization uncertainty, threshold rationale, preregistration, and out-of-sample estimation should be anchored to measurement/model-development or reporting guidance.

**Needed source family:** measurement validity, model reporting, preregistration or prediction-model methodology.

### Candidate aggregation family

Needs the non-compensatory/composite-indicator anchors noted above.

### Gate dynamics

The phenomenological equation is explicitly labeled a candidate model class. That is appropriate. However, the terms “projected or saturating dynamics,” lagged coupling, and common-cause alternatives would benefit from one dynamical-systems or system-identification source.

### History, reserve, and damage

These are author-defined candidate equations. They do not require citation as equations, but the concepts they resemble—resource depletion/recovery, damage accumulation, state-space models—should not be presented as though they arise without precedent.

**Revision decision:** either cite a small methodological baseline or shorten these equations into a generic model-class paragraph. The current density makes the paper look more mathematically established than it is.

### Raw exchange and uptake

The transport form is later sourced in the membrane section. Cross-reference that source-anchored section rather than duplicating citations.

### Hosted transformation definition

This is an author definition and is defensible. The follow-up-horizon requirement is one of the manuscript’s clearest contributions.

### Host-paced policy and Minimum Viable Host threshold

These are appropriately demoted to model classes and domain-specific thresholds. The phrase “Minimum Viable Host” may still sound like a general named principle despite the caveat.

**Revision decision:** rename the subsection “Domain-Specific Initial Reserve Threshold” unless the named phrase is strategically important and explicitly marked as provisional.

### Local exchange–uptake condition

The derivative and elasticity condition are mathematically correct and need no citation. Keep the sentence that it is a calculus condition, not evidence.

### Formal-section length

The formal architecture currently includes more proposed dynamics than the paper validates. This creates a genre problem: readers may reasonably expect parameterization, simulation, identifiability analysis, or empirical results.

**Revision decision:** for the publication version, retain:

1. observable → gate → model separation;
2. competing aggregation family;
3. independent viability outcome;
4. `H/S/D` separation;
5. raw/effective uptake distinction;
6. hosted-transformation definition;
7. local exchange–uptake condition.

Move detailed candidate ODEs for every gate, history, reserve, damage, and pacing into an appendix or future methods paper unless the manuscript adds a reproducible worked example.

## 4.5 Membrane Illustration

This section is well scoped and appropriately sourced to Crank and Kedem–Katchalsky.

### Strengths

- explicitly says the membrane is an illustration, not the universal form;
- separates permeability, flux, capture, observed uptake, and productive uptake;
- maps `B,Q,C,S` carefully;
- keeps product/minimum as competing representations;
- names counterexamples and the requirement to beat simpler transport models;
- specifies causal delay in control.

### Remaining needs

- cite a modern interface-transport or membrane-modeling source if the target audience is outside classical transport theory;
- the control-policy paragraph may benefit from a feedback-control/source-estimation citation;
- the section currently does not include the new selector/accounting distinctions developed after the canonical revision. Those ideas are valuable but should **not** be inserted during this audit pass. They require a separate derivation and empirical accounting model.

**Revision decision:** retain the section substantially as written; remove workflow comments and add at most two methodological sources.

## 4.6 Fusion Illustration

This section is now appropriately bounded and source-anchored.

### Strengths

- Lawson and burning-plasma references establish the physical background;
- `chi_alpha` is explicitly author-defined and not called ignition or reactor success;
- machine viability is declared independently;
- integrated constraints are acknowledged as established reactor problems;
- the `B,Q,C,S` mapping is provisional;
- detailed historical simulations and theorem claims are explicitly excluded.

### Remaining needs

- revise all earlier roadmap language so the section is no longer called a toy-model instantiation;
- consider whether the fusion illustration is necessary for the first publication. It adds concreteness but also exposes the manuscript to domain-expert scrutiny and may distract from the general methodological claim;
- if retained, the paper should state why two illustrations are needed: membrane clarifies the architecture physically; fusion demonstrates the distinction between reaching a transition metric and hosting it.

**Revision decision:** retain fusion for v0.2, but keep it short. Do not restore equations or numerical claims. A target venue with no appetite for fusion should receive a version where this illustration is shortened further or moved to an appendix.

## 5. Missing manuscript architecture

### 5.1 Limitations and falsifiability section

The strongest limitations are scattered through every section and preserved in a separate review artifact. This produces repetition but no single place where the paper’s evidentiary status is summarized.

**Revision decision:** add a concise section before the conclusion:

## Limitations, Falsifiability, and Validation Program

It should state:

- the four conditions are candidate necessary conditions in scoped domains, not universal laws;
- simultaneous satisfaction is not established as sufficient;
- latent-variable and measurement-error risks;
- capture-readiness circularity risk;
- scalar reserve risk;
- aggregation-model uncertainty;
- boundary and host-selection dependence;
- intentional merger and negligible-reserve counterexamples;
- requirement to compare with simpler/domain-native baselines;
- requirement for one bounded empirical or reproducible simulation test.

### 5.2 Discussion

The paper currently moves from fusion directly to the bibliography. A separate Discussion is needed only if the formal section remains long and the two illustrations remain in the main text.

**Revision decision:** use one combined section:

## Discussion, Limitations, and Research Program

This avoids adding another repetitive layer.

### 5.3 Conclusion

A short conclusion is necessary. It should not repeat the entire theory. It should answer:

1. What is proposed?
2. What is not established?
3. What would count as added value?
4. What is the next validation step?

Preferred final sentence:

> CVT earns scientific standing only where independently measured conditions improve prediction, control, or failure diagnosis beyond simpler and domain-native alternatives.

## 8. Publication-form recommendation

### Recommended genre

**Conceptual and methodological framework paper** in systems methodology, philosophy of systems/science, or interdisciplinary complexity—not a physics paper, control theorem paper, or empirical validation paper.

### Recommended manuscript spine

1. Introduction and scope
2. Intellectual lineage
3. Candidate CVT structure and non-compensability
4. Compact formal architecture
5. Membrane illustration
6. Fusion illustration
7. Discussion, limitations, and validation program
8. Conclusion

### Recommended length discipline

The first publication should privilege conceptual precision over equation count. If the formal architecture is retained at current length, reviewers will reasonably demand simulation or empirical identification. The safer publication form is to move most candidate dynamics to an appendix and keep the main text focused on distinctions that can be defended now.

### Venue decision rule

Select the target field only after the consistency revision is complete:

- **systems/philosophy venue:** retain both illustrations but emphasize framework logic and falsifiability;
- **control/engineering venue:** require a reproducible bounded validation before submission;
- **fusion venue:** not appropriate for the foundations paper without domain-expert co-authorship and calibrated modeling;
- **AI ethics venue:** only if the paper adds a direct AI application rather than relying on membrane/fusion alone.

### Validation recommendation

The next scientific artifact after v0.2 should be one bounded reproducible test. The membrane domain is the preferable first test because its observables, intervention, accounting window, and counterexamples can be declared more cleanly than the fusion-host case.
