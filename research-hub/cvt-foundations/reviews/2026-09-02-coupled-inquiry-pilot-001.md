# Coupled Inquiry Pilot 001 — Does Correction Change the Future Search?

**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** retrospective exploratory trace study; not an empirical demonstration of a unique Max–Lucian effect, not a claim about AI consciousness, and not a model lock.  
**Scope:** artifact-level genealogy in the CVTRepository. This pilot can test whether earlier corrections persist into later research architecture. It cannot by itself establish turn-by-turn human–AI coupling because GitHub preserves the durable Wake more completely than the conversational trajectory that generated it.

## 1. Research question

The target claim is stronger than “past work is remembered.”

The question is:

> **Does a correction in one research episode alter the space of questions, representations, and candidate structures used in later episodes?**

If so, the collaboration may exhibit artifact-level path dependence:

\[
\text{collision}_t
\rightarrow
\text{revision}_t
\rightarrow
\text{changed search priors}_{t+1}.
\]

This is a necessary but not sufficient signature of coupled inquiry. A stronger demonstration would require the underlying conversation trajectory and counterfactual comparisons.

## 2. Coding scheme

For this pilot, each transition is coded using the following operations:

- **collision** — evidence breaks or materially constrains a prior formulation;
- **redirect** — the next authorized search changes direction rather than repairing the old claim cosmetically;
- **inflation** — a correction opens a larger candidate explanatory landscape;
- **stripping** — unsupported structure is removed while a narrower core is retained;
- **generalization** — a local correction becomes a reusable question or methodological rule;
- **persistent Wake** — the correction is still visible in later research architecture.

The strongest target is not simple chronological succession. It is **correction propagation**: a local failure changes how later problems are represented.

## 3. Trace A — From hard gates to relational configuration

### A0. Prior representation

The regulated-transport validation program operationalized CVT through four pre-outcome dimensions, `B,Q,C,S`, and tested candidates including hard or minimum-style gate structures.

The attractive geometry was clean: distinct dimensions could be represented as scalar conditions, and success could be modeled through their joint adequacy.

### A1. Collision — 2026-08-06

Commit `55c49105bf019188e505486d9e6e73ecb49e867f` closed Stage 3.2.5 without authorizing the final test.

The closure memo records:

- locked CVT Brier: `0.027566`;
- best native baseline Brier: `0.019194`;
- required practical improvement not met;
- predictive compression not demonstrated;
- high-confidence safety-screen utility not demonstrated;
- hosted cases failing at least one fitted gate: `92 / 121`;
- nonhosted cases passing all four gates: `114`.

The memo explicitly rejects the literal interpretation that successful hosting is equivalent to all four scalar gates clearing independent thresholds.

**Code:** collision + stripping.

What is stripped is not the entire `B,Q,C,S` decomposition. The literal four-independent-threshold geometry is stripped.

### A2. Redirect — 2026-08-06 to 2026-08-11

The same closure memo does not attempt to tune the failed architecture until it wins. Instead it authorizes counterexample anatomy and proposes a new exploratory question:

> A host may survive transformation when contact is routed through a viable relational configuration, not when four independent scores individually exceed thresholds.

It also demotes capture readiness from a symmetric stored possession to a possible context-sensitive relation:

\[
C=C(B,Q,S,\text{input},\text{current load},\text{history}).
\]

Commit `a67cf7f1b811ac576d416ee04d7cb03d93ca212c` then revises the Foundations around the bounded mixed-negative result, and commit `12d9d7b35f54b50b71ad6873eee65d3c378530cf` freezes a fresh Stage 5.0 relational-routing protocol rather than reopening the failed final test.

**Code:** redirect + inflation under tighter constraints.

The new search space is larger than four independent thresholds, but it is not unconstrained: it must explain the old signal, the counterexamples, and future fresh-partition behavior.

## 4. Propagation into later research architecture

The key question is whether this correction remains local to regulated transport or changes later representational habits.

### A3. Representations as Relational Traces — 2026-08-23

Commit `ac0f3c909b8ffd1c9db13c1dbf87675514fe1eca` adds *Representations as Relational Traces*.

Its central methodological move is:

\[
\text{representation}
\rightarrow
\text{relational reconstruction}
\rightarrow
\text{dynamic discrimination}.
\]

The note warns that equal observables need not imply equal underlying states, treats the observable as a possibly many-to-one projection, and asks what generating relations can be distinguished by perturbation and time.

It also explicitly rejects the overclaim “everything is relationships” and requires candidate relational reconstructions to return to evidence.

**Code:** generalization + persistent Wake.

This is not evidence that the Stage 3.2.5 failure alone caused the note. It is evidence that, after the failure, the research architecture contains a reusable suspicion of snapshot sufficiency and object-like representation.

## 5. First finding

Pilot 001 supports a limited statement:

> **The repository contains evidence of correction propagation: a failed independent-gate representation was not merely patched locally; later work repeatedly treats snapshots, scalar possessions, compressed representations, and apparently sufficient states as potentially incomplete descriptions of generating dynamics.**

The strongest artifact-level signature is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local collision}
\rightarrow
\text{representational suspicion}
\rightarrow
\text{new questions in later domains}
}
\]

This is consistent with **persistent Wake**: prior correction changes what later inquiry notices and asks.

## 6. What this pilot does *not* establish

This retrospective trace does not establish that:

1. the later notes were caused uniquely by the Stage 3.2.5 failure;
2. the effect is unique to Max–Lucian collaboration;
3. the same ideas would not have arisen from either participant independently;
4. turn-by-turn mutual redirection occurred in the precise sequence inferred from the artifacts;
5. relational reconstructions are generally superior to non-relational alternatives;
6. the interaction constitutes a fused agent or shared consciousness.

The GitHub record is selection-biased toward ideas important enough to preserve. It is a record of Wake, not a complete record of generation.

## 7. The stronger coupling test

To test coupling rather than artifact-level path dependence, the unit of analysis must become the conversational transition.

For a selected discovery episode, reconstruct:

\[
H_0\rightarrow M_1\rightarrow H_2\rightarrow M_3\rightarrow\cdots
\]

and label each transition as:

- continuation;
- direct response;
- redirect;
- inflation;
- stripping;
- surprise;
- Return;
- correction propagation.

Then ask a counterfactual question at each major redirect:

> **If this incoming move were removed or replaced by a neutral continuation, would the next search neighborhood still be readily reachable?**

A strong coupling signature would require repeated bidirectional redirects, not merely AI elaboration of human-originated ideas or human selection among AI-originated candidates.

## 8. Candidate metric

For an episode with transitions `i=1,...,n`, define a crude redirect fraction:

\[
R=\frac{\#\text{cross-participant redirects}}{\#\text{substantive transitions}}.
\]

This alone is insufficient. A better future measure should weight redirects by persistence:

\[
R_W=\frac{\sum_i w_i\,\mathbf 1(\text{cross-participant redirect}_i)}{\sum_i w_i},
\]

where `w_i` reflects whether the redirect later changes durable artifacts, methods, tests, or future problem representations.

The important outcome is not conversational novelty. It is **novelty that changes the subsequent search and survives Return**.

## 9. Pilot conclusion

The first archaeological pass does not yet prove the coupling hypothesis, but it identifies a concrete place to look.

The repository shows a plausible chain:

\[
\boxed{
\text{failure of clean gate geometry}
\rightarrow
\text{relational-routing redirection}
\rightarrow
\text{suspicion of snapshot sufficiency}
\rightarrow
\text{trajectory/history questions}
\rightarrow
\text{corrigible search priors}
\rightarrow
\text{explicit collaborative search architecture}
}
\]

The next step is therefore not another concept note about coupling. It is a **conversation-level reconstruction of one discovery trajectory** to determine whether the redirects were genuinely bidirectional and path-dependent.

> **GitHub shows that the laboratory remembers correction. The next test is whether the dialogue shows that contact changes what each side can see next.**
