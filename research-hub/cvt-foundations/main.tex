\title{\textbf{Coherence Viability Theory:}\\
Bounded Exchange, Capture Readiness, and the Conditions for Hosted Transformation}

\author{Daniel Varela Arevalo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We propose Coherence Viability Theory (CVT) as a testable framework for boundary-mediated transformations in which the continued viability of an identifiable receiving or hosting system is part of the success criterion. CVT begins from four candidate dimensions: bounded coupling, preserved identity-defining organization, capture readiness, and available restorative reserve. These elements are inherited from viability theory, autopoiesis, passivity-based control, resilience theory, and basin dynamics; the proposed contribution is their explicit measurement separation in hosted transformation, together with the distinction between exchange delivered to a system and exchange retained as productive uptake. In a first bounded regulated-transport simulation, the measured dimensions contained modest pre-outcome signal but failed preregistered thresholds for incremental predictive value, predictive compression, and high-confidence safety-screen utility. The literal hard-intersection geometry was neither necessary nor sufficient for the measured proxies: 92 of 121 hosted cases failed at least one fitted gate, while 114 nonhosted cases passed all four. We retain product, minimum, geometric-mean, soft-minimum, and direct constraint formulations as falsifiable first-generation candidates, not preferred results. Relational routing is introduced only as an exploratory next hypothesis. CVT does not establish universal necessity or sufficiency; future standing requires fresh preregistration, independent outcomes, domain-native baselines, counterexamples, and delayed-failure evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many systems depend on exchange across a boundary. They must receive energy, matter, information, influence, or control while retaining the organization that allows them to continue functioning as hosts. Neither closure nor openness alone solves this problem. Insufficient exchange can starve a system; excessive or poorly received exchange can overload it, erase relevant organization, exhaust recovery capacity, or drive it outside an admissible regime.

This paper studies a bounded class of such processes: \emph{boundary-mediated transformations in which continued host viability is part of what counts as success}. We call a transformation \emph{hosted} when a declared host undergoes or supports a meaningful transition while remaining viable over a stated observation and follow-up horizon. The host, boundary, transformation variable, success criterion, timescale, and level of analysis must therefore be specified before CVT is applied.

CVT begins from four candidate dimensions of hosted transformation---bounded coupling, preserved organization, capture response, and restorative reserve---but the first bounded simulation does not support treating their measured proxies as four jointly necessary independent gates:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{bounded coupling}: exchange is present but constrained relative to the host's changing handling range;
    \item \textbf{preserved identity-defining organization}: the organization, constraints, or independent control structure relevant to the declared success criterion persists through the transformation;
    \item \textbf{capture readiness}: before uptake is observed, the host has a measurable response relation through which a specified input may enter and remain within an admissible basin;
    \item \textbf{available restorative reserve}: sufficient internal or reliably accessible recovery capacity remains to absorb strain, repair damage, or preserve control authority.
\end{enumerate}

In compact form, the candidate structure is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CVT}_{\mathrm{candidate}}
    =
    B\cap Q\cap C\cap S.
\end{equation}
This expression records the first-generation strong hypothesis about essential conditions. It is not a proof that the conditions are universal or sufficient, and the regulated-transport validation described below did not support its literal threshold intersection for the measured proxies. Hidden state variables, delayed damage, environmental change, path dependence, adversarial interference, and measurement error may still produce failure.

In that fixed simulation, the measured \(B,Q,C,S\) variables contained modest pre-outcome signal but did not meet the preregistered thresholds for incremental predictive value, predictive compression, or high-confidence safety-screen utility. In the opened validation partition, 92 of 121 hosted cases failed at least one fitted gate, while 114 nonhosted cases passed all four. These simulation-specific results motivate an exploratory relational-routing revision; they do not validate a replacement law.

A central distinction is between what is delivered and what is productively retained. Let \(J_{\mathrm{raw}}\) denote boundary exchange delivered to the host and \(J_{\mathrm{eff}}\) denote retained productive uptake over a declared accounting window. When these quantities are commensurate and \(J_{\mathrm{raw}}>0\), realized uptake efficiency may be observed as
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{obs}}
    =
    \frac{J_{\mathrm{eff}}}{J_{\mathrm{raw}}}.
\end{equation}
This ratio is an outcome, not an explanation. CVT must predict it from independently measured host conditions rather than define capture readiness through the same outcome later used to evaluate the model. The testable claim is that increased delivered exchange can, in some regimes, reduce productive uptake when capture readiness, restorative reserve, or another essential mechanism degrades sufficiently.

The individual ingredients of this framework are inherited from mature traditions. Viability theory studies admissible trajectories under state constraints. Autopoiesis studies self-producing organization and structural coupling. Passivity and port-Hamiltonian control formalize constrained energetic exchange. Resilience theory studies persistence and recovery under disturbance. Basin dynamics studies nonlinear capture and escape. CVT does not claim these concepts as new. Its proposed contribution is narrower: it asks whether a class of boundary-mediated failures is better understood when bounded coupling, preserved organization, capture readiness, and restorative reserve are treated as potentially non-compensatory rather than freely substitutable.

Non-compensability is conceptually prior to any product equation. A product of normalized gates is one smooth representation because a near-zero essential gate sharply lowers the aggregate. It is not established as the uniquely correct form. Minimum-gate, weighted geometric-mean, normalized soft-minimum, and simultaneous constraint-intersection formulations remain competing representations to be compared against the same independently defined outcomes.

The membrane formulation provides the paper's clearest structural illustration. A boundary can permit flux while the receiving domain remains unable to retain that flux productively. Permeability is therefore not identical to capture readiness or effective uptake. The fusion section provides a bounded illustration of the distinction between a transformation metric and independently declared host viability; it does not restore the former numerical toy model or present CVT as a fusion-control result.

Section~\ref{sec:candidate-structure} states the candidate four-condition structure and the non-compensability proposal. Section~\ref{sec:formal} develops a compact formal architecture that separates observables, gates, candidate models, and independent outcomes. Section~\ref{sec:membrane} gives the regulated-interface illustration. Section~\ref{sec:fusion} presents fusion as a bounded domain mapping. \section{Intellectual Lineage}
\label{sec:lineage}

CVT is assembled from established literatures on viability, self-maintaining organization, open-system control, resilience, and nonlinear basin structure. The present claim is not that these traditions unknowingly contain one already-demonstrated CVT invariant. It is narrower: their concepts motivate a proposed decomposition of hosted transformation into bounded coupling, preserved identity-defining organization, capture response, and available restorative reserve. The first bounded simulation constrained the strong independent-gate version of that decomposition; whether a context-conditioned formulation adds explanatory or predictive value remains a question for fresh comparison, intervention, and counterexample testing.

\subsection{Viability Theory}

Viability theory studies evolutions constrained to remain within admissible sets and characterizes the initial states from which at least one viable trajectory exists, including through viability kernels and regulation under constraints \cite{aubin2011}. CVT inherits this concern with admissible evolution. Its proposed emphasis is boundary-mediated forcing: the host must not only remain inside constraints, but do so while receiving inputs whose rate, timing, and form may alter the host's capacity to remain viable.

This is not yet a mathematical extension of viability theory. It is a proposed decomposition of one class of viability problems. CVT earns distinct value only if the explicit separation of boundary regulation, capture readiness, preserved organization, and restorative reserve improves analysis beyond an ordinary state-constraint formulation.

\subsection{Autopoiesis and Structural Coupling}

Maturana and Varela describe living systems through autopoietic organization: a network of processes that continuously produces and maintains the components and boundary constituting the system as a unity \cite{maturana1980}. CVT inherits the importance of organization and boundary-mediated relation, but it does not identify every CVT host as autopoietic and does not claim organizational closure or structural coupling as new.

Preserved identity-defining organization in CVT means continuity of the organization or constraints relevant to the declared host and success criterion. It does not mean static form, impermeability, or isolation. A host may change profoundly while retaining what makes it the same host for the purpose of the analysis.

\subsection{Passivity and Port-Hamiltonian Systems}

Port-Hamiltonian theory models open physical systems through storage, interconnection, dissipation, and ports that expose exchanges with an environment \cite{vanderschaft2014}. It provides a substantially more rigorous account of energetic exchange than CVT presently offers. CVT does not modify that formalism in this manuscript.

The proposed CVT question is whether exchange capacity and productive retention must be represented separately in some hosted transformations, and whether identity, capture, or repair constraints matter in addition to energetic balance. In physical applications, those claims must be translated into domain-native variables and tested against port-Hamiltonian, passivity, and constraint-based baselines rather than asserted by analogy.

\subsection{Resilience and Recovery}

Holling distinguished ecological resilience from local stability by emphasizing persistence and the capacity of systems to absorb change without losing their organizing relations \cite{holling1973}. CVT inherits this concern with persistence under disturbance. Its term \emph{available restorative reserve} is intended to be narrower than resilience as a whole: it denotes currently accessible recovery headroom, not every property that contributes to long-run persistence.

Restorative reserve may coincide with an established resilience measure in some domains and add nothing in others. It should be retained as a separate CVT variable only where it can be independently operationalized and improves prediction of recovery, delayed damage, or repeated-load failure.

\subsection{Basin Dynamics and Capture Readiness}

Basin-based approaches characterize nonlocal stability by considering the set or measure of initial conditions that return to or remain associated with a desired attractor after finite perturbation. Basin stability, for example, complements local linear stability with a nonlocal measure related to basin volume \cite{menck2013}.

CVT uses this lineage to define \emph{capture readiness}: before uptake is observed, the host has a measurable response capacity through which a specified input may enter and remain within an admissible basin over a declared horizon. This capacity need not be a context-free stored scalar. Capture readiness is not the same as the later outcome of productive uptake. A boundary may deliver input into a system that has no suitable capture path, while a capture-ready response may remain unexpressed because the boundary does not deliver the required input.

\subsection{Status of the Synthesis}

These traditions overlap, but conceptual overlap does not establish a shared mechanism or a universal invariant. CVT's first-generation strong synthesis hypothesis was:

\begin{quote}
For a scoped class of boundary-mediated hosted transformations, failure may be better explained when bounded coupling, preserved identity-defining organization, capture readiness, and restorative reserve are treated as jointly required and potentially non-compensatory.
\end{quote}

The regulated-transport validation constrained this claim: hosted outcomes often occurred despite fitted gate failure, and nonhosted outcomes occurred despite all four fitted gates passing. The surviving synthesis is therefore a question about relations among boundary regulation, organization, capture response, reserve, specified input, current load, and history. It is supported only if a revised decomposition can be preregistered on fresh development data, operationalized independently, compared with simpler or domain-native models, and exposed to cases in which one proposed condition appears absent without loss of hosted viability.

\section{Candidate CVT Structure and Non-Compensability}
\label{sec:candidate-structure}

\begin{definition}[Hosted coherence viability]
Fix a declared host, boundary, transformation, success criterion, observation horizon, and level of analysis. The host is coherence-viable for that transformation when it can undergo or support the transition while remaining inside the declared viable set and retaining the identity-defining organization required by the success criterion.
\end{definition}

Let \(\mathcal K_B(t)\), \(\mathcal K_Q(t)\), \(\mathcal K_C(t)\), and \(\mathcal K_S(t)\) denote the state sets satisfying four proposed conditions at time \(t\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{bounded coupling} \(B\): the exchange schedule remains within a domain-specific admissible range relative to the host's changing handling capacity;
    \item \textbf{preserved identity-defining organization} \(Q\): the organization, constraints, or independent control structure relevant to the declared host persists through the transformation;
    \item \textbf{capture readiness} \(C\): before realized uptake is measured, the host has an estimable response relation through which the specified input may enter and remain within an admissible basin over the stated horizon;
    \item \textbf{available restorative reserve} \(S\): sufficient internal or reliably accessible recovery capacity remains to absorb strain, repair damage, or preserve control authority.
\end{enumerate}

The candidate CVT viability region is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K_{\CVT}(t)
    =
    \mathcal K_B(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_Q(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_C(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_S(t).
    \label{eq:candidate-cvt-region}
\end{equation}
The shorthand \(B\cap Q\cap C\cap S\) refers to simultaneous satisfaction of these conditions; it should not be read as though four scalar variables were themselves sets.

Equation~\eqref{eq:candidate-cvt-region} documents the first candidate geometry, not a demonstrated universal law. The regulated-transport validation did not support the literal threshold intersection for the measured proxies. The equation and the scalar representations below therefore record the original non-compensability burden; they are not the empirically preferred architecture after the counterexample analysis. Delayed damage, hidden variables, regime change, path dependence, adversarial interference, and measurement error may still produce failure.

\subsection{Conceptual Non-Compensability}

A proposed condition is non-compensatory in a specified domain when crossing its failure threshold prevents the declared viable outcome despite admissible improvement in the remaining conditions, unless the failed condition itself is restored or the scope of the host is changed. In gate notation, a strong form would be
\begin{equation}
    G_k < \Theta_k
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    y \notin \mathcal Y_{\mathrm{viable}}
    \qquad
    \text{for all admissible values of }\{G_i\}_{i\neq k},
    \label{eq:strong-noncompensability}
\end{equation}
under stated domain assumptions.

Equation~\eqref{eq:strong-noncompensability} is an empirical or model-class hypothesis. A valid case in which severe failure of one condition is reliably offset by another would constrain strict non-compensability, reveal an omitted restoration pathway, or show that the failed condition was not essential in that domain.

Three different claims must now be separated. \emph{Strict variable-level non-compensability} says that no admissible value of the other measured variables can rescue a failed gate; this claim was rejected for the fitted regulated-transport gates. \emph{Conditional non-compensability} says that such rescue is unavailable only within a declared forcing or history regime. \emph{Configuration-level viability constraints} say that some relations or routing configurations are inadmissible even when no single scalar variable is universally decisive. The latter two remain hypotheses, not results.

\subsection{Product Gate as a Candidate Representation}

One smooth representation of essential-gate fragility is
\begin{equation}
    \V_{\Pi}
    =
    \prod_{i=1}^{n}G_i,
    \qquad G_i\in[0,1].
    \label{eq:product-candidate}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Zero-gate property of the product representation]
If viability is represented by Equation~\eqref{eq:product-candidate}, then \(G_k\to0\) implies \(\V_{\Pi}\to0\), regardless of the remaining gate values.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because each \(G_i\in[0,1]\),
\[
    \V_{\Pi}
    =
    G_k\prod_{i\neq k}G_i.
\]
The remaining product is bounded above by one, so \(G_k\to0\) implies \(\V_{\Pi}\to0\).
\end{proof}

This proposition proves a property of the selected aggregator; it does not prove that real systems aggregate viability multiplicatively. Composite-indicator methodology likewise treats normalization, weighting, aggregation, robustness, and sensitivity as substantive modeling choices rather than neutral arithmetic \cite{oecd2008}. The product must therefore compete with alternatives such as
\begin{equation}
    \V_{\min}=\min_i G_i,
    \qquad
    \V_G=\prod_i G_i^{w_i},\quad \sum_iw_i=1,
\end{equation}
and with direct simultaneous constraint satisfaction \(G_i\geq\Theta_i\) for every essential condition. Model comparison should use independently defined viability outcomes, common data or simulations, and out-of-sample evaluation.

\subsection{Capture Readiness and Realized Uptake}

Capture readiness must be measured before or independently of the evaluated uptake outcome, but it need not be a context-free stored scalar. It may instead be a probe-estimated response relation conditional on the receiving state, specified input, current load, and declared history. An exploratory notation is
\begin{equation}
    C_t\!\left(u;B,Q,S,L,H\right),
    \label{eq:conditional-capture-readiness}
\end{equation}
where \(u\) describes the specified input and its timing, \(L\) is current load, and \(H\) is declared history. Equation~\eqref{eq:conditional-capture-readiness} is notation for a candidate measurement relation, not a universal law.

\begin{samepage}
Realized uptake efficiency remains an observed outcome:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{obs}}
    =
    \frac{J_{\mathrm{eff}}}{J_{\mathrm{raw}}},
    \qquad J_{\mathrm{raw}}>0.
\end{equation}
\end{samepage}
A model that defines \(C\) by \(\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}\) cannot then use \(C\) to explain the same observed ratio. CVT must operationalize capture readiness from state, basin, probe response, or response-capacity information available before or independently of the uptake outcome.

The immediate research burden is therefore not to defend one equation. It is to determine, domain by domain, whether the four proposed conditions are identifiable, whether any are genuinely essential, and which representation best predicts hosted viability without circular measurement.

\section{Formal Architecture: Observables, Gates, and Candidate Models}
\label{sec:formal}

The formal architecture separates what is measured from how it is normalized, how context-conditioned responses are estimated, and how candidate models are compared. CVT therefore distinguishes four layers:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{observable layer}: domain-native measurements with units, time windows, and uncertainty;
    \item \textbf{gate layer}: documented transformations of those measurements into normalized scores;
    \item \textbf{relational candidate layer}: prespecified context-conditioned response estimates and interactions among state, input, load, and history;
    \item \textbf{model and outcome layer}: candidate routing, aggregation, or prediction rules whose performance is compared against an independently defined viability outcome.
\end{enumerate}
A normalized gate is not itself an observation, and no scalar aggregate or routing score defines success merely by being chosen. The exploratory relational sequence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{observables}
&\longrightarrow
\text{context-conditioned response estimates}
\\
&\longrightarrow
\text{candidate routing model}
\longrightarrow
\text{independent outcome}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Interactions must be prespecified and counted as model capacity, and domain-native baselines should receive the same context variables where scientifically appropriate.

\subsection{Observable, Gate, and Outcome Layers}

For each proposed condition \(i\in\mathcal I=\{B,Q,C,S\}\), let \(z_i(t)\) denote a raw domain-specific measurement or vector of measurements. A normalization map
\begin{equation}
    G_i(t)
    =
    g_i\!\left(z_i(t);\vartheta_i\right)
    \in[0,1]
    \label{eq:gate-normalization}
\end{equation}
converts the raw measurement into a gate score. The parameters \(\vartheta_i\), normalization range, uncertainty interval, and threshold rationale must be reported for each application. The maps need not be monotone. Bounded coupling generally requires a two-sided map: both insufficient and excessive exchange may receive low scores, while an admissible operating band receives a high score.

Let \(y(t)\) denote a domain-native viability outcome and let \(\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{viable}}\) be declared before the gates are fitted. The binary event
\begin{equation}
    V_{\mathrm{set}}(t)
    =
    \mathbf{1}\!\left\{y(t)\in\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{viable}}\right\}
    \label{eq:independent-viability-event}
\end{equation}
is an independently defined outcome, not a CVT aggregate. Continuous outcomes may be used when binary classification would discard relevant information.

\subsection{Candidate Aggregation Family}

The intersection in Equation~\eqref{eq:candidate-cvt-region} specifies simultaneous constraint satisfaction. When a scalar score is useful, several non-compensatory or weakly compensatory representations should be compared:
\begin{align}
    \V_{\Pi}(t)
    &=
    \prod_{i\in\mathcal I}G_i(t),
    \\
    \V_{\min}(t)
    &=
    \min_{i\in\mathcal I}G_i(t),
    \\
    \V_G(t)
    &=
    \prod_{i\in\mathcal I}G_i(t)^{w_i},
    \qquad
    w_i\geq0,
    \quad
    \sum_iw_i=1,
    \\
    \V_{\tau}(t)
    &=
    -\tau\log\!\left(
        \frac{1}{|\mathcal I|}
        \sum_{i\in\mathcal I}e^{-G_i(t)/\tau}
    \right),
    \qquad \tau>0.
    \label{eq:aggregation-family}
\end{align}
The last expression is a normalized soft minimum. Direct simultaneous constraints \(G_i\geq\Theta_i\) for every essential condition remain a further alternative. These representations encode different assumptions and must be evaluated against the same independent outcome using common data or simulations, sensitivity analysis, and out-of-sample comparison \cite{oecd2008}.

\subsection{State Separation and Notation}

Recovery history, current reserve, and accumulated damage should not be collapsed. Let \(H(t)\) denote learned recovery history or maturity, \(S(t)\) currently available restorative reserve, and \(D(t)\) accumulated damage. The reserve construct may be a vector or a family of independently constrained margins; it should not be forced into one latent scalar when a domain does not support such aggregation. These states may enter domain-specific dynamical models, but CVT does not assert a universal differential equation for their evolution in this foundations paper.

The notation hierarchy is therefore:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Role \\
\midrule
\(z_i\) & raw domain-native measurement or measurement vector \\
\(G_i\) & normalized candidate gate \\
\(\mathcal K_i\) & state set satisfying a proposed condition \\
\(y\), \(V_{\mathrm{set}}\) & independent domain-native outcome \\
\(\V\) & candidate scalar aggregate or score \\
\(H,S,D\) & recovery history, available reserve, and accumulated damage \\
\(u,L\) & specified input and current load \\
\(C_t(u;B,Q,S,L,H)\) & exploratory context-conditioned capture-response estimate \\
\(\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}\) & observed uptake efficiency when defined \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Raw Exchange, Productive Uptake, and Prediction}

Let \(J_{\mathrm{raw}}(t)\) be measured delivered exchange and \(J_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\) independently measured retained productive uptake over the same accounting window. When the quantities are commensurate and \(J_{\mathrm{raw}}>0\),
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{obs}}(t)
    =
    \frac{J_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)}{J_{\mathrm{raw}}(t)}.
    \label{eq:effective-exchange}
\end{equation}
In systems with endogenous amplification or noncommensurate units, productive uptake must instead be defined through an explicit conversion or target-response measure.

Capture readiness is measured before or independently of the uptake outcome. CVT may then test a first-generation predictor
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\rho}_{\theta}(t)
    =
    f_{\theta}\!\left(
        G_B(t),G_Q(t),G_C(t),G_S(t),\mathbf w(t)
    \right),
    \label{eq:uptake-predictor}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf w(t)\) contains declared exogenous covariates. The predictor may be multiplicative, minimum-gated, nonlinear, or domain-native. Defining \(\rho\) as a product of the same gates and then treating that product as an explanation of uptake is not permitted.

A relational candidate may instead use prespecified state--input and history interactions, for example through \(C_t(u;B,Q,S,L,H)\), before predicting an independent outcome. Productive uptake, rejection, damage, and recovery measured after the evaluated input remain outcomes or counterexample-anatomy labels unless they are independently predicted from information available before evaluation. A fresh model must not adapt those labels into predictors on the already-opened validation partition.

\subsection{Hosted Transformation as an Independent Outcome}

\begin{definition}[Hosted transformation]
Fix a host state \(x(t)\), a transformation variable \(X(t)\), a domain-native viable set \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{declared}}\), a transition threshold \(X_{\mathrm{crit}}\), and a follow-up horizon \(\tau_f\geq0\). A transformation is hosted on \([t_0,t_1]\) when
\begin{equation}
    X(t_1)-X(t_0)
    \geq
    X_{\mathrm{crit}},
    \label{eq:transformation-success}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    x(t)\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{declared}}
    \qquad
    \text{for all }t\in[t_0,t_1+\tau_f].
    \label{eq:delayed-host-viability}
\end{equation}
An independently specified damage limit may be added when damage is not already included in \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{declared}}\).
\end{definition}

The follow-up horizon permits delayed failure to count against apparent success. Gate scores and CVT aggregates are predictors or explanatory variables for this outcome; they do not define the outcome they are supposed to predict.

\subsection{Local Exchange--Uptake Condition}

Suppose \(J_{\mathrm{raw}}>0\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}>0\), and
\[
    J_{\mathrm{eff}}
    =
    \rho_{\mathrm{obs}}(J_{\mathrm{raw}})J_{\mathrm{raw}}
\]
locally with differentiable \(\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}\). Then
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dJ_{\mathrm{eff}}}{dJ_{\mathrm{raw}}}
    =
    \rho_{\mathrm{obs}}
    +
    J_{\mathrm{raw}}
    \frac{d\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}}{dJ_{\mathrm{raw}}}.
    \label{eq:exchange-uptake-derivative}
\end{equation}
Effective uptake decreases with additional delivered exchange exactly when
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\log\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}}{d\log J_{\mathrm{raw}}}
    <
    -1.
    \label{eq:uptake-elasticity-condition}
\end{equation}
This is a calculus condition, not evidence that such degradation occurs. The empirical CVT claim is that some scoped systems may enter regimes satisfying Equation~\eqref{eq:uptake-elasticity-condition} because capture readiness, restorative reserve, or another essential mechanism deteriorates under load.

\section{Membrane Illustration: Regulated Transport Across an Interface}
\label{sec:membrane}

A regulated transport interface provides a bounded physical illustration of CVT. It is not presented as the universal mathematical form of the theory. The purpose is narrower: to show how delivered flux, interface integrity, pre-outcome capture response, productive uptake, restorative reserve, and delayed damage can be represented as distinct quantities in a system whose boundary is physically explicit.

\subsection{Established Interface-Transport Form}

Let domains \(A\) and \(B\) meet at an interface \(\Gamma\), and let \(c_A(x,t)\) and \(c_B(x,t)\) denote concentrations or other transport potentials on the two sides. Classical diffusion and membrane-transport models use boundary or interface laws that relate flux to a potential difference and an interface-transfer coefficient \cite{crank1975,kedem1958}. A linear permeability law may be written
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{raw}}(t)
    =
    \Pi_{\Gamma}(t)
    \bigl(c_B(t)-c_A(t)\bigr)
    \qquad \text{on }\Gamma,
    \label{eq:membrane-raw-flux}
\end{equation}
with the corresponding diffusive boundary flux on side \(A\), under a stated sign convention,
\begin{equation}
    -D_A\nabla c_A\cdot n_A
    =
    J_{\mathrm{raw}}.
    \label{eq:membrane-robin-law}
\end{equation}
Here \(D_A\) is a diffusion coefficient, \(n_A\) is the outward normal, and \(\Pi_{\Gamma}\) is an interface conductance or permeability with units chosen consistently with the transported quantity. Equations~\eqref{eq:membrane-raw-flux}--\eqref{eq:membrane-robin-law} are established transport forms, not CVT inventions. State dependence of \(\Pi_{\Gamma}\) is a modeling choice that must be justified by a physical mechanism or controller.

The interface law identifies what can cross. It does not by itself establish whether the receiving state can capture, retain, or survive the delivered input.

\subsection{Separating Permeability, Flux, Capture, and Uptake}

The membrane illustration distinguishes five quantities that should not be collapsed:
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_{\Gamma}
    \neq
    J_{\mathrm{raw}}
    \neq
    C
    \neq
    \rho_{\mathrm{obs}}
    \neq
    J_{\mathrm{eff}}.
    \label{eq:membrane-separation}
\end{equation}
The permeability \(\Pi_{\Gamma}\) is an interface property or control setting. The raw flux \(J_{\mathrm{raw}}\) is delivered transport. Capture readiness \(C\) is a pre-outcome response estimate of the receiving state to a specified input, measured before or independently of the uptake outcome; it need not be context-free. The quantity \(J_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is retained productive uptake measured over a declared accounting window. When raw and effective quantities are commensurate and delivered flux is positive, observed uptake efficiency is given by Equation~\eqref{eq:effective-exchange}. This ratio must not be used to define the same capture-readiness variable then claimed to explain it.

\subsection{Operational Mapping to \(B,Q,C,S\)}

For a declared receiving host, the membrane illustration maps the four candidate CVT conditions as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{bounded coupling \(B\)}: delivered flux remains within a two-sided admissible band relative to the host's changing handling capacity;
    \item \textbf{preserved identity-defining organization \(Q\)}: the interface and host organization required by the success criterion remain functionally intact;
    \item \textbf{capture readiness \(C\)}: before the uptake outcome is observed, a probe-estimated receiving-state response indicates whether the specified input can be retained within an admissible basin over the declared horizon;
    \item \textbf{available restorative reserve \(S\)}: measurable recovery headroom remains after transport-induced strain.
\end{enumerate}

Let \(z_B,z_Q,z_C,z_S\) denote domain-native measurements and let \(G_i=g_i(z_i;\vartheta_i)\) be documented normalizations. The candidate membrane viability region is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K_{\mathrm{mem}}(t)
    =
    \mathcal K_B(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_Q(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_C(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_S(t).
    \label{eq:membrane-viability-region}
\end{equation}
This intersection is the representation-independent claim. When a scalar score is useful, candidate forms include
\begin{equation}
    \V_{\mathrm{prod,mem}}
    =
    G_BG_QG_CG_S,
    \qquad
    \V_{\min,\mathrm{mem}}
    =
    \min\{G_B,G_Q,G_C,G_S\},
    \label{eq:membrane-candidate-scores}
\end{equation}
and the wider aggregation family in Equation~\eqref{eq:aggregation-family}. No one form is privileged without comparative evidence against an independently declared viability outcome.

\subsection{Transport Regimes and Failure Signatures}

The same interface can enter several distinct regimes.

\paragraph{Insufficient delivery.}
Raw flux remains below the lower edge of the admissible operating band. The host may preserve organization and reserve while failing to receive enough input for the declared transformation.

\paragraph{Overexposure.}
Raw flux exceeds the receiving system's handling band. Damage, rejection, spillover, saturation, or escape from the viable set may increase even when permeability and gradient remain physically large. Observed uptake loss may follow but should be measured rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Capture failure.}
The interface delivers input while the receiving state lacks a viable capture path. A low value of \(\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}\) may be consistent with this mechanism, but it does not uniquely identify it: conversion losses, measurement error, transport delay, or an omitted state variable remain alternatives.

\paragraph{Loss of identity-defining organization.}
The interface or host organization required by the declared success criterion fails. Complete merger is a CVT failure only when continued distinction is part of that criterion. If merger is the intended transformation, the host and level of analysis must be redefined.

\paragraph{Reserve exhaustion.}
Transport remains possible and uptake may remain temporarily positive, but restorative reserve falls below the level required for repair or repeated-load survival. Failure may therefore appear only during a follow-up horizon after the target state has been reached.

These regimes show why permeability alone cannot classify interface health. The same value of \(\Pi_{\Gamma}\) may be viable or destructive depending on the gradient, receiving state, damage, reserve, observation window, and declared success criterion.

\subsection{Causal State-Dependent Interface Control}

A regulated interface may adapt permeability to estimates of receiving-state condition. A causal policy class is
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_{\Gamma}(t)
    =
    \Pi_{\min}
    +
    \bigl(\Pi_{\max}-\Pi_{\min}\bigr)
    h_{\theta}\!\left(
        \widehat C(t-\Delta t),
        G_S(t-\Delta t),
        D(t-\Delta t),
        z_{\Gamma}(t-\Delta t)
    \right),
    \label{eq:membrane-causal-control}
\end{equation}
where \(0\leq h_{\theta}\leq1\), \(\Delta t\geq0\) is the sensing and actuation delay, and \(z_{\Gamma}\) contains declared interface measurements. A lagged value of \(\rho_{\mathrm{obs}}\) may enter \(z_{\Gamma}\) as feedback from a completed accounting window. Contemporaneous observed uptake cannot be treated as a pre-existing causal input without specifying how it is measured before the control action it is supposed to determine.

Equation~\eqref{eq:membrane-causal-control} is a controller class, not a universal membrane law. It should be compared with fixed permeability, capacity-maximizing control, and domain-native controllers using the same hosted-transformation outcome and follow-up horizon.

\subsection{Preregistered Membrane Test}

A reproducible test should declare the host, transported quantity, interface law, viable set, transformation criterion, and follow-up horizon before fitting CVT variables. It should measure pre-input state and reserve, perturb permeability or driving gradient over a stated range, record delivered flux, retained productive uptake, rejection or damage, and recovery, and then compare product, minimum, soft-minimum, and direct-constraint representations out of sample. Deliberate counterexamples should include intended merger, successful transport with negligible measured reserve, high flux without uptake loss, and low capture estimates followed by stable productive retention.

The membrane illustration supports CVT only if the \(B,Q,C,S\) decomposition improves prediction or explanation beyond simpler transport, damage, or state-constraint models. A standard transport model that performs equally well with fewer independently measured quantities would constrain the added value of CVT in this domain.

\subsection{First Bounded Regulated-Transport Result}

The measured \(B,Q,C,S\) variables contained modest pre-outcome signal, but none of the three preregistered claims passed: incremental predictive value over the transport-only baseline was below threshold, compression relative to a domain-native model was not demonstrated, and the proposed high-confidence safety screen was not supported.

\subsection{From the Interface Illustration to Fusion}

The next section uses artificial fusion as an illustrative boundary-mediated host problem. The transfer is structural: a plasma--machine system contains interfaces across which heat, particles, waves, fuel, ash, and control influence are transported or regulated while machine viability matters. This does not establish that fusion is reducible to Equation~\eqref{eq:membrane-robin-law}, that a tokamak is a biological membrane, or that the membrane illustration captures reactor physics.

\section{Fusion Illustration: Self-Heating and Host Viability}
\label{sec:fusion}

Fusion provides a bounded illustration of hosted transformation because the target transition occurs inside a coupled plasma--machine system whose continued viability is part of success. This section does not present CVT as an alternative to fusion physics, nor does it treat the membrane illustration as a reactor model. It asks a narrower question: can a transformation metric be kept distinct from the independently specified conditions under which the machine can sustain that transition?

\subsection{Self-Heating Is Not a Complete Viability Criterion}

Lawson's power-balance analysis already shows that reactor relevance cannot be inferred from temperature alone; density, confinement, duration, and losses enter the criterion for a power-producing system \cite{lawson1957}. Burning-plasma research likewise distinguishes externally supplied heating from heating supplied by fusion-born alpha particles. Experimental evidence for a self-heated burning-plasma regime has been reported in inertial confinement fusion \cite{zylstra2022}; that result supports the general concept of fusion self-heating but does not establish tokamak-specific exhaust, material, confinement, or control performance.

For the limited purpose of this illustration, define the local diagnostic
\begin{equation}
    \chi_{\alpha}(t)
    =
    \frac{P_{\alpha}(t)}{P_{\alpha}(t)+P_{\mathrm{aux}}(t)},
    \label{eq:fusion-alpha-fraction}
\end{equation}
where \(P_{\alpha}\) is alpha-particle heating and \(P_{\mathrm{aux}}\) is auxiliary heating. Equation~\eqref{eq:fusion-alpha-fraction} is an author-defined heating fraction, not a universal definition of ignition, fusion gain, or reactor success. An increase in \(\chi_{\alpha}\) records progress toward alpha-dominated self-heating; it does not by itself establish a viable operating state.

Let \(y_{\mathrm{fus}}(t)\) denote a domain-native outcome vector and let \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{fus}}\) be an operating set declared independently of CVT. Depending on the device and scenario, this set may constrain fusion performance, confinement, MHD and kinetic stability, heat and particle exhaust, helium and impurity control, plasma-facing-component loads, actuator and diagnostic availability, and pulse duration. A hosted self-heating transition over a follow-up horizon \(\tau_{\mathrm{follow}}\) may then be stated as
\begin{equation}
    \chi_{\alpha}(t_1)
    \geq
    \chi_{\mathrm{target}},
    \qquad
    y_{\mathrm{fus}}(t)
    \in
    \mathcal K_{\mathrm{fus}}
    \quad
    \text{for all }t\in[t_1,t_1+\tau_{\mathrm{follow}}].
    \label{eq:fusion-hosted-condition}
\end{equation}
The first condition describes the selected transformation target. The second describes continued host viability. Their separation is the point of the illustration.

\subsection{The Host Problem Is Already Physical}

The ITER Physics Basis and its update treat burning-plasma performance as an integrated problem involving confinement, stability, fuelling, power and particle control, plasma-facing components, diagnostics, actuators, and energetic-particle behavior \cite{iter1999,shimada2007}. Heat and particle exhaust, helium removal, impurity control, and material loading are established reactor problems \cite{loarte2007}; energetic alpha particles also create established confinement, redistribution, and instability questions \cite{fasoli2007}. CVT does not claim discovery of these constraints.

The proposed CVT question is whether one useful decomposition of the pre-transition host state is:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{bounded coupling \(B\)}: heating, fuelling, particle, momentum, and exhaust loads remain inside declared operating envelopes;
    \item \textbf{preserved identity-defining organization \(Q\)}: the plasma configuration and machine functions required by the declared operating state remain intact;
    \item \textbf{capture readiness \(C\)}: before the outcome is observed, a specified probe or model estimates whether the plasma--machine state can accommodate the specified increase in self-heating without leaving \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{fus}}\);
    \item \textbf{available restorative reserve \(S\)}: measurable actuator, exhaust, thermal, magnetic, diagnostic, or recovery headroom remains after the transition.
\end{enumerate}
This mapping is provisional. Several margins listed under \(S\) may be independent hard constraints rather than indicators of one latent reserve variable. Likewise, a measured failure after the transition does not by itself identify which proposed condition was absent.

The first-generation candidate fusion viability region may be written at the level of simultaneous constraints:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K_{\mathrm{CVT,fus}}(t)
    =
    \mathcal K_B(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_Q(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_C(t)
    \cap
    \mathcal K_S(t).
    \label{eq:fusion-cvt-region}
\end{equation}
No product score or demonstrated necessity claim is asserted here. The regulated-transport result constrains the independent hard-gate geometry but does not adjudicate fusion. Any fusion aggregation or relational-routing model would have to be preregistered and compared with established integrated models using the same independently declared outcome and appropriately shared context variables.

\subsection{Evidentiary Limit}

In this paper fusion illustrates, but does not validate, CVT. The decomposition adds scientific value only if independently measured pre-transition variables improve out-of-sample prediction, control, or failure diagnosis beyond simpler and domain-native baselines. It would be constrained if sustained operation occurs despite persistent failure of a proposed essential condition, if the capture-readiness proxy merely restates the outcome, if the reserve construct adds no information beyond existing engineering margins, or if ordinary integrated models perform equally well with fewer variables.

The detailed controller equations, historical numerical runs, reserve sweep, and former Minimum Viable Host statement are excluded from the foundations argument. They belong, if pursued, in a separate reproducible fusion-host model with complete equations, units, parameter provenance, initial and boundary conditions, solver details, sensitivity analysis, and domain-native comparison controllers.

The bounded conclusion is:
\begin{quote}
Progress toward alpha-dominated self-heating is a transformation metric. Hosted operation additionally requires that the coupled plasma--machine system remain inside an independently declared viable operating set while carrying that transition.
\end{quote}

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Validation Program}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Publication and Evidentiary Status}

CVT is presented here as a conceptual and methodological framework paper informed by one completed bounded simulation study. It is not a theorem establishing universal conditions, an empirical-domain validation study, or a calibrated fusion-control model. In the regulated-transport world, the measured dimensions carried modest signal, but the locked CVT model did not meet the preregistered practical-improvement threshold and was outperformed by the higher-capacity native model. The literal fitted hard-gate geometry was contradicted in both directions.

The four-dimension measurement separation remains useful, but its structure may prove domain-dependent. Some dimensions may collapse into existing variables; some may be vectors or response relations rather than scalars; some may not be independently essential; and additional context may be required. The paper therefore treats \(B,Q,C,S\) as a candidate decomposition rather than a settled ontology. Relational routing is an exploratory programmatic hypothesis, not the validated successor to the rejected fitted gates.

\subsection{Principal Threats to Validity}

The principal threats are circular measurement, flexible host definition, post-hoc thresholds, correlated gates, hidden state, delayed failure, adaptive reuse of opened data, model-capacity imbalance, and overinterpretation of analogies. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item capture readiness must not be defined by the uptake outcome it is intended to predict;
    \item the host, boundary, viable set, and transformation criterion must be declared before results are interpreted;
    \item gate normalizations and thresholds must be reported with uncertainty and sensitivity analysis;
    \item apparent cross-gate effects must be distinguished from common-cause forcing;
    \item short-term target attainment must not count as hosted success when delayed damage removes viability during the follow-up horizon;
    \item membrane and fusion mappings must not be treated as evidence for universal equivalence across domains;
    \item counterexample anatomy from an opened validation partition may generate hypotheses but cannot validate a repaired model on that same partition;
    \item a higher-capacity CVT revision must be compared fairly with domain-native models given scientifically appropriate shared context and explicit capacity accounting.
\end{enumerate}

Transparent reporting of model development, validation, predictor handling, outcome definition, and missing information is a prerequisite for evaluating predictive claims. The TRIPOD statement provides one established reporting model in clinical prediction research, although CVT applications will require domain-specific adaptation rather than literal transfer \cite{collins2015}.

\subsection{Falsification and Model Comparison}

A bounded CVT application should be considered constrained or unsuccessful when any of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item hosted transformation occurs reliably despite persistent failure of a condition claimed to be essential;
    \item a gate or proxy merely restates the independent outcome;
    \item a simpler state-constraint, transport, resilience, or domain-native model performs equally well or better with fewer independently measured quantities;
    \item the preferred aggregation form fails out of sample or is unstable under reasonable normalization and threshold choices;
    \item the proposed reserve construct adds no information beyond already established margins;
    \item the declared success disappears when the observation horizon is extended to include delayed damage.
\end{enumerate}

A successful test need not vindicate the whole framework. It may instead identify a narrower domain, remove a nonessential condition, replace a scalar with a vector, or show that direct constraint intersection performs better than any aggregate score.

\subsection{First Bounded Validation: Regulated Transport}

The first bounded regulated-transport study has been completed. It declared the simulated host, transported quantity, interface schedule, viable set, transformation criterion, follow-up horizon, measured \(B,Q,C,S\) proxies, comparison models, development and validation partitions, and three quantitative success claims before validation evaluation. The locked validation result demonstrated modest pre-outcome signal and a small improvement over the transport-only baseline. It failed to demonstrate the required practical improvement, predictive compression relative to native gradient boosting, or a high-confidence safety screen. The counterexample anatomy rejected literal necessity and sufficiency for the fitted threshold intersection.

The anatomy also suggested a narrower next question: whether hosted viability depends on input-conditioned routing among boundary state, organization, capture response, reserve, load, schedule, and history. This interpretation is exploratory because it was formed after examining the opened validation partition. Any Stage 5 continuation requires a new protocol, a fresh development partition, prespecified interactions and capacity accounting, fair native baselines, and a new stop/go decision before any final test is generated or opened. No empirical-domain claim follows from success or failure inside the simulated native world.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CVT asks a bounded research question: when a system must receive exchange while remaining an identifiable and viable host, do measurements of bounded coupling, preserved identity-defining organization, capture response, and restorative reserve improve prediction or explanation of hosted transformation, and in what relation to specified input, load, schedule, and history?

The present paper does not establish a universal invariant. The first hard-gate geometry failed in the bounded regulated-transport simulation: fitted gate failure was compatible with hosted outcomes, fitted all-pass status was compatible with nonhosted outcomes, and a native gradient-boosting model predicted better. The methodological separations remain useful: raw observations are distinct from normalized gates and independent outcomes; delivered exchange is distinct from productive uptake; and delayed host viability remains part of success. The membrane illustration shows why permeability, flux, capture response, uptake, reserve, and damage should not be collapsed. The fusion illustration shows why progress toward a transition metric does not by itself establish that the coupled host can sustain the resulting state.

Relational routing is the next hypothesis, not a result. CVT earns further scientific standing only through fresh preregistration and data, explicit counterexamples, independent measurement, fair competing models, and reproducible validation. Its strongest present contribution is therefore not one universal equation, but a disciplined separation of questions that are often conflated: what crosses a boundary, how a specified input meets a receiving state, what is productively retained, what organization must persist, and what recovery capacity remains after change.
