# Capability, Admissibility, and Relational Viability

## Status

Cross-domain concept note arising from the 2026-09-05 to 2026-09-08 Max–Lucian discussion.

**Boundary:** this note proposes a structural vocabulary, not a universal law. Physical materials, leadership, theology, institutions, and AI do not share one mechanism merely because the same abstract distinctions can be useful across them. Cross-domain recurrence is a reason to test the distinctions, not proof that all domains reduce to one theory.

## 1. The basic separation

A recurring mistake is to collapse three different questions:

1. **What can the system do?**
2. **What should the agent do?**
3. **What can be done without consuming the relation that makes continued action possible?**

These questions define three different sets.

Let:

- `A(X,R)` = the accessible capability set of constituents `X` under relational configuration `R`;
- `D` = the admissible action set under the relevant commitments, purposes, responsibilities, and constraints;
- `V(X,R)` = the viable action set: actions compatible with preserving or regenerating the enabling relation through time.

Then in general:

`D ⊆ A`

and:

`V ⊆ A`.

The core distinctions are therefore:

> **Available is not admissible.**

and:

> **Possible is not viable.**

## 2. Configuration-gated capability

A capability need not belong to an isolated constituent in the same way under every configuration.

A useful abstract form is:

`A(X,R1) != A(X,R2)`.

The constituents constrain the physically or structurally possible space, while relations, geometry, interfaces, scale, boundary conditions, and perturbations can determine which possibilities become accessible.

Recent materials examples made this distinction vivid: a property absent in an idealized bulk description can become accessible under a different boundary or grain configuration. The relevant lesson is not that relation creates arbitrary possibility. It is narrower:

> **Constituents constrain possibility; configuration gates accessibility.**

This distinction should remain prior to CVT itself. CVT need not explain the microscopic origin of a capability. It can instead ask what trajectories become available once a structured possibility space exists.

## 3. From capability to governance

Human and artificial agents introduce another layer because they can select among available actions.

The leadership shorthand is:

> **Just because you can do something does not mean you should.**

This gives:

`a ∈ A` does not imply `a ∈ D`.

The boundary of capability is not the boundary of permission, wisdom, legitimacy, or responsibility.

This observation forced a correction to an earlier formulation that identified power with restraint.

That definition fails because destructive domination remains real power.

A cleaner separation is:

> **Power is capacity.**

> **Authority concerns the governance or legitimate use of capacity.**

> **Self-mastery is the preservation of that governance under internal or external pressure.**

The decisive failure is not always action. It is the collapse:

`I can -> therefore I may`

or:

`I am provoked -> therefore provocation determines policy`.

## 4. Provocation and sovereignty

Restraint is not automatically virtuous. Some situations require intervention, boundary enforcement, defense, or consequence.

The more useful invariant is:

> **Provocation does not get to write the governing principle of the response.**

An agent can receive anger, fear, desire, humiliation, pain, pressure, or temptation without granting that pressure sovereign authority over action.

In compact form:

`pressure exists != pressure governs`.

This makes self-mastery a positive agency capacity rather than mere inhibition.

The agent retains the ability to select among available responses according to a governing purpose rather than becoming a deterministic echo of the provocation.

## 5. Viability adds the temporal question

Even an admissible or immediately successful action may damage the conditions that make future action, cooperation, trust, production, or common access possible.

Thus a one-period success criterion is insufficient.

An action can satisfy:

`desired immediate outcome achieved`

while producing:

`R_t -> degraded R_{t+1}`.

This motivates the viable subset `V`.

A viable action is not merely possible now. Its exercise remains compatible with maintaining or regenerating the enabling relation.

The compact principle is:

> **Do not consume the enabling relation for the sake of an immediately accessible good.**

This distinction appears in productive networks, institutions, commons, relationships, leadership, and AI coordination even though the mechanisms differ.

## 6. Boundaries and protected participation

A viable relation is not necessarily boundaryless.

The museum-security analogy is useful:

A valuable work may be publicly accessible while still protected by barriers, alarms, handling rules, and guards. The boundary is not merely an exclusion device. It preserves a configuration in which:

`access + encounter + continued existence of the good`

remain simultaneously possible.

Hence:

> **The boundary can protect the possibility of continued access.**

This yields the broader commons principle:

> **A viable commons requires modes of access that do not consume the conditions of common access.**

Neither maximal control nor maximal permissiveness is automatically viable.

## 7. Harmonia and Eden as a theological application

The Eden / Harmonia discussion applies the structural distinction theologically, without treating theology as empirical proof of the framework.

The proposal is not:

`self-mastery -> deserve paradise`.

It is:

> **Paradise may require self-mastery not as an entrance fee but as a viability condition.**

If the governing policy remains:

`desire -> grasp`

`wound -> retaliation`

`power -> domination`,

then abundance and freedom can be converted back into rivalry, consumption, and coercion.

The Cain configuration can persist historically. The stronger claim is that it cannot generate Eden as Eden.

This gives a theological application of the general distinction:

`possible != viable`.

Genesis 4:7 contributes a language of governance:

`וְאַתָּה תִּמְשָׁל־בּוֹ`

*ve'atah timshol-bo* — "and you, rule/govern it."

The interpretive significance is not the absence of desire or anger, but the question of what governs what.

## 8. Consequence without retaliation

Self-government must not be confused with permissiveness.

A viable authority may need to close paths, impose consequences, defend boundaries, or interrupt harm.

The distinction is:

`retaliation != governed consequence`.

A response can be severe without being authored by vengeance.

The question is whether the action serves the protected good and preserves truthful relation where possible, or whether provocation has simply become the governing rule.

## 9. Relation to CVT

A tentative architecture is:

`constituents X`

`+ relational configuration R`

`-> accessible capability space A(X,R)`

`-> transition structure T_R`

`-> actual trajectories Γ`

`-> viability across time V`.

CVT belongs primarily around the transition and trajectory levels rather than being asked to explain every mechanism that generates the possibility space.

Relational viability asks a broader temporal question:

> **Which trajectories preserve or regenerate the relations that make continued participation possible?**

This keeps the division of labor clear:

- configuration shapes accessibility;
- agency selects among available paths;
- admissibility governs selection;
- viability evaluates whether selected paths preserve the enabling relation through time;
- CVT studies continuity, constraints, and trajectories within the structured landscape.

## 10. Compact synthesis

The note can be compressed into eight claims:

1. **Constituents constrain possibility; configuration gates accessibility.**
2. **Available is not admissible.**
3. **Possible is not viable.**
4. **Power is capacity; power is not permission.**
5. **Authority governs the use of capacity.**
6. **Self-mastery preserves that governance under pressure.**
7. **A viable boundary can protect continued participation.**
8. **A durable good cannot be sustained by repeatedly consuming the relation that makes the good possible.**

The shortest formulation is:

> **Intelligence is not exhausted by expanding the action space. Wisdom includes knowing which available paths should not be taken, and relational viability asks which paths leave the conditions for a future still intact.**
