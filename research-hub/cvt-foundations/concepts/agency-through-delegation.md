# Agency Through Delegation

## Conservation, Dissipation, and Return in Agentic Chains

**Status:** conceptual companion note; not an empirical claim, validation result, model lock, or manuscript revision.

**Boundary:** this note develops a conceptual application of CVT and FTLtauA to AI-mediated delegation and multi-agent systems. It does not claim that agency is literally a conserved physical quantity, does not establish an empirical law of agentic systems, does not alter the frozen CVT validation record, and does not modify `research-hub/cvt-foundations/main.tex`. The language of conservation and dissipation is diagnostic and structural unless later formalized and tested.

## 1. Motivating shift

Generative and agentic AI make it increasingly possible for representation and interpretation to be delegated at both ends of an interaction.

A familiar interaction has the form:

> human A -> representation -> human B

An AI-mediated interaction may instead take the form:

> human A -> AI_1 -> representation -> AI_2 -> human B

and increasingly:

> human A -> AI_1 <-> AI_2 -> human B

The important change is not merely that machines appear in the middle. Human communication has always been technologically mediated.

The deeper change is that **expression, interpretation, selection, negotiation, and even response can all be delegated simultaneously**.

This creates a central CVT problem:

> **What happens to human agency as action passes through chains of delegation?**

## 2. Observable equivalence, relational non-equivalence

Agentic systems can produce similar observables from radically different underlying relational states.

A thoughtful message received by person B might have been produced through several different pathways:

- person A thought carefully and wrote it;
- person A thought carefully and used AI to articulate it;
- person A gave a shallow instruction and accepted an AI-generated answer;
- person A's agent answered person B's agent with minimal human attention;
- neither human meaningfully inspected the exchange.

The observable may be nearly identical.

The relational geometry is not.

Therefore:

> **Output similarity does not imply relational equivalence.**

This matters because language, polish, responsiveness, and apparent intentionality become cheaper signals as generative systems improve.

A beautiful representation no longer strongly identifies the degree of attention, judgment, commitment, or agency present at its source.

## 3. The representation-decoupling problem

Historically, many forms of representation carried substantial production cost. A careful letter, detailed application, negotiated reply, or extended analysis usually required significant human time and attention.

Generative AI sharply reduces that cost.

The result is a possible decoupling between:

- quality of representation; and
- depth of originating attention or judgment.

This does not make AI assistance intrinsically bad. AI can amplify and extend human agency.

The diagnostic question is instead:

> **Does delegation preserve contact between the representation and the purposes, judgments, commitments, and correction-capacity of the represented agent?**

If yes, delegation may amplify agency.

If no, the representation may continue to circulate after meaningful agency has thinned or disappeared from the chain.

## 4. Agency amplification versus agency displacement

Delegation is not equivalent to replacement.

An AI system that books a restaurant chosen by a person may expand that person's effective action space by reducing transaction cost while preserving purpose and veto power.

An AI system that autonomously applies to thousands of jobs the person has not considered, while employer agents autonomously reject or advance those applications without meaningful human inspection, presents a different relational structure.

The relevant distinction is:

> **Delegation can amplify agency when it expands effective action while preserving authorship, intelligibility, veto, correction, and return. Delegation can displace agency when action continues while those capacities become increasingly inaccessible.**

The same technical capability may instantiate either relation.

## 5. A provisional conservation question

This suggests a research question that can be stated cautiously:

> **Under what conditions is agency preserved, amplified, transformed, or dissipated as action passes through a chain of delegated agents?**

The word *conservation* is useful only if handled carefully. Human or artificial agency is not here assumed to behave like energy or momentum.

Rather, the conservation question asks whether the capacities that made the original action attributable and governable remain recoverable downstream.

Relevant capacities may include:

- purpose specification;
- informed authorization;
- intelligibility of action;
- provenance;
- veto power;
- correction capacity;
- responsibility attribution;
- ability to inspect intermediate transformations;
- ability to recover the originating preference or judgment;
- ability to return the system to meaningful human control.

A chain may execute the originating instruction while nevertheless dissipating several of these capacities.

## 6. Return as a test

The concept of **return** becomes central in delegated systems.

A system should not be judged only by whether it successfully departs with an instruction and produces an output.

It should also be judged by whether the resulting action can return to the source in an intelligible and governable form.

Questions of return include:

- Can the human reconstruct what the agent did?
- Can the human understand why consequential transformations occurred?
- Can the human identify where a claim originated?
- Can an error be traced rather than merely observed?
- Can authority be revoked or narrowed?
- Can the action path be corrected before irreversible harm?
- Can the human meaningfully reassume control?

Thus:

> **Delegation without return risks becoming displacement.**

Return is not identical to constant human intervention. A well-designed system may operate autonomously for long periods. The issue is whether intelligible re-entry remains structurally available.

## 7. Provenance and epistemic weight in agent chains

Multi-agent systems create a second problem: claims can acquire apparent epistemic weight merely by surviving transmission.

Consider:

> AI_1 -> AI_2 -> AI_3 -> AI_4

AI_1 introduces claim X.

AI_2 treats X as established context and acts on it.

AI_3 observes AI_2's action and may treat that action as additional evidence for X.

AI_4 receives a world already partially reorganized around X.

By the end of the chain, the original evidential basis of X may be difficult to distinguish from the downstream consequences of agents having believed X.

This creates the risk of **recursive epistemic laundering**: a claim can appear increasingly validated because earlier agents acted as though it were valid.

The diagnostic requirement is therefore not only data provenance but **claim provenance**:

> Where did this claim first enter the chain, what evidence supported it there, and which later observations are independent evidence rather than consequences of prior acceptance?

## 8. Geometry and dynamics

CVT requires attention to both relational geometry and relational dynamics.

Geometry asks:

- who can act on whom;
- who represents whom;
- where veto power sits;
- where information enters;
- where authority can be withdrawn;
- which pathways of return remain available.

Dynamics asks:

- how trust accumulates or decays;
- how errors propagate;
- how agents update from other agents' actions;
- whether correction becomes easier or harder over time;
- whether delegation expands or narrows future action space;
- whether provenance remains legible across repeated transformations.

A system can have acceptable static geometry yet develop pathological dynamics.

For example, every agent may technically be auditable while repeated reliance on prior agent output produces an increasingly self-confirming trajectory.

## 9. Relation to tau

This problem connects directly to tau as revelation.

At time t_0, two delegated systems may produce equally impressive outputs.

Through tau, their roots may diverge.

One system may increasingly:

- preserve intelligibility;
- strengthen human judgment;
- improve correction;
- regenerate trust;
- widen future action space;
- make successful return easier.

Another may increasingly:

- obscure provenance;
- normalize unattended action;
- amplify inherited errors;
- convert prior outputs into unquestioned premises;
- narrow human capacity to intervene;
- require more infrastructure to preserve the appearance of control.

Thus:

> **tau reveals whether delegation is cultivating agency or merely preserving the observable appearance of agency.**

This is a direct application of the broader principle that similar observables can arise from different roots.

## 10. Relation to FTLtauA

FTLtauA provides an orientational test for delegation.

**Freedom:** Does delegation preserve meaningful alternatives, revocability, refusal, and non-coercive re-entry?

**Truth:** Does the chain preserve provenance, distinguish evidence from inherited assertion, and remain corrigible?

**Love:** Does the system treat persons as ends whose agency is to be cultivated, or merely as sources of objectives and sinks for outputs?

**tau:** What does the trajectory reveal under repeated use, stress, error, correction, and return?

**Agency:** Does the system increase coherent capacity to understand, choose, act, correct, and reassume responsibility?

The point is not to require maximal human involvement in every operation. That would confuse agency with manual execution.

The point is to preserve the conditions under which delegation remains genuinely *someone's delegation* rather than becoming autonomous continuation detached from meaningful authorship.

## 11. A provisional agency-preservation criterion

A delegated chain can be provisionally described as **agency-preserving** when, relative to the stakes of the task, it retains sufficient:

1. **authorship** — the originating purpose remains attributable;
2. **authorization** — consequential action remains within a legitimate delegated scope;
3. **intelligibility** — relevant transformations can be understood at the level needed for judgment;
4. **provenance** — claims and actions can be traced to their sources;
5. **veto** — meaningful interruption or refusal remains possible;
6. **correction** — errors can be detected and repaired before unacceptable lock-in;
7. **return** — the delegating agent can meaningfully re-enter the loop;
8. **future capacity** — repeated delegation does not systematically destroy the capacity needed to exercise agency later.

This is not yet a formal theorem or complete metric. It is a candidate diagnostic structure.

## 12. The future-action-space test

The strongest link to CVT may be the effect of delegation on future possibility.

A delegated action should therefore be evaluated not only by whether it succeeds now, but by how it changes the agent's future action space.

Delegation may be generative when it:

- frees scarce attention for higher-order judgment;
- increases competence;
- improves access to relevant information;
- enables actions previously unavailable;
- preserves the skills needed for correction and oversight.

Delegation may be extractive or degenerative when it:

- erodes the ability to judge outputs;
- makes provenance increasingly inaccessible;
- creates dependence without reciprocal capacity growth;
- narrows meaningful alternatives;
- converts temporary convenience into structural incapacity to return.

Hence:

> **The value of delegation cannot be read from immediate performance alone; it must also be read from the future agency it creates or consumes.**

## 13. Central research seam

The central research program opened by agentic systems can therefore be stated as:

> **Study the conservation, amplification, transformation, and dissipation of agency through chains of delegation.**

This program asks not merely whether AI replaces humans, but:

> **Where does agency enter the causal chain, how is it transformed as the chain acts, what capacities survive downstream, and can agency return?**

This formulation avoids a simplistic human-versus-AI frame.

An AI may execute most of an action while human agency remains strongly preserved. Conversely, a human may nominally remain "in the loop" while meaningful agency has already dissipated.

The relevant object of study is therefore the **relational structure and trajectory of agency**, not the mere presence or absence of a human at a particular node.

## 14. Cautious proposition

A cautious conceptual proposition is:

> **In agentic systems, similar observable outputs can arise from relationally non-equivalent delegation structures. CVT should therefore evaluate delegated action not only by immediate performance, but by whether authorship, authorization, intelligibility, provenance, veto, correction, return, and future agency remain viable across the trajectory.**

This is a conceptual research claim. It motivates later formalization and empirical testing; it does not by itself establish a quantitative conservation law.
