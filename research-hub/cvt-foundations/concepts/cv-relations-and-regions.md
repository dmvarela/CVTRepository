# CV-Relations and CV-Regions

## The Relational Geometry of Cultivated Outcomes

**Status:** conceptual note; not an empirical claim, model lock, or manuscript revision.

**Boundary:** this note does not alter the frozen Stage 5.0 relational-routing protocol, reinterpret the Stage 3/4 validation record as positive evidence, authorize access to a final test, or modify `research-hub/cvt-foundations/main.tex`. Its purpose is to clarify terminology suggested by the developing CVT program after the first-generation independent-gate geometry produced mixed negative results.

## 1. Why this note exists

An early CVT operationalization asked whether a small set of candidate conditions could function as jointly necessary gates for a hosted transformation. That formulation was useful because it was strong enough to fail.

The regulated-transport validation produced counterexamples to the literal independent-gate interpretation: some hosted transformations occurred while a fitted gate failed, and some non-hosted configurations passed all fitted gates. The repository therefore preserved the negative result, withheld final-test authorization, and moved toward fresh testing of relational interactions rather than rescuing the original geometry post hoc.

This note does not claim that relational routing is correct. It introduces a broader conceptual vocabulary within which independent gates, interactions, manifolds, basins, corridors, or other geometries can be formulated and tested.

## 2. Cultivated outcomes

Let \(Y\) denote a specified **cultivated outcome**.

A cultivated outcome is an outcome that emerges only when relevant conditions, capacities, histories, and interactions are configured in ways that permit the process generating the outcome to operate. The term does not imply conscious cultivation.

Examples may include successful hosted transformation, ecological recovery, learning, trust, institutional cooperation, or other outcomes in which obtaining a terminal state is not enough unless specified forms of viability are preserved.

The outcome must be declared independently of the variables proposed to explain it. This preserves CVT's anti-circularity requirement.

## 3. Generative conditions

Let

\[
x=(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)
\]

represent the relevant state of a system.

The components of \(x\) are **generative conditions or capacities**. They are not presumed to be independent gates. Their effects may depend on one another, on history, on forcing, and on the current configuration.

Thus the contribution of one condition may be conditional:

\[
\frac{\partial Y}{\partial x_i}
=
f(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n).
\]

What \(x_i\) does may depend on what it is doing **with** the rest of the configuration.

Agriculture provides the simplest intuition. Water, nutrients, sunlight, soil, temperature, drainage, seed quality, and care may all matter, but their effects are relational. More fertilizer cannot compensate for no water; yet the amount of water required depends on temperature, soil, humidity, plant type, developmental stage, and time.

## 4. CV-relation

A **CV-relation** is the organized configuration among differentiated conditions, capacities, or agents relevant to the cultivation of a specified outcome.

We may write:

\[
R_{CV}(x).
\]

The relation is not an additional ingredient added to the variables. It is their configuration.

Accordingly,

\[
R_{CV}(x) \neq \sum_i x_i
\]

in general.

Two systems containing similar amounts of the same components may occupy very different CV-relations because the components are organized differently.

This separates **having the ingredients** from **being configured so that the ingredients can generate the outcome**.

## 5. CV-region

For a cultivated outcome \(Y\), define the **CV-region**

\[
\mathcal{R}_{CV}(Y)
\]

as the region of configuration space within which the generative relations required for \(Y\) remain viable under the declared horizon and domain-specific conditions.

Thus

\[
x\in\mathcal{R}_{CV}(Y)
\]

means that the current configuration is compatible with cultivation of \(Y\), subject to measurement uncertainty, scope conditions, and independently declared viability criteria.

The geometry of \(\mathcal{R}_{CV}(Y)\) is an empirical question.

It might approximate a hard intersection,

\[
x_i>\theta_i \quad \forall i,
\]

or instead resemble a curved manifold, disconnected basins, a corridor, an interaction surface, a history-dependent region, or another geometry.

CVT should not decide this geometry in advance.

## 6. Non-compensability revisited

The failure of an independent hard-gate model does not imply that all conditions are freely substitutable.

Some deficits cannot be compensated indefinitely by increasing another input. A plant without water cannot be rescued by unlimited fertilizer. But this does not imply a universal water threshold independent of temperature, soil, humidity, developmental stage, or other variables.

CVT therefore distinguishes several possibilities:

- **strict non-compensability:** no admissible increase in other variables can compensate for the loss of a specified condition;
- **conditional non-compensability:** compensation fails only under specified states, histories, or forcing regimes;
- **configuration-level non-compensability:** a relational configuration becomes non-viable in a way that cannot be repaired by increasing any single remaining input without changing the relation itself.

The empirical question becomes:

> Which relational failures can be compensated, under what configurations, and where does compensation cease to be possible?

This turns non-compensability from an assumed algebraic property into a property of the geometry to be discovered.

## 7. CV-trajectory and tau

A CV-relation is not necessarily stationary.

Let

\[
x(t)
\]

denote the evolving configuration. A **CV-trajectory** is the path

\[
\Gamma_{CV}=\{x(t):t\in[t_0,t_1]\}
\]

through configuration space.

A snapshot can therefore be misleading. A system may occupy an apparently viable state while travelling toward collapse, or temporarily leave an ordinary operating region while retaining enough restorative capacity to recover.

This gives \(\tau\) a specific analytical role. The relevant questions are not only:

> Where is the system now?

but also:

> Where is it going, what capacities are being consumed along the way, and can it return?

The relevant region may therefore be history-conditioned:

\[
\mathcal{R}_{CV}(Y\mid H_t).
\]

History or maturity must remain conceptually distinct from presently available restorative reserve.

In this descriptive CVT sense, \(\tau\) tracks trajectory through viability space. It asks how the configuration moves, what it consumes, what it preserves, and whether return or recovery remains possible.

For FTLtauA, \(\tau\) carries an additional revelatory role. It is the temporal axis through which trajectory, stress, correction, sacrifice, persistence, and return disclose whether a claimed good is rooted, counterfeit, or merely maintained by compensatory effort.

A compact bridge is:

> **In CVT, \(\tau\) tracks trajectory through viability space; in FTLtauA, \(\tau\) reveals what the trajectory actually serves, preserves, consumes, and builds.**

## 8. Relational geometry

**Relational geometry** names the structure of the CV-region and the relations within it.

It may include boundaries, basins, corridors, gradients, bottlenecks, interaction surfaces, hysteresis, recovery paths, irreversible boundaries, and regions of conditional or strict non-compensability.

A CV-relation is a configuration.

A CV-region is the set or region of configurations compatible with the specified cultivated outcome and viability conditions.

A CV-trajectory is movement through that region over time.

Relational geometry describes the shape and structure of the space through which those trajectories move.

## 9. Relational routing

The repository's Stage 5 relational-routing program can be located within this vocabulary without being presupposed by it.

Relational routing is one candidate empirical mechanism by which different input configurations may route toward different outcomes. If supported, it may reveal part of the relational geometry of the bounded regulated-transport system. If rejected, CV-relation and CV-region remain conceptual terms within which alternative candidate geometries can still be formulated.

This separation is essential:

> Stage 5 tests a particular candidate model. It does not test or validate this entire conceptual vocabulary.

## 10. FTLtauA is a compass, not another gate model

For systems involving agents, viability alone does not determine which viable configurations ought to be pursued.

A dictatorship can persist. A coercive institution can reproduce itself. An abusive relation can possess strong self-maintaining dynamics. Persistence therefore cannot be equated with goodness.

FTLtauA serves a different function. It is an **orientational compass**, not another set of CVT gates.

It asks whether an agent-involving trajectory remains oriented toward:

- **F — Freedom**;
- **T — Truth**;
- **L — Love**;
- **tau — the revelatory temporal axis through which trajectory discloses what a claimed good serves, preserves, consumes, and builds**;
- **A — coherent Agency**.

CVT can ask:

> Can this configuration remain viable, what does it generate, and what does it consume?

FTLtauA can ask:

> Is this a trajectory along which Freedom, Truth, Love, revelatory temporal depth, and coherent Agency remain meaningfully available?

This distinction keeps CVT analytically useful for harmful systems without silently converting viability into moral approval.

Put simply:

> **\(\tau\) is where the fruit reveals the root.**

## 11. Right relation

The phrase **right relation** remains useful in normative, philosophical, and theological contexts, but it should not function as the primitive technical term of CVT.

A proposed distinction is:

> **CV-relation:** descriptive and dynamical.
>
> **Right relation:** normative and orientational.

For agent-involving systems, *right relation* may name a CV-relation whose trajectory remains compatible with the FTLtauA compass and whose apparent viability does not depend on systematic destruction, ownership, or falsification of another participant's agency or standing.

This preserves the older language while avoiding the implication that every stable or generative configuration is morally "right."

## 12. Cultivation, extraction, and the grasping error

A cultivated outcome is not generally obtainable by applying force directly to the visible form of the desired result.

A plant is not made to grow by pulling on it. The pulling may create the temporary appearance of greater height while damaging the roots that generate actual growth.

This motivates a distinction between **cultivation** and **extraction**:

- cultivation preserves or develops the generative conditions from which the outcome can continue to arise;
- extraction obtains a proximate output while consuming or bypassing those generative conditions.

Through time, the difference may appear as increasing **maintenance dependence**: the apparent outcome persists only while compensatory intervention continues. In the project's informal vocabulary, this is the difference between **roots** and **malabares**.

A useful temporal diagnostic is therefore:

> Can the intervention be reduced or the hand opened without the cultivated good collapsing?

If yes, capacity may have been developed. If no, the system may only be held in the desired shape.

This can be stated in the language of mirage and cultivation:

> **A rooted good increasingly lets reality carry the claim; a mirage requires the claim to carry reality.**

## 13. Emerging CVT proposition

A cautious conceptual formulation is:

> **Cultivated outcomes may depend not merely on the presence or magnitude of relevant conditions, but on their relational configuration through time. CVT studies the regions, trajectories, boundaries, and relational geometries within which such outcomes can emerge while specified forms of viability are preserved.**

This is not a universal law and is not established by the current validation record. It defines a research program.

The central empirical question is no longer only:

\[
\text{Which gates must pass?}
\]

It becomes:

\[
\boxed{\text{What configurations can cultivate this outcome, and what makes those configurations viable through time?}}
\]

## 14. Research boundary

This note should remain firewall-separated from any frozen or preregistered validation protocol unless a future protocol explicitly incorporates these definitions before data generation or inspection.

In particular, this note must not be used to:

- relabel a failed independent-gate result as confirmation of CVT;
- tune Stage 5 after inspection of held-out results;
- redefine outcomes after results are known;
- treat relational routing as validated merely because it fits the vocabulary introduced here;
- replace domain-native variables or independently declared viability outcomes with metaphorical proxies.

The framework remains correction-survivable only if candidate geometries are allowed to fail.
