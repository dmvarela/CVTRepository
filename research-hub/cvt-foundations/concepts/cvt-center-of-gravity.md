# CVT Center of Gravity: Relations That Reproduce or Consume Their Own Viability

**Status:** Foundational concept note  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Program:** Coherence Viability Theory (CVT)

## Core claim

CVT should not exist merely to restate the broader idea of **relational viability**. If CVT adds nothing beyond that established problem-space, it should be dissolved into the relational-viability tradition.

The surviving and more precise role of CVT is this:

> **Coherence Viability Theory studies the endogenous dynamics by which relations preserve, regenerate, consume, or destroy the conditions of their own future viability.**

A more intuitive formulation is:

> **CVT asks whether a relation is growing the constituents from which healthy relation can continue, or quietly consuming them while appearing to succeed.**

This distinction is often not visible in a snapshot.

## Why this is different from apparent success

Two relations can generate the same observable outcome at time `t` while having very different effects on the conditions required for future viable relation.

Schematically:

```math
Y_A(t)=Y_B(t)
```

while

```math
\Delta C_A > 0,
\qquad
\Delta C_B < 0,
```

where `C` denotes constitutive relational capacities such as trust, agency, restorative reserve, identity-preserving organization, receptivity, legitimacy, or other domain-specific conditions.

Relation A may be **regenerative**:

```text
interaction
    → trust / capacity / agency / reserve
    → greater future relational possibility
```

Relation B may be **extractive**:

```text
interaction
    → proximate output
    → trust depletion / agency loss / reserve consumption
    → narrower future relational possibility
```

Both may look successful now.

Only one is reproducing the conditions from which healthy relation can continue.

## Maintaining a relation versus a relation maintaining itself

A central CVT diagnostic is therefore:

> **How do we distinguish a relation being maintained from a relation maintaining itself?**

A system may appear stable because external effort, coercion, compensation, hidden reserve consumption, or continuous intervention is holding it in shape. This is **maintenance dependence**, not necessarily cultivated capacity.

In informal project language:

```text
roots ≠ malabares
```

A cultivated relation increasingly allows its own generative conditions to carry the outcome. An extractive or counterfeit relation requires increasing compensatory effort to preserve the appearance of the outcome.

This does not imply that healthy systems require zero maintenance. It means the relevant question is whether maintenance develops, preserves, and restores generative capacity or merely substitutes for its erosion.

## Relational viability as ecology; CVT as changing topography

Relational viability supplies the broader inherited ecology: relations, position, function, meaning, resources, and viability cannot be reduced to isolated components.

CVT focuses more narrowly on the **changing topography of that relational ecology**.

Let `x_i` denote the state of participant or process `i`, and `L` the viability landscape within which interaction occurs:

```math
\dot{x}_i=f_i(x_i,x_{-i},L).
```

CVT emphasizes that the landscape need not be fixed:

```math
\dot{L}=g(L,x_1,\ldots,x_n).
```

The participants move through the landscape, but their interactions can also rewrite it.

Thus history is not merely a path **over** a landscape. Repeated interaction may deepen, flatten, fragment, repair, or redirect the landscape itself.

This gives a compact CVT intuition:

> **The relation writes the landscape on which its next act will occur.**

## The future-viability question

CVT is therefore concerned not only with present viability but with what present interaction does to the **future viability landscape**.

Two systems may both be viable now:

```math
V_A(t)=V_B(t)=1,
```

while one cultivates future viability,

```math
\frac{d\mathcal V_A^{future}}{dt}>0,
```

and the other consumes it,

```math
\frac{d\mathcal V_B^{future}}{dt}<0.
```

This distinction links several existing CVT research threads:

- poisoned wells;
- cultivation versus extraction;
- restorative reserve;
- trust decay and trust repair;
- relational backcasting;
- non-annihilating correction;
- viable delegation and appealability;
- development basins;
- reproductive capability;
- basin capture and recoverability;
- preservation of agency under constraint;
- maintenance dependence;
- path dependence and hysteresis.

These are not separate metaphors if they instantiate the same structural problem: **present outputs can be produced either by reproducing or by consuming the conditions of future relation.**

## Durability is not mere persistence

CVT must not identify durability with simple duration or persistence.

Harmful relations can be highly persistent. A coercive institution, dictatorship, addiction, exploitative organization, or abusive relation may reproduce itself for long periods.

A healthy durable bond therefore means something more demanding:

> **the capacity of a relation to continue, transform, correct, recover, or where appropriate separate without systematically destroying the constitutive viability of its participants and relational field.**

Accordingly:

```text
persistence ≠ health
continuation ≠ viability
compliance ≠ agency
stability ≠ regenerative durability
```

## Role of time

Time is essential because regenerative and extractive relations may be observationally similar at one moment.

A relation can produce cooperation, stability, output, or apparent harmony while consuming trust, reserve, legitimacy, or agency beneath the visible outcome.

Through time, their trajectories separate.

This gives `τ` an important diagnostic role in the broader CVT/FTLτA architecture: trajectory reveals what the relation preserves, consumes, restores, and makes possible next.

The relevant question is not only:

> What outcome exists now?

but:

> What did producing this outcome do to the conditions from which the next outcome must be generated?

## Relation to relational viability

CVT is situated **inside** the relational-viability tradition, not above it and not in competition with it.

A useful division of labor is:

```text
RELATIONAL VIABILITY
Broad inherited problem-space:
What makes a relational configuration viable?

    CVT
    Dynamics and geometry of relational viability under transformation:
    How is viability preserved, regenerated, consumed, damaged,
    repaired, routed, and carried through time?

        FTLτA
        Normative orientation for agency-bearing relations:
        Does the trajectory preserve the non-substitutable
        conditions of Freedom, Truth, Love, temporal disclosure,
        and coherent Agency?
```

This placement must remain defeasible. If the relational-viability literature already supplies the same transformation dynamics with equal or greater precision, CVT should not defend a redundant label.

## Falsification and scholarly boundary

The existence of a coherent research question does not establish the empirical truth, novelty, or universality of CVT's candidate mechanisms.

CVT must still demonstrate that its constructs provide explanatory, predictive, or design value beyond established relational viability, viability theory, resilience, autopoiesis, dynamical systems, control theory, and domain-native approaches.

The project should therefore preserve the following rule:

> **No sentimental protection of the acronym.**

CVT survives only to the extent that it contributes a clearer, testable account of relational transformation, landscape deformation, regenerative versus extractive dynamics, path dependence, recoverability, or future viability.

## Center-of-gravity formulation

The current best concise statement is:

> **CVT studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

Expanded:

> **CVT studies whether relational processes reproduce, restore, or deepen the constituents required for healthy durable relation, or instead consume those constituents while maintaining an apparently successful state.**

This is the current center of gravity of the CVT research program.
