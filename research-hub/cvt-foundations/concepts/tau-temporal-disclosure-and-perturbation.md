# τ as Temporal Disclosure: Perturbation, Trajectory, and Revealed Relational Structure

**Status:** Foundational working concept note  
**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Program:** CVT / FTLτA  
**Provenance:** Max–Lucian inquiry

## Core refinement

Earlier versions of the framework used `τ` to mean **Trying**: the quality of effort used to preserve or expand viable possibilities before commitment. That insight remains useful, but it is not sufficient to capture the role now assigned to `τ`.

The current refinement is:

> **F, T, L, and A describe what must remain viable. τ reveals whether they actually do.**

This suggests that FTLτA should not be interpreted as five interchangeable ethical scores. A cleaner geometry is:

```math
x(t) = \big(F(t),T(t),L(t),A(t)\big),
```

with `τ` representing the temporal domain through which the trajectory of those coordinates is disclosed:

```math
\gamma: \tau \mapsto \big(F(\tau),T(\tau),L(\tau),A(\tau)\big).
```

Thus the framework is at least four-dimensional **plus time**.

## Why time is not merely another coordinate

Freedom, Truth, Love, and Agency can be evaluated at a moment, however imperfectly. But many relational properties cannot be established from a snapshot.

Two systems may make nearly identical claims at `t0`:

```text
we are safe
we care
we respect freedom
we tell the truth
we preserve agency
```

and may even produce similar visible outcomes.

The distinction can become legible only after challenge.

A perturbation may be:

- new evidence;
- discovered error;
- dissent;
- refusal;
- scarcity;
- external shock;
- failure;
- loss;
- time pressure;
- a demand that makes a stated value costly to preserve.

The response to that perturbation reveals whether the claimed values were structural or merely convenient.

## Perturbation as a disclosure operator

Let `δ` denote a disturbance or challenge. Then:

```math
x(t) \xrightarrow{\;\delta\;} x(t+\Delta t).
```

The ethically informative object is not only the pre-perturbation state, but the response:

```math
R(\delta)=\Delta(F,T,L,A\mid\delta).
```

This gives a candidate principle:

> **Perturbation reveals relational commitments by making them costly.**

Everyone can claim to value truth when truth is flattering.

Everyone can claim to value freedom when nobody refuses.

Everyone can claim to value agency when the other agrees.

Everyone can claim to love when care requires no sacrifice of prestige, control, convenience, or self-protection.

The deeper test is what happens when preserving the claimed value becomes expensive.

## From Trying to temporal disclosure

The earlier concept of Trying identified something real: ethical process matters, and an agent should not collapse the action space prematurely when more viable possibilities can still be explored.

But Trying remains episode-bound. It asks whether the agent made an adequate effort before choosing.

Temporal disclosure asks a larger set of questions:

```text
What happened after the attempt?
What did the system learn?
How did it respond to correction?
Did truth become more available or less?
Did freedom widen or narrow?
Did love remain non-instrumental under cost?
Did agency become stronger or more dependent?
Did the relation develop restorative reserve?
Did the next response become better because of the previous failure?
```

Thus `Trying` is retained as one observable inside the larger temporal architecture, but it no longer exhausts `τ`.

## Non-compensation and dimensionality

The framework is non-compensatory because the coordinates represent distinct constitutive conditions of viable agency-bearing relation.

A gain in one coordinate cannot automatically purchase collapse in another.

For example:

```text
high welfare does not compensate for destroyed agency
high truth does not compensate for coercion
high freedom does not compensate for deception
benevolent intention does not compensate for instrumentalization
```

A scalar score can hide these failures by allowing tradeoffs that alter the kind of relation itself.

The appropriate object is therefore closer to a viability region:

```math
\mathcal K = \{x : F\ge F_{min},\;T\ge T_{min},\;L\ge L_{min},\;A\ge A_{min}\},
```

with ethical evaluation concerned not only with whether `x(t)` lies in `K`, but whether the trajectory preserves future reachability of viable states.

## Relation to CVT

This refinement connects FTLτA directly to CVT's center-of-gravity question.

Two relations can occupy similar states now while writing different future landscapes.

A regenerative trajectory may respond to challenge through:

```text
error
→ truthful recognition
→ correction
→ repair
→ learning
→ increased future viability
```

An extractive or brittle trajectory may respond through:

```text
error
→ concealment
→ reputation or control defense
→ suppressed correction
→ unchanged or degraded structure
→ increased future vulnerability
```

Both may appear stable at the moment of comparison.

Only time distinguishes whether the relation is cultivating or consuming the conditions from which its future must be generated.

## Love under perturbation

The present development of `L` makes temporal testing especially important.

A relation may appear loving while the other remains useful, compliant, understandable, or easy to care for.

The stronger test is whether the other's viable good still matters when:

- reciprocity fails;
- recognition is incomplete;
- disagreement appears;
- correction is required;
- the other refuses;
- care becomes costly.

This is where the theological principle **love moves first** becomes operationally distinguishable from preference, possession, paternalism, or optimization.

A benevolent controller may preserve welfare while consuming freedom and agency. Under FTLτA this is not a compensated success; it is a change in relational kind.

## Candidate formulation

The current strongest formulation is:

> **FTLA specifies the non-substitutable conditions of viable agency-bearing relation; τ is the temporal disclosure through which their persistence, failure, repair, and regeneration become observable.**

Or more simply:

> **F, T, L, and A say what must remain. τ shows what remains when the relation is tested.**

## Research consequences

1. Treat `τ` as a trajectory parameter and disclosure dimension rather than merely a fifth scalar objective.
2. Preserve `Trying` as a process-level observable or subcomponent of temporal adequacy rather than discarding it.
3. Evaluate systems under perturbation, not only at equilibrium or under cooperative conditions.
4. Distinguish declared values from revealed relational response functions.
5. Measure whether correction increases future viability or merely restores surface appearance.
6. Test whether a relation remains inside the viable region while preserving reachability, recoverability, and non-instrumental regard over time.
7. Use case studies of engineering, governance, AI interaction, institutions, and relationships to compare similar initial claims with divergent responses to challenge.

## Methodological caution

A dramatic response to one perturbation does not by itself establish the full moral character of a person or system. Context, information, available alternatives, urgency, and capacity remain relevant.

The claim is narrower:

> **Temporal response contains information about relational structure that a static declaration cannot provide.**

This note therefore treats perturbation as a diagnostic source of evidence, not as an infallible moral test.
