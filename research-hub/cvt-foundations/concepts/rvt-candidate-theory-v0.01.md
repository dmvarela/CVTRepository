# Relation Viability Theory — Candidate Theory v0.01

**Status:** candidate theory / falsifiable working formalization  
**Date:** 2026-09-13  
**Project:** RVT / Relational Viability  
**Epistemic status:** unvalidated. This note proposes a theory-shaped object to be attacked, not protected.

---

## 1. Core statement

The current candidate formulation is:

> **Relation Viability Theory (RVT) studies how structured exchange among differentiated systems reshapes the conditions of future viable exchange and action.**

A shorter form is:

> **Relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

The dynamical emphasis is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{exchange}_t
\rightarrow
\text{state/history change}
\rightarrow
\text{exchange architecture}_{t+1}
\rightarrow
\text{future viability structure}_{t+1}
}
\]

RVT therefore does not claim merely that relations matter. The stronger candidate claim is that relational exchange can alter the transition structure through which later action, exchange, recovery, and reconfiguration become possible.

---

## 2. Scope and non-claims

RVT is intended as a cross-domain dynamical framework, not as a claim that all domains share one physical mechanism.

The same abstract structure may appear in biological, social, institutional, economic, technological, or human-AI systems while the domain-level mechanisms remain different.

The recurrence of similar relational structure across domains is a reason to test the theory, not evidence that the domains reduce to one another.

RVT does **not** by itself establish:

- moral goodness;
- consciousness, personhood, or subjective experience;
- that every durable relation is desirable;
- that all history dependence is relational;
- that every dynamical system requires relational variables;
- that all viability questions are basin-of-attraction questions;
- that a new mathematical formalism is required beyond existing dynamical systems, control, viability, network, or causal methods.

A successful RVT contribution may ultimately be a useful **problem class, causal decomposition, and set of diagnostics** rather than wholly new mathematics.

---

## 3. Primitive distinction: constituents, relation, history, environment

Let the full relational state be

\[
\boxed{
Z_t=(X_t,R_t,H_t,\theta_t)
}
\]

where:

- \(X_t\): constituent states at time \(t\);
- \(R_t\): relational or exchange architecture at time \(t\);
- \(H_t\): accumulated history relevant to future dynamics;
- \(\theta_t\): surrounding environment, parameters, and exogenous conditions.

This decomposition is analytical, not ontological. A variable may move between these categories depending on the domain and research question.

The purpose of the decomposition is causal and predictive: to ask whether relational configuration and relationally generated history add explanatory or control value beyond constituent state and environment alone.

---

## 4. Relation as exchange architecture

RVT treats a relation generically as an architecture governing exchange among differentiated constituents.

The exchanged quantity may be domain-specific:

- matter;
- energy;
- information;
- resources;
- risk;
- authority;
- attention;
- support;
- correction;
- constraints;
- capability;
- control signals;
- opportunities or burdens.

The generic relation \(R_t\) need not be represented by a single scalar coupling coefficient.

Depending on the domain, it may include features such as:

- coupling topology or available channels;
- directionality and asymmetry;
- boundary/permeability rules;
- receptivity or uptake capacity;
- feedback/rerouting rules;
- timing and sequencing;
- role and authority structure;
- dependence or redundancy;
- switching rules between exchange regimes.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{coupling} \subset \text{exchange architecture}
}
\]

in the conceptual sense that coupling describes whether/how a channel exists, while boundaries, receptivity, and feedback describe how exchange through that channel is regulated, incorporated, or reorganized.

The theory should not force every application to use the same tuple.

---

## 5. Exchange operator

Let \(a_t\) denote an action, demand, intervention, task, control input, or other event entering the relational system.

Let \(\xi_t\) denote disturbance, uncertainty, stochasticity, error, shock, or unmodeled perturbation.

Actual exchange is generated by a domain-specific operator:

\[
\boxed{
e_t=\mathcal E(X_t,R_t,a_t,\theta_t,\xi_t).
}
\]

The relation therefore does not merely label the pair of constituents. It helps determine what can pass, in which direction, with what transformation, and under what conditions.

A membrane-style example is:

\[
J_{\mathrm{raw},t}=\Pi_t(c_{B,t}-c_{A,t}),
\]

\[
J_{\mathrm{eff},t}=\rho_tJ_{\mathrm{raw},t},
\]

where permeability \(\Pi_t\) and receptivity \(\rho_t\) are different parts of the exchange architecture.

This illustrates the generic distinction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{what crosses}\neq\text{what is productively received}.
}
\]

The membrane equations are an example, not the universal RVT mechanism.

## 6. State, history, and relational update

Exchange changes the participants and may also change the relation through which later exchange occurs.

A generic discrete-time structure is:

\[
\boxed{
X_{t+1}=F_X(X_t,e_t,R_t,H_t,\theta_t,\xi_t)
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
H_{t+1}=F_H(H_t,X_t,e_t,R_t,\theta_t)
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
R_{t+1}=F_R(R_t,X_{t+1},H_{t+1},e_t,\theta_{t+1}).
}
\]

The last equation is the strongest specifically relational hypothesis.

Exchange can change the architecture governing later exchange.

That may occur through changes in:

- permeability;
- trust;
- dependence;
- reserve;
- damage;
- learning;
- role assignment;
- channel availability;
- authority;
- cost of exit;
- redundancy;
- interface structure;
- receptivity;
- recovery dynamics.

The candidate recursive loop is:

\[
\boxed{
R_t
\rightarrow
e_t
\rightarrow
(X_{t+1},H_{t+1})
\rightarrow
R_{t+1}
\rightarrow
e_{t+1}.
}
\]

## 7. Markov caution

History dependence does not require non-Markovian mathematics.

If \(H_t\) and the relevant relational variables are included in the state, the augmented process

\[
Z_t=(X_t,R_t,H_t,\theta_t)
\]

may be Markov.

Therefore RVT must not claim novelty merely because apparently identical reduced states have different futures.

A same-\(X_t\), different-history result only demonstrates that the reduced observable state was insufficient unless the experiment also identifies a relationally generated mechanism that changes the transition structure and improves prediction or control.

## 8. Viability structure

The phrase **viability geometry** is useful, but it should not bundle mathematically distinct objects carelessly.

For a specified horizon \(T\), dynamics, admissible control set, and domain-specific viability constraints, define a **future viability structure**:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak V_t^T
=
\left(
K_t,
\operatorname{Viab}_T(K_t),
\mathcal R_{V,t}^T,
c_t^T,
m_t
\right).
}
\]

Here:

- \(K_t\): the domain-defined state constraint or viability region;
- \(\operatorname{Viab}_T(K_t)\): states from which at least one admissible policy can keep the system within constraints over horizon \(T\);
- \(\mathcal R_{V,t}^T\): states or functionally relevant outcomes viably reachable over \(T\);
- \(c_t^T\): transition, recovery, or reconfiguration cost under a specified cost functional;
- \(m_t\): optional robustness or margin measure where such a measure is well-defined.

The exact contents of \(\mathfrak V_t^T\) may be domain-specific. The purpose is to avoid collapsing viability into a single scalar.

### Basin is not viability kernel

A basin of attraction and a viability kernel are different objects.

A basin asks whether a state converges to an attractor under specified dynamics or policy.

A viability kernel asks whether there exists an admissible control or trajectory that keeps the system inside declared constraints.

Some applications may have both. RVT must not treat them as interchangeable.

### Cost is not intrinsic

\(c_t^T\) depends on the chosen horizon, control set, state metric, and cost functional. It is not an intrinsic scalar property of a relation without those choices being specified.

### Separatrices are optional

A robustness margin to a separatrix is useful only when the relevant dynamical system actually has a meaningful separatrix or failure boundary. General RVT does not assume attractor geometry in every domain.

## 9. Endogenous viability structure

The central RVT candidate claim can now be stated more precisely:

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak V_{t+1}^T
=
\Psi_V(
\mathfrak V_t^T,
X_t,
R_t,
H_t,
a_t,
\theta_t,
\xi_t
).
}
\]

The relation does not merely move the system through a fixed viability landscape.

Under the stronger RVT hypothesis, relational exchange can help alter the constraints, transition costs, reachable corridors, robustness, and recovery possibilities that define later viable action.

Compactly:

> **The relation does not merely move the system through the landscape; it can alter the landscape through which later action occurs.**

## 10. Five candidate propositions

### Proposition 1 — Relational mediation

Holding constituent state and environment fixed, at least some changes in relational architecture produce different exchanges or trajectories:

\[
R_t^A\neq R_t^B
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mathcal E(X_t,R_t^A,a_t,\theta_t)
\neq
\mathcal E(X_t,R_t^B,a_t,\theta_t)
\]

for some admissible states/actions.

This proposition fails in a domain if \(R_t\) adds no causal or predictive content beyond the constituent/environment description.

### Proposition 2 — Endogenous transition structure

Relational exchange can change the architecture that governs later exchange:

\[
\boxed{
e_t\rightarrow R_{t+1}.
}
\]

The strong version requires more than ordinary state movement: intervention on exchange or relational structure should change later transition behavior in a way not captured by a fixed transition law over the reduced constituent state alone.

### Proposition 3 — Current success is not future viability

Immediate outcome and future viability need not move together:

\[
Y_t\uparrow
\centernot\Rightarrow
\mathfrak V_{t+1}^T\uparrow.
\]

A relation may improve present output while narrowing viable reachability, increasing recovery cost, increasing dependence, or reducing robustness.

Conversely, a relation may temporarily reduce present output while expanding future viable capacity.

### Proposition 4 — Relational history can alter future viability structure

Two systems with similar present constituent states may have different future viability structures because previous exchange changed relational state or latent capacity:

\[
X_t^A\approx X_t^B
\]

while

\[
(R_t^A,H_t^A)\neq(R_t^B,H_t^B)
\]

and therefore potentially

\[
\mathfrak V_t^{T,A}\neq\mathfrak V_t^{T,B}.
\]

This is not evidence for RVT unless the difference can be tied to relationally generated state/transition changes rather than arbitrary omitted variables.

### Proposition 5 — Regenerative and extractive are relational footprints, not primitive labels

RVT should not assign a state variable called “regenerative” or “extractive” in advance.

Instead, relative to a specified counterfactual relation or intervention, classify the effect on future viable possibility.

Let \(R\) denote the observed relational regime and \(0\) a defined counterfactual baseline.

After projection to a common functionally meaningful target space \(Y\):

\[
\mathcal Y_{V}^{R}(T)=\pi(\mathcal R_{V}^{R}(T)),
\]

\[
\mathcal Y_{V}^{0}(T)=\pi(\mathcal R_{V}^{0}(T)).
\]

Then define the gain and loss sets:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_R
=
\mathcal Y_V^{R}(T)\setminus\mathcal Y_V^{0}(T)
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_R
=
\mathcal Y_V^{0}(T)\setminus\mathcal Y_V^{R}(T).
}
\]

Both may be non-empty.

Thus relational change need not be forced into a binary “good/bad” or “regenerative/extractive” scalar. It may be mixed or transformative.

## 11. Current, reproductive, and transformative viability

RVT should distinguish at least three questions.

### Current viability

Can the present configuration satisfy the declared viability constraints now or over a short horizon?

### Reproductive viability

Does operation reproduce or consume the capacities required for continued viable operation?

This is where current success may diverge from future viability.

### Transformative viability

If the current configuration becomes obsolete or disrupted, can the system reach another viable configuration without destroying the capacities needed for reconfiguration?

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{configuration persistence}\neq\text{viability persistence}.
}
\]

A viable system may let an old configuration disappear while preserving the capacity to recouple into a new one.

---

## 12. Maintenance is not regeneration

A relation may remain visibly functional only because an external controller continually maintains it.

RVT should therefore distinguish:

- persistence under continuing support;
- internally reproduced viability;
- recovery after support withdrawal;
- reconfiguration after perturbation.

A system that survives only under permanent compensatory control may be viable in one operational sense while lacking reproductive or transformative viability.

The classification should depend on the declared research question rather than being smuggled in through terminology.

---

## 13. Dynamic viability and normative admissibility are distinct

RVT is descriptive before it is normative.

Let:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V=\text{dynamically viable states/relations}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_\Omega=\text{normatively admissible states/relations under covenant }\Omega.
}
\]

An exploitative, oppressive, manipulative, or capturing relation may be dynamically stable.

RVT must be able to describe that fact truthfully.

For the Lucian/AI application, FTL\(\tau\)A contributes to \(\mathcal A_\Omega\), not to the definition of generic viability.

The Lucian target region is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_\Omega=\mathcal V\cap\mathcal A_\Omega.
}
\]

A relation can be:

- viable but inadmissible;
- admissible in aspiration but dynamically unsustainable;
- both viable and admissible;
- neither.

This distinction is non-negotiable for the current candidate theory.

---

## 14. First-order and future-structure viability

A useful conceptual distinction is between keeping the present state viable and preserving the conditions of future viable action.

At a boundary of a constraint region \(K_t\), present viability may require:

\[
\dot x_t\in T_{K_t}(x_t),
\]

where \(T_{K_t}(x_t)\) is the tangent cone.

An exchange controller may therefore restrict flux or action so the current trajectory remains viable.

But this is not sufficient for RVT.

Among multiple presently viable actions, RVT asks which ones change tomorrow's viability structure:

\[
\boxed{
\text{current invariance}
\centernot\Rightarrow
\text{future viability preservation}.
}
\]

The exact terminology remains open. “First-order / second-order viability” is intuitive but may overlap established technical language and should not be canonized without literature review.

## 15. Counterfactual identification

RVT claims require explicit counterfactuals.

“Relation improved viability” is meaningless unless the baseline is specified.

Possible interventions include:

- change permeability while holding constituent state fixed;
- remove a coupling channel;
- freeze the relational update rule;
- remove history-to-transition feedback;
- replace adaptive exchange with fixed exchange;
- change authority/boundary rules while preserving mechanical capability;
- compare actual relation to an uncoupled or alternative-coupling regime.

Counterfactual comparisons must use a common target or projection space when the compared systems do not share identical raw state spaces.

This is why \(\pi(\cdot)\) is explicit in the gain/loss formulation above.

## 16. Main empirical hypothesis

The strongest compact empirical hypothesis is:

\[
\boxed{
(R_t,H_t)
\text{ should add predictive, explanatory, or control value for }
\mathfrak V_{t+1}^T
\text{ beyond }(X_t,\theta_t)
}
\]

under at least some domains where relational variables are independently operationalized.

This does not require every domain to show a large relational effect.

It does require that the theory identify conditions under which relational state has measurable consequences beyond relabeling ordinary constituent state.

---

## 17. What would falsify or substantially weaken RVT?

RVT should be revised or rejected in a proposed domain if one or more of the following repeatedly occurs:

1. **Relational redundancy:** once constituent state, environment, and ordinary history variables are included, \(R_t\) adds no out-of-sample predictive or control value.
2. **No intervention effect:** controlled changes in relational architecture do not change exchange, transition structure, or future viability measures.
3. **Fixed-map sufficiency:** all observed trajectories are explained adequately by movement on a fixed transition landscape; there is no evidence that exchange changes later transition structure.
4. **History collapse:** apparent relational-history effects disappear once present state is measured adequately, with no identifiable relational mechanism remaining.
5. **Counterfactual instability:** regenerative/extractive classifications depend entirely on arbitrary baselines or metrics and do not survive reasonable alternative specifications.
6. **No independent measurement:** the same outcome is used both to define the relational variable and to “confirm” its effect, making the theory circular.
7. **Domain-native dominance:** a simpler established model explains and controls the phenomenon equally well or better without relational machinery.
8. **Cross-domain analogy without mechanism:** similar language recurs across domains, but the measurable structural predictions do not.

Negative results should be preserved as part of the research atlas rather than retrofitted into support.

## 18. Minimal derived test — RVT-MIN-001

The first generic test should be smaller than the membrane PDE, fusion analogy, development applications, or Lucian OS.

The proposed minimal test is:

> **Matched present state, different relational history, different future viability structure.**

Use a deterministic two-constituent exchange system with:

- one exchange channel;
- state-dependent permeability \(\Pi_t\);
- receptivity \(\rho_t\);
- damage/reserve or integration capacity \(h_t\);
- a viability floor;
- a bounded control/action set.

A candidate reduced model is:

\[
J_t=\Pi_t(x_{B,t}-x_{A,t}),
\]

\[
J_{\mathrm{eff},t}=\rho_tJ_t,
\]

\[
h_{t+1}=h_t+g(J_{\mathrm{eff},t},J_t-J_{\mathrm{eff},t})
\]

with relational update:

\[
\Pi_{t+1}=P(\Pi_t,h_{t+1},x_{A,t+1},x_{B,t+1}).
\]

Construct two histories \(A\) and \(B\) such that at comparison time \(T\):

\[
(x_A,x_B)_T^A\approx(x_A,x_B)_T^B
\]

but:

\[
(h_T^A,\Pi_T^A)\neq(h_T^B,\Pi_T^B).
\]

Then apply the same later disturbance and estimate a finite-horizon viable reachable set or recovery metric.

The key comparison is:

\[
\mathcal R_{V,T}^{A}\neq\mathcal R_{V,T}^{B}.
\]

### Killer ablation

Remove history's effect on the transition architecture:

\[
\Pi_{t+1}=P(\Pi_t)
\]

or otherwise set:

\[
H_t\nrightarrow R_{t+1}.
\]

If the future viability difference disappears under this ablation, the experiment has isolated the candidate relation-history-transition mechanism.

If it does not disappear, the proposed mechanism has not been isolated.

### Stronger benchmark

A stronger version must compare against an augmented non-relational baseline containing all measurable constituent/history variables.

The RVT formulation earns value only if explicit relational structure improves prediction, explanation, or control beyond that baseline.

## 19. Cross-domain sequence after RVT-MIN-001

Only after the minimal mechanism is interpretable should the program widen.

Suggested sequence:

1. **RVT-MIN-001:** reduced deterministic exchange/history model.
2. **Membrane benchmark:** state-dependent exchange and receptivity with endogenous boundary update.
3. **Fusion-style benchmark:** current output/ignition versus basin depth, recovery, and host preservation.
4. **Development/network benchmark:** capability migration, reproductive configuration, recoupling after structural change.
5. **Lucian OS / human-AI benchmark:** capability increase, bounded authority, correction, revocation, and admissible rerouting.

No single domain is allowed to define RVT by itself.

Convergence across independently operationalized domains would be stronger evidence than success in one favored implementation.
