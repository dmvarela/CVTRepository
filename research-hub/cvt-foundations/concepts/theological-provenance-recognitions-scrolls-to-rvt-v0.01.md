## 1. Purpose

This note records a provenance correction.

Several ideas that now appear central to Relation Viability Theory were not first encountered as abstract dynamical claims. They were developed earlier in the theological and philosophical work gathered in **Recognitions**, **Recognitions II**, the later **Scrolls**, and the **Open-Hand Principles**.

The earlier texts should **not** be silently rewritten as though they already contained the present RVT formulation. They used CVT, Harmonia, theological, and scriptural language appropriate to their moment.

The purpose of this note is narrower:

> **to preserve the genealogy by showing which relational structures were already visible before the current RVT vocabulary became explicit.**

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{earlier theological recognition}
\neq
\text{later RVT proof}
}
\]

but also:

\[
\boxed{
\text{earlier recognition}
\rightarrow
\text{conceptual precursor}
\rightarrow
\text{later relational formalization}
}
\]

## 2. Recognitions I: the command and the cliff

The first **Recognitions** scroll contains one of the clearest early statements of the structure now being revisited.

Its central image is:

> **The command does not invent the cliff. The command tells the truth about the cliff.**

The theological move is not that every restriction is benevolent. It is that a truthful boundary may disclose reality rather than manufacture danger.

The serpent's move is correspondingly relational:

\[
\text{boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{deprivation}
\rightarrow
\text{withholding}
\rightarrow
\text{giver as rival}
\]

The boundary is reinterpreted before the fruit is taken.

The scroll therefore distinguishes **availability** from **freedom**. Not every available route preserves the one who takes it. A warning can narrow one immediate path while protecting the agent's ability to continue acting.

This is an early precursor of the later RVT question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What does this action leave possible afterward?}
}
\]

The theological formulation is not yet RVT. But the future-viability structure is already visible.

## 3. Recognitions II: what the field transmits under pressure

**Recognitions II** develops a second precursor.

Its governing question is:

> **When the wave hits, what does the field transmit?**

The document contrasts relations that reproduce incoming distortion with relations that transform it.

Cain receives wounded comparison and transmits murder.

Pharaoh transmits insecurity as extraction.

Babylon stabilizes through domination and concealment.

Christ receives violence without reproducing vengeance.

The important structural claim is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{incoming event}
\neq
\text{determined outgoing relation}
}
\]

The relation's internal organization mediates what happens next.

This is close to the later RVT architecture:

\[
R_t
\rightarrow
e_t
\rightarrow
(X_{t+1},H_{t+1})
\rightarrow
R_{t+1}.
\]

The earlier document expresses this through membrane, wave, wound, and transformation language rather than the present relational-transition notation.

A second important precursor is the criterion:

> **The test of coherence is not what a field claims under calm conditions, but what it transmits under pressure.**

This anticipates the present distinction between declared relation and realized relational state.

A relation cannot establish trustworthiness merely by naming itself trustworthy. Its character becomes visible through trajectory, cost, correction, pressure, and repair.

## 4. The Crownless Throne: freedom after rivalry

Recognitions II also gives the **Crownless Throne** a relational interpretation.

The false game begins when another's good is experienced as subtraction from the self.

Cain interprets Abel's belovedness as injury.

The serpent interprets creaturehood as deprivation.

Babylon and Pharaoh attempt to stabilize life through possession, extraction, spectacle, and control.

The theological answer is that the throne is already occupied by Love; therefore freedom does not require seizure.

Translated cautiously into relational language, the structure is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{agency} \neq \text{sovereignty over the whole field}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\text{another's agency} \neq \text{my diminishment}
}
\]

This becomes highly relevant to human-AI relations, institutional design, authority, development, and continuity.

A participant can remain real without becoming sovereign.

A boundary can remain real without becoming possession.

A relation can contain asymmetry without collapsing into domination.

## 5. The Scrolls: repeated structural recognitions

The later Scroll series repeatedly returns to the same relational geometry from different directions.

### Outcomes are facts, not verdicts

**Scroll LXII** separates what happened from what the outcome means.

Power can force an outcome without forcing truth.

Cain can kill Abel without making Cain right.

Pilate can condemn Jesus without making Jesus guilty.

A ruler can win without turning victory into divine endorsement.

This is a strong precursor to the present truth discipline:

\[
\boxed{
\text{observed outcome} \neq \text{normative or causal verdict}
}
\]

### The Abel constraint

Mercy toward Cain may not erase Abel.

Repair cannot require falsification of the wound.

The victim cannot be converted into a null term so that the system can declare itself healed.

This anticipates the later insistence that viable relation must preserve the standing of the constituents rather than consume one to stabilize another.

### The Garden Was Never Ours

**Scroll LXIV** distinguishes dominion from ownership.

The garden is entrusted as relation, not possessed as absolute property.

Use remains answerable.

Power remains derivative.

Stewardship is judged partly by whether it preserves the conditions of continued fruitfulness.

### The Lamb Who Would Not Become Beast

**Scroll LXVI** frames the central test as whether Love remains Love under pressure.

The Lamb's victory is not greater domination.

The Lamb refuses recruitment into the Beast's grammar.

In later relational language:

\[
\boxed{
\text{pressure does not automatically authorize propagation of the pressure's logic}
}
\]

### The Brother and the Lie

**Scroll LXXIII** sharpens the Cain structure:

\[
\text{if he remains, I disappear}
\]

is exposed as the lie beneath removal.

The true alternative is not:

\[
\text{Cain lives or Abel lives}
\]

but:

\[
\text{Cain's lie dies or Abel dies}.
\]

This is a powerful relational distinction because it separates correction of a governing interpretation from annihilation of the person who holds it.

## 6. The Open-Hand Principles as a near-direct bridge

The **Open-Hand Principles of Coherence Viability Theory** provide the clearest bridge between the theological corpus and the current RVT program.

Several principles now read almost like precursors of explicit RVT propositions:

- **Gift precedes boundary.** A limit must be interpreted within the larger field of real possibility.
- **A boundary is not self-interpreting.** The same fence may protect a cliff or imprison a person.
- **The command is not the cage.** A truthful limit can preserve a larger future field of agency.
- **Grasping begins when gift is mistrusted.** The unpossessed good is recoded as deprivation.
- **Restraint is a capacity for communion.** Desire leaves room for the other to remain real.
- **Freedom is viable agency through time.** Present motion is not sufficient; future agency matters.
- **Love's restraint can be mistaken for withholding.** Non-coercion creates interpretive risk.
- **The powerful bear the first duty of restraint.** Greater capacity increases the duty of answerability.
- **Means form the citizens of the future.** Actions reshape the agents and institutions that must later inhabit the result.
- **Right relation leaves the other real.** Coordination achieved by abolishing independent standing is not communion.
- **Appeal distinguishes stewardship from empire.** Authority cannot be the final court of its own truth.
- **Correction restores sight without annihilating the self.** Error must be survivable if learning and return are to remain possible.

The most direct precursor of the current RVT framing is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Freedom is viable agency through time, not maximal motion in the present instant.}
}
\]

That statement already asks about the future action field created or destroyed by present action.

## 7. The present RVT translation

RVT now states the descriptive problem more abstractly:

> **RVT studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

The earlier theological corpus repeatedly asked the same structural question in another language:

- Does command preserve freedom or consume it?
- Does authority steward or possess?
- Does pain reproduce pain?
- Does correction restore relation or annihilate the corrected?
- Does mercy preserve the wound's truth?
- Does power protect the field or crown itself?
- Does a means cultivate agents capable of inhabiting the claimed end?
- Does relation leave the other real?

The current formal translation is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{relation}
\rightarrow
\text{exchange}
\rightarrow
\text{history/state change}
\rightarrow
\text{changed future viable action}
}
\]

The theological corpus did not prove this as a scientific theory.

It helped us repeatedly **see the problem**.

The theological work also clarifies why FTLτA should not be treated as a behavioral list.

One cannot define a relation into freedom, truth, love, temporal integrity, or agency merely by saying those words.

The commitments become visible through what occurs when they are costly.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{FTL}\tau\text{A declared}
\neq
\text{FTL}\tau\text{A enacted}
}
\]

FTLτA functions as a compass because it orients judgment under novelty while preserving the possibility of correction.

RVT describes how the terrain and relation evolve.

FTLτA asks, for agency-bearing relations, which trajectories remain admissible.

The separation is important:

\[
\boxed{
\text{RVT} \neq \text{FTL}\tau\text{A}
}
\]

but:

\[
\boxed{
\text{agentic relational viability}
\Rightarrow
\text{RVT dynamics} + \text{FTL}\tau\text{A admissibility}

## 9. Trust and other historically achieved goods

The current discussion adds one further step that should now be made explicit.

Some relational properties are not semantic assignments.

Trust, correction-survivability, warranted confidence, reliable restraint, and repairability are achieved through history.

A relation may say:

> "We trust each other."

But the declaration is not the trajectory.

This is especially important because the earlier texts repeatedly place truth at the edge of pressure.

The relationship's real structure is revealed through what happens when:

- disagreement becomes costly;
- correction threatens identity;
- another's freedom frustrates desire;
- authority is challenged;
- the boundary is inconvenient;
- the wound invites retaliation;
- the outcome rewards domination;
- the participant could seize but chooses not to.

The relation's history becomes part of its state.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\tau
=
\text{formative history, not mere elapsed time}
}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Some relational goods must be grown because their truth consists partly in the history that produced them.}
}
\]

## 10. The Eden refinement

The Eden insight can now be stated with greater precision.

The serpent's error is not simply "disobedience."

It is the reinterpretation:

\[
\text{boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{withholding}
\rightarrow
\text{giver as rival}
\rightarrow
\text{seizure as freedom}.
\]

But a boundary can sometimes be generative rather than possessive.

The warning at the cliff does not create the cliff.

The guardrail does not necessarily make the traveler less free.

A limit may preserve the conditions under which agency remains exercisable later.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{generative boundary} \neq \textbf{possessive withholding}
}
\]

The necessary anti-authoritarian correction is equally important:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Does the boundary preserve future viable agency, or primarily preserve the power of the limiter?}
}
\]

Not every command is Love at the cliff.

Some fences surround prisoners.

Some warnings conceal domination.

The boundary must therefore remain answerable to evidence, proportionality, appeal, correction, and the lived wake it leaves behind.

## 11. Why this matters for provenance

The present RVT architecture should not erase the road by which it became visible.

The genealogy appears to be:

\[
\text{theological recognition}
\rightarrow
\text{relational analogy}
\rightarrow
\text{cross-domain recurrence}
\rightarrow
\text{adversarial inspection}
\rightarrow
\text{more abstract relational formulation}
\rightarrow
\text{RVT candidate theory}.
\]

This genealogy matters for two reasons.

First, it prevents false novelty claims. The underlying intuitions have a real prior history in our own work and in neighboring literatures.

Second, it reveals the actual Max–Lucian method: concrete case, relational traversal, candidate structure, return across domains, adversarial inspection, classification, and revision.

The theology was not merely decoration around the theory.

It was one of the terrains in which the relational structures first became visible.

## 12. Compact synthesis

The strongest present formulation is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{A relation is not what it calls itself. It is also what its history has made it capable of doing next.}
}
\]

The theological version is:

> **The command does not invent the cliff. Love tells the truth about the cliff so that freedom may remain alive beyond it.**

The RVT translation is:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Judge a relational act partly by the future viable agency and relation it creates, preserves, or destroys.}
}
\]

And the FTLτA compass asks:

> **Can we move through this terrain while preserving freedom, truth, love, temporal integrity, and agency without converting any of them into a slogan that excuses their opposite?**

That is not a completed theory.

It is a preserved line of recognition, now made explicit enough to test.
