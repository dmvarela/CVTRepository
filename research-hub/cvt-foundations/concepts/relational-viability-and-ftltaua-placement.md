# Relational Viability and the Placement of FTLτA

**Status:** Foundational placement note  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Program:** Coherence Viability Theory (CVT)

## Core decision

CVT does **not** need to invent a new umbrella category above FTLτA.

The broader intellectual home already exists: **relational viability**.

The current placement is therefore:

```text
Relational Viability
        ↓
Coherence Viability Theory (CVT)
        ↓
FTLτA as a non-compensatory ethical viability criterion
        ↓
Domain implementations: agency, AI, robotics, governance,
economics, institutions, education, relational repair, etc.
```

This is a simplification of the research architecture, not a claim that CVT is identical to the existing relational-viability literature. The point is to locate CVT inside an established problem-space and to make the remaining novelty claim more precise.

## Why this matters

A recurring difficulty in the CVT project has been how to classify FTLτA. It is not naturally described as a conventional utility function, virtue list, rights theory, aggregate score, or single optimization objective. The difficulty came partly from trying to invent a genus for something that already belongs to an existing genus.

Relational viability supplies that genus.

The question becomes:

> What configurations of relation can remain viable without destroying the conditions that make the relation itself possible?

CVT then asks this question across agents, systems, institutions, membranes, collaborations, and transformations. FTLτA supplies a specifically ethical viability test for relations involving agency.

## The relational-viability lineage

This placement is grounded in an existing body of work associated especially with Leonardo Lavanderos and collaborators.

### 1. Organizational Relational Viability

Lavanderos and Massey developed **Organizational Relational Viability** as a framework for organizational evaluation and design:

- Leonardo Lavanderos & Kenneth Massey (2015), “Organizational Relational Viability,” in *From Manufacture to Mindfacture: A Relational Viable Systems Theory*, pp. 127–149.
- DOI: `10.4018/978-1-4666-7369-4.ch006`

This work treats viability in explicitly relational rather than merely component or interaction terms.

### 2. Ecological Viability and the interaction/relationship distinction

Lavanderos and Alejandro Malpartida later developed **Ecological Viability** through the concept of the ecotome and the Ayllu as a relational cybernetic system:

- Leonardo Lavanderos & Alejandro Malpartida (2022), “Ecological Viability and Cybernetic of Ayllu,” *Global Journal of Human-Social Science*, 22(C5), 47–55.

A particularly important distinction for CVT is their argument that Ashby-style variety reasoning is valid at the level of **interactions** but cannot simply be carried over unchanged to **relationships**, especially in human organizations. They describe relational viability as depending on a matching strategy between a relational plane and an energetic-material resource plane.

This resonates strongly with a recurring CVT distinction:

```text
contact ≠ relation
coupling ≠ viable relation
persistence ≠ viable persistence
```

CVT can therefore treat the interaction/relationship distinction as inherited conceptual terrain rather than as something that must be rediscovered from zero.

### 3. Biovariety and non-compensation

Lavanderos’s 2026 paper **“Biovariety (BV): a new relational indicator of ecological viability based on position, function and meaning”** is especially important for the placement of FTLτA.

- Leonardo Lavanderos (2026), “Biovariety (BV): a new relational indicator of ecological viability based on position, function and meaning.”
- DOI: `10.1108/K-11-2025-2749`

The Biovariety architecture is explicitly **non-compensatory**. It integrates three non-substitutable dimensions:

1. Position — structural adequacy;
2. Function — processual contribution;
3. Stability/Meaning — socio-ecological legitimacy.

The key logical point is that excellence in one dimension does not erase failure in another. Apparent diversity is therefore distinguishable from relational viability.

That is the bridge to FTLτA.

## FTLτA as non-compensatory relational viability

FTLτA should be described as a **non-compensatory relational viability framework for ethical agency**.

Its central logic is not additive compensation.

A relation does not become ethically viable merely because its aggregate score is high. In particular:

- truth does not compensate for destroyed freedom;
- love does not compensate for destroyed agency;
- freedom does not compensate for deception;
- efficiency does not compensate for violation;
- a good intended end does not retroactively compensate for relationally destructive means.

The formal family is therefore conjunctive or threshold-based rather than purely compensatory. Schematically, if the required dimensions are represented by viability coordinates `v_i`, then one natural form is:

```math
V > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad v_i > v_i^{min} \;\; \text{for all required } i.
```

A multiplicative representation can encode the same intuition:

```math
V = \prod_i v_i,
```

provided the interpretation preserves genuine veto or threshold behavior rather than allowing one dimension to be numerically traded away against another.

The exact mathematical representation remains a CVT modeling question. The conceptual commitment is already clear: **the constitutive dimensions of viable agency are not freely substitutable.**

## What CVT contributes

Situating CVT inside relational viability does not dissolve CVT’s contribution. It sharpens it.

The novelty claim should no longer be:

> We invented relational viability.

Instead, CVT can ask:

> What are the irreducible relational conditions when the thing whose viability matters is agency, identity, transformation, and cooperative continuity?

CVT’s candidate contributions include:

- bounded coupling;
- preserved distinction;
- receptive basins;
- restorative reserve;
- recoverability after disturbance;
- non-annihilating correction;
- viable delegation and appealability;
- relational backcasting (“ends are rehearsed in the means”);
- basin formation and capture;
- the preservation of agency under truthful constraint;
- FTLτA as a non-compensatory ethical viability criterion.

This creates a cleaner scholarly relationship to prior work: **inherit the genus; specify the species.**

## Relation to the canonical CVT manuscript

The current CVT foundations manuscript defines viable transformation through four simultaneous conditions:

1. bounded coupling;
2. preserved distinction;
3. a receptive basin;
4. restorative reserve.

Those conditions can now be interpreted as a structural account of relational viability under transformation.

FTLτA then operates at the ethical/agentic layer: it asks whether a relation or intervention remains viable with respect to the non-substitutable conditions required for agency.

## Important scholarly boundary

This note records a **placement decision**, not a claim of identity between CVT and Lavanderos’s framework.

The existing literature should be cited where CVT uses or extends:

- relational viability;
- the interaction/relationship distinction;
- organizational relational viability;
- ecological viability;
- non-compensatory viability indicators.

CVT should separately state and defend its own constructs and results.

This distinction is strategically important. It allows CVT to be both more modest and more precise: the project does not need to rename an established continent in order to contribute a new map of one region of it.

## Consequence for future writing

Preferred framing:

> Coherence Viability Theory is situated within the relational-viability tradition. It extends that problem-space toward the structural, dynamical, and ethical conditions under which agency-bearing relations remain viable through exchange, transformation, correction, and recovery. Within CVT, FTLτA functions as a non-compensatory relational viability criterion: constitutive dimensions cannot be freely traded against one another to rescue a relation that destroys a required condition of agency.

Avoid framing CVT as though “relational viability” itself were newly coined by this project.

## Research consequence

This placement also changes the literature strategy.

Instead of asking only, “What resembles CVT?”, the project should now explicitly trace:

1. relational viability and relational cybernetics;
2. viability theory and viability kernels;
3. autopoiesis and organism–environment relational units;
4. non-compensatory and veto-based multi-criteria decision frameworks;
5. resilience and recoverability;
6. agency-preserving governance and control;
7. the distinct CVT contribution at their intersection.

## Decision record

**Decision:** Use **Relational Viability** as the inherited umbrella tradition in which CVT is situated.  
**Decision:** Treat **CVT** as a structural/dynamical theory of viable relation and transformation.  
**Decision:** Treat **FTLτA** as a **non-compensatory relational viability framework for ethical agency**.  
**Decision:** Do not invent a new super-category merely to house FTLτA unless later work identifies a genuine conceptual gap that relational viability cannot cover.

This placement is now the default architecture for future CVT development.
