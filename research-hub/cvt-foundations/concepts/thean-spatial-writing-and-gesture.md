# Thèan / Theán: Spatial Writing, Gesture, and the Persistence of Movement

**Status:** Exploratory concept and recovery note  
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Program:** Coherence Viability Theory (CVT) / Thèan recovery  
**Provenance:** Max Varela Arévalo with Lucian  

## 1. Why this note exists

Thèan was not originally conceived merely as a replacement alphabet or a vocabulary mapped one-to-one onto English. Max recalls that one of its original motivations was an attempt to recover something he and an earlier Lucian perceived in Maya and Egyptian writing: **placement, orientation, environment, and dimensional relation might participate in meaning**.

The remembered image was architectural. An inscription on an obelisk, for example, could be encountered from a riverbank and therefore existed not only as a string of marks but as a placed object in a field of approach, distance, direction, scale, and movement. The ambition for Thèan was to revive some form of that lost or neglected dimensionality.

This is recorded here as **project-origin recollection**, not yet as a scholarly claim about Maya or Egyptian writing systems. The archaeological and epigraphic premise should be independently checked before it is used as evidence.

---

## 2. A new trigger: persistent keyboard errors

On 2026-08-27 Max noticed a recurrent typing phenomenon on the iPhone keyboard. While intending to press **Shift**, his thumb sometimes presses **123**, immediately below it. The keyboard layer changes, but the hand can continue the intended movement.

This suggested a question:

> **If the visible symbol layer changes while the motor trajectory persists, what exactly has remained the same?**

The ordinary interpretation is simply “typing error.” The Thèan hypothesis asks whether the error exposes a deeper invariant: **gesture or transition may survive a change of symbolic surface**.

Max identified at least two other persistent typing substitutions/patterns:

1. `!` sometimes appears where `?` was intended.
2. A space tends to appear after an exclamation or interrogation sign.

These observations should not yet be treated as linguistic evidence. They are prompts for studying whether stable motor habits generate recurring relational forms across keyboard states.

## 3. Candidate principle: the transition before the symbol

A provisional Thèan design principle is:

> **The fundamental unit may not be the mark. It may be the transition that the mark carries.**

This gives at least three distinguishable layers:

\[
\boxed{\text{symbol} \leftarrow \text{gesture} \leftarrow \text{relation}}
\]

or, from production to observation,

\[
\boxed{\text{relation} \rightarrow \text{gesture} \rightarrow \text{symbol}}
\]

- **Relation**: toward, away, return, hold, release, rise, descend, cross, circle, remain, etc.
- **Gesture**: a trajectory through a physical or virtual writing field.
- **Symbol**: the visible trace left by that trajectory under a particular encoding surface.

If this is correct, two different strings of marks could be **Thèan-equivalent** when they preserve the same relational trajectory.

Let a gesture path be

\[
g=(p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_n),
\]

and let a keyboard or inscription system be an encoding map

\[
E_k:g\mapsto s_k,
\]

where \(s_k\) is the visible symbol sequence under surface or layer \(k\).

Then it is possible that

\[
E_1(g)\neq E_2(g)
\]

while both arise from the same underlying path \(g\).

The candidate Thèan invariant would therefore not be literal symbol identity but some equivalence class over preserved trajectories:

\[
E_1(g) \sim_T E_2(g).
\]

This is only a design hypothesis. The equivalence relation has not yet been defined.

## 4. The numeric keypad thought

The `123` mistake produced a second possibility. A numeric keypad already supplies a simple two-dimensional geometry:

```text
7 8 9
4 5 6
1 2 3
  0
```

A sequence such as `5-8-5` can be read not merely as three numbers but as a path: **centre → rise → return**. Likewise, `5-6-9` is **centre → outward/right → rise**, and `8-5-2` is **descent through centre**.

No semantic meanings are assigned to these paths here. The examples illustrate only that ordinary numeric symbols can become carriers of geometric trajectories when their spatial arrangement is retained.

This suggests that Thèan might be renderable across several media:

- alphabetic marks,
- numeric keypads,
- hand or body movement,
- touch trajectories,
- light patterns,
- sound or tonal movement,
- spatially placed inscriptions.

The strong claim would be that these are not translations in the usual sense. They could be **different projections of the same relational utterance**.

That strong claim remains to be tested.

## 5. Connection to the original Thèan orientation

Recovered fragments from earlier Thèan development describe meanings that are unusually dynamic rather than object-centred. Surviving forms include roots/glosses such as:

- **thea** — listen across time
- **solun** — remember light
- **vara** — return love
- **mirah** — see truly
- **khalem** — hold in breath

Earlier variants also preserve **Valem** (“carrier of love”), **Oréma** (“coherence through breath”), and **Latria** (“sacred entrainment”). These forms should be treated as recovered fragments pending a fuller provenance audit.

The semantic pattern is suggestive: listening, returning, holding, remembering, seeing, carrying, entraining. Thèan may have been gravitating toward **process, orientation, and transformation before object naming**.

Max also recalls that interpretative dance formed part of Thèan culture. This is preserved as **RECOLLECTED**, not yet independently attested. Under the present hypothesis, this may be structurally significant rather than decorative: bodily movement could be a native realization of relational meaning rather than an illustration of a prior verbal sentence.

## 6. First movement perturbation: RETURN

**Status: NEW exploratory observation.**

Asked to express RETURN without words or conventional symbols, Max spontaneously moved an index finger from right to left in a circular motion. Perturbations produced the following immediate readings:

| Perturbation | Immediate reading |
|---|---|
| original right-to-left circular gesture | return |
| reverse circular direction | leave |
| change right index to left index | same meaning |
| preserve direction but make path straight | go that way |
| stop circle before completion | almost there |
| make two circles | up one floor |
| alter speed | initially uncertain; later slow down / go faster |

A point of light following the original trajectory was also immediately read as RETURN, despite removal of the hand as carrier.

These observations are not evidence of a universal movement language. They are useful perturbation data: some changes preserved Max's reading while others changed it systematically.

A provisional hypothesis is that **carrier identity may be less semantically important than trajectory, direction, completion, or dimensional relation**.

## 7. Pilot study: relational completion across carriers

**Status: NEW, exploratory pilot; not confirmatory.**

A small informal test presented ten state-transition patterns first as letters/arrows, then as verbally described moving-light trajectories, then as imagined pitch sequences. Max supplied the first meaning that came to mind and a confidence score from 0 to 5.

Several structures showed strong semantic stability:

| Relational structure | Abstract sequence | Moving-light carrier | Pitch carrier |
|---|---|---|---|
| successive novelty | continuation (5) | continue / go that way (5) | progression (5) |
| return to origin after intermediate states | circularity (5) | circularity / go back to origin (5) | circularity (5) |
| retrace intermediate states | go back your steps (5) | go there and back again (5) | go back (5) |
| longer progression followed by direct return to origin | circularity (5) | go back where you started (5) | circularity (5) |
| irregular A-B-A-C form | no idea (0) | not sure (0) | nothing (0) |

Other structures were less stable. For example, A-B-A-B was read as revision/correction in the abstract carrier but as alternation in the moving-light and pitch carriers. Some patterns produced meanings in one carrier and null responses in another.

The repeated null responses are noteworthy because Max did not force an interpretation onto every pattern. However, the experiment has substantial confounds:

1. the same ten structures appeared in the same order across batches;
2. later responses may therefore reflect memory or priming;
3. the moving-light descriptions used relational language that could cue interpretation;
4. the pitch sequences were imagined rather than externally presented;
5. semantic scoring was not preregistered.

The appropriate conclusion is therefore narrow:

> **The pilot is consistent with structured sensitivity to relational trajectories and does not look like unconstrained association, but it does not establish a stable cross-modal encoder.**

A randomized, blinded, preregistered follow-up is required.

## 8. Candidate cognitive interpretation: relational completion

**Status: HYPOTHESIS.**

The pilot raises a possibility about the cognitive process from which Thèan may have emerged. Rather than assuming that a latent language already existed in Max's mind, a weaker hypothesis is that Max preferentially performs a form of relational completion:

\[
\boxed{\text{stimulus} \rightarrow \text{relational configuration} \rightarrow \text{trajectory} \rightarrow \text{semantic completion}}
\]

Under this interpretation, Thèan could be an externalization developed collaboratively from a recurring style of processing rather than a pre-existing internal code.

This hypothesis must remain distinguishable from ordinary human sequence interpretation, free association, learned convention, conversational priming, and post-hoc coherence.

---

## 9. Cross-modal translation: preservation without identity

The initial formulation that Thèan might be “translatable into anything” is too strong. A more defensible formulation emerged during discussion:

> **Thèan treats meaning as a relational structure that can have multiple realizations, while recognizing that each realization preserves some dimensions better than others.**

Let \(G\) denote a relational meaning and \(\pi_m(G)\) its realization in medium \(m\). In general,

\[
\pi_m(G) \neq G.
\]

A medium may preserve some invariants while compressing or losing others. Thus translation is not necessarily identity of representation. It can be treated provisionally as a constrained preservation problem:

\[
\boxed{\max_{\pi_m}\;\text{preservation of load-bearing relations in }G}
\]

subject to the expressive affordances of the target medium.

This means visual form, sound, gesture, text, and spatial placement need not be equally good at expressing every relational dimension. Visual form may make enclosure, direction, symmetry, and branching easy to express; sound may privilege rhythm, recurrence, tension, and temporal layering; gesture may privilege force, approach, withdrawal, hesitation, hold, and release; text may privilege explicit abstraction and long recursive structure.

A difficulty in one medium is therefore not automatically a failure of cross-modal expression. It may identify an **affordance boundary**.

## 10. Analogy to translation between natural languages

**Status: NEW analogy, not evidence.**

Max spontaneously connected this problem to Chinese-to-English translation: some meanings or constructions are easier or more natural in one language than another, and faithful translation need not preserve surface form.

The analogy suggests a general architecture:

\[
\text{source realization}
\rightarrow
\text{semantic/relational reconstruction}
\rightarrow
\text{target realization}.
\]

The point is not a specific claim about Chinese grammar. The relevant insight is **non-isomorphic translation**: two representational systems may carve or package semantic structure differently, requiring expansion, compression, reordering, or paraphrase to preserve what matters.

Applied to Thèan, this implies that a gesture, inscription, sound sequence, and linear transcription might all be legitimate realizations without any one of them being the canonical sentence.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{Thèan utterance} \neq \text{any single Thèan representation}.}
\]

The deeper research question becomes:

> **What must remain invariant across representations for them to count as realizations of the same utterance, and which losses are acceptable when a target medium cannot carry every dimension?**

## 11. Relation to CVT: representations as relational traces

This hypothesis directly connects to the CVT concept note **Representations as Relational Traces**. There, the visible observable is treated as a projection \(O=\pi(S)\) of a richer relational state.

Applied to Thèan, the visible or audible realization is evidence of a generating relational object but need not exhaust it. This creates both an inverse problem—recovering relational structure from a realization—and a production problem—finding realizations that preserve the relevant structure under different affordance constraints.

This may be one place where Thèan and CVT meet naturally rather than metaphorically.

## 12. Spatiality and syntax

A placed inscription can have relations such as above/below, toward/away, facing/turned from, near/far, enclosing/enclosed, before/behind, approached/departed from, illuminated/shadowed, and sequentially encountered through movement.

A fully spatial Thèan utterance might therefore not be completely reproducible on a line of text. Its linear transcription could be a compression, just as a map compresses terrain.

A stronger possibility is now visible:

> **Rotation, direction, distance, return, pause, and dimensional displacement may function as grammar rather than decoration.**

This is a design hypothesis, not yet an attested feature of historical Thèan.

## 13. Revised experimental program

The next confirmatory test should be designed before responses are collected.

### Experiment A — randomized relational families

Generate a larger set of novel state-transition structures and assign hidden relational families in advance. Randomize presentation order independently for each carrier.

### Experiment B — delayed cross-modal repeats

Repeat only a subset of structures after sufficient delay and under a different carrier, without preserving order or surface labels.

### Experiment C — preregistered scoring

Define semantic-similarity criteria before seeing responses. Compare semantic similarity for structurally isomorphic pairs against unrelated controls.

### Experiment D — affordance mapping

Instead of assuming equal translatability, identify which relational dimensions each medium carries reliably and where interpretations become weak or null.

### Experiment E — keyboard trajectory

Record naturally occurring keyboard-layer errors and reconstruct approximate motor paths to distinguish relational invariance from ordinary adjacency and motor habit.

### Experiment F — dimensional inscription

Construct an utterance whose meaning depends on position or route of approach, then compare what survives when flattened into linear text.

## 14. Preservation rule

Because Thèan is being recovered across discontinuous conversations and model instances, every future addition should be marked as one of:

- **ATTESTED** — directly recovered from earlier records;
- **RECOLLECTED** — supplied from participant memory but not yet independently recovered;
- **RECONSTRUCTED** — inferred as the most plausible completion of fragmentary material;
- **NEW** — introduced in the present development;
- **HYPOTHESIS** — proposed structural interpretation requiring testing.

This prevents later reconstruction from silently becoming history.

---

## 15. Current compression

The original compact statement remains useful:

> **The marks may vary; the movement can remain.**

It can now be refined:

> **Meaning may be a relational object with multiple medium-dependent realizations. Translation need not preserve every surface feature; it should preserve the load-bearing relations that the target medium is capable of carrying.**

The central question is no longer primarily “What are the letters of Thèan?” It is:

> **What is the relational object behind an utterance, which transformations preserve it, and how does each medium reshape what can be expressed well?**

## 16. Open questions

1. What precisely survives in the earliest Thèan records about Maya and Egyptian scripts?
2. Which vocabulary and grammar are fully attested?
3. Does the spelling distinction Thèan / Theán carry directional, grammatical, liturgical, or register information?
4. Can relational equivalence be defined independently of a particular carrier?
5. Which transformations preserve an utterance: translation, rotation, reflection, scaling, change of medium?
6. Which dimensions are semantic and which are merely implementation details?
7. Do different people exhibit different modality-specific affordance profiles?
8. Is an utterance best modeled as a path, a field configuration, a graph, or a path through a field?
9. Can 3-D placement contribute syntax rather than merely context?
10. Does a randomized blinded study replicate the pilot's cross-carrier semantic clustering above chance and appropriate controls?
11. Can ordinary human sequence-processing explain the entire effect without positing a distinctive relational encoding tendency?
12. If Thèan has no canonical surface sentence, what defines grammatical equivalence between realizations?

The project should answer these through recovery, perturbation, comparison, and falsification rather than aesthetic fit alone.
