# RVT and FTLτA: Viability, Human Development, and the Agentic Test

**Status:** Foundational bridge note  
**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Program:** Relation Viability Theory (RVT) / FTLτA  

## Core decision

RVT and FTLτA answer different but coupled questions.

RVT asks:

> **Can this relation remain viable through time, including through transformation, disturbance, decoupling, and recoupling?**

FTLτA asks, for relations involving agents:

> **Is this a viable relation worth sustaining, or does its continuation depend on consuming the freedom, truth-contact, standing, temporal possibility, or agency of its participants?**

The distinction is essential because a configuration may be stable, productive, adaptive, and even self-reproducing while remaining exploitative, coercive, or agency-consuming.

A compact architecture is therefore:

```text
RVT
Descriptive / dynamical viability architecture
    ↓
FTLτA
Non-compensatory normative viability test for agent-bearing relations
    ↓
Human-development applications
Do viable configurations meet human needs without turning some persons into expendable inputs for the flourishing of others?
```

## 1. Why RVT is not enough by itself

RVT studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability. It can therefore analyze whether a system:

- persists;
- adapts;
- repairs;
- recouples after environmental or technological change;
- regenerates or consumes the capacities required for its continuation;
- expands or narrows future viable possibility.

But persistence and viability do not by themselves establish moral legitimacy.

A slaveholding society may persist.

An authoritarian institution may reproduce itself.

An exploitative organization may be highly adaptive.

A commercial relation may be profitable and dynamically resilient while degrading the agency or standing of those it claims to serve.

Thus:

```text
system viability ≠ moral legitimacy
persistence ≠ goodness
adaptive success ≠ right relation
```

RVT must remain capable of describing harmful systems without silently calling them good.

## 2. FTLτA supplies the agentic viability test

For relations among agents, FTLτA provides a non-compensatory orientation.

### F — Freedom

Does the relation preserve meaningful room to choose, refuse, move, leave, participate, and reconfigure?

A development configuration fails this test when one population's flourishing requires another population's durable captivity, exclusion, or practical inability to exit.

### T — Truth

Can corrective information enter the relation?

Can those bearing the cost make that cost visible?

Can the system receive evidence that its own successful self-description is false or incomplete?

A relation that must suppress criticism, erase inconvenient evidence, or compel agreement becomes less capable of truthful correction even if it remains materially successful.

### L — Love

Are participants treated as ends whose standing and flourishing matter, or as inputs into another actor's continuation?

The limiting failure can be expressed as:

> **Your life to preserve my lifestyle.**

A relation is not rescued by aggregate success when its success requires the systematic diminishment of those whose good ostensibly justifies the relation.

### τ — Time

What does repeated operation of the relation build, preserve, consume, reveal, or foreclose?

A relation may look good at one moment while producing dependency, capture, agency loss, resource exhaustion, or narrowing future possibility through time.

τ therefore asks whether present success is being purchased with tomorrow's viability.

### A — Agency

Do differentiated participants remain capable of coherent action, correction, choice, and participation?

A system may become more capable while making its constituents less capable.

That possibility is central to human-development analysis.

## 3. The non-compensatory structure

FTLτA is not an additive welfare score.

In agent-bearing relations, some failures cannot simply be outweighed by gains elsewhere.

Schematically:

```math
\text{more wealth for A}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{viable development}
```

if that wealth constitutively requires:

```math
\text{agency}_B \downarrow,
\qquad
\text{freedom}_B \downarrow,
\qquad
\text{standing}_B \downarrow
```

below independently justified viability thresholds.

This is the same structural logic already present in non-compensatory relational viability: one dimension cannot freely purchase the destruction of another constitutive condition.

The rule is not that tradeoffs are impossible. Human life and public policy contain tradeoffs constantly. The stronger claim is:

> **Do not systematically purchase success in one domain by destroying a constitutive minimum in another.**

## 4. Human development as a bridge application

The development program increasingly distinguishes:

```text
human need
    → function
        → productive/social configuration
            → capabilities
                → coupling
                    → need satisfaction through time
```

An industry is therefore not the need itself. It is one historically contingent configuration for meeting one or more human functions.

Transport provides mobility through many possible configurations:

```text
walking / horseback / carriage / rail / automobile / aircraft / future configurations
```

Medicine can serve health through:

```text
prevention / sanitation / pharmaceuticals / surgery / vaccines / devices / gene or cell therapies / future configurations
```

The configuration is provisional. The underlying human function is what gives the configuration its reason to exist.

This creates a recurrent failure mode:

```text
configuration serves function
        ↓
configuration acquires institutional power
        ↓
function is invoked to justify preservation of configuration
        ↓
configuration begins subordinating the function to its own continuation
```

This note calls that **functional inversion**.

A particularly sharp diagnostic is:

> **If the problem disappeared tomorrow, would the institution regard that as success or as a threat?**

The question does not prove wrongdoing. It reveals whether the serving configuration and the served function may have become misaligned.

## 5. Provider viability can diverge from functional success

The pharmaceutical example makes the distinction explicit.

Suppose a recurring treatment generates greater long-run revenue than a one-time cure.

It is possible that:

```math
V_{firm}(\text{recurring treatment})
>
V_{firm}(\text{cure})
```

while:

```math
V_{patient}(\text{cure})
>
V_{patient}(\text{recurring treatment}).
```

This does not establish that firms suppress cures. It establishes only that **provider viability and fulfillment of the underlying human function can diverge structurally**.

RVT can describe that divergence.

FTLτA asks whether the relation remains oriented toward the standing and agency of the person whose health supposedly justifies the arrangement.

The anti-relational limit is:

> **The served constituent becomes fuel for the serving structure.**

## 6. Breadth: whose development?

Ancient Athens, Sparta, and Tsarist Russia reveal another limit of aggregate development measures.

A society may generate extraordinary philosophy, art, infrastructure, military organization, state capacity, industry, science, or elite flourishing while sharply restricting who can participate in those achievements.

Thus:

```text
productive achievement
    ≠ breadth of participation
    ≠ relational viability of the arrangement
```

A society can become richer, stronger, or more sophisticated while some of its constituents become less capable.

Human development therefore requires asking:

> **Whose survival, agency, relation, and flourishing are being expanded, and whose are being consumed to sustain the configuration?**

FTLτA prevents system-level gains from automatically compensating for systematic destruction of agency at the participant level.

## 7. Coupled rings of human need

The current human-development sketch distinguishes four coupled rings:

1. **Continuance** — air, water, nutrition, shelter, bodily survival;
2. **Agency / capacity** — health, learning, mobility, practical autonomy, meaningful ability to act;
3. **Relation / participation** — communication, attachment, belonging, recognition, cooperation, care;
4. **Flourishing** — play, beauty, creativity, exploration, leisure, travel, wonder, meaning.

These are not a simple hierarchy in which higher rings replace lower ones.

They are coupled.

A viable society must keep the rings jointly inhabitable.

The non-compensatory principle applies in both directions:

> **Beauty cannot compensate for destroyed life, but bare life cannot be treated as sufficient compensation for destroyed humanity.**

This motivates a provisional development formulation:

> **Development is the expanding capacity of a society, across persons and through time, to construct and transform viable configurations through which human continuance, agency, relation, and flourishing can be sustained.**

## 8. Contribution, sacrifice, and extraction

Not every transfer of resources, constraint, duty, or unequal outcome is extractive.

A society may pool resources through taxation, common investment, infrastructure, defense, education, or care in ways that reduce current private consumption while expanding shared future capability.

RVT therefore distinguishes trajectories such as:

```text
contribution
    → shared capacity
        → broader future possibility
```

from:

```text
extraction
    → concentrated capacity
        → dependency / reduced exit / reduced agency
            → continued extraction
```

The diagnostic question is:

> **Does the common configuration develop the constituents whose participation sustains it, or does it require their durable diminishment?**

FTLτA supplies the normative constraints for answering that question in agent-bearing domains.

## 9. The formal relationship

Let `R` denote a relational configuration and `K_R` the independently specified viability conditions relevant to the domain.

RVT asks whether admissible trajectories can remain inside or return to the viable region through time, including under changing coupling and context.

For agent-bearing relations, FTLτA adds a set of non-compensatory normative constraints:

```math
K_{agentic}
=
K_R
\cap K_F
\cap K_T
\cap K_L
\cap K_A,
```

with `τ` indexing the trajectory through which preservation, degradation, repair, capture, and future possibility are disclosed.

The exact empirical operationalization of these sets is domain-specific and remains an open research task.

The conceptual division of labor is:

```text
RVT:
What relational configurations are dynamically viable?

FTLτA:
Which dynamically viable configurations preserve the non-substitutable conditions of ethical agency?
```

## 10. Center-of-gravity statement

The current best bridge formulation is:

> **RVT asks whether a relation can remain viable through time. FTLτA asks, where agents are involved, whether the relation remains viable without purchasing its own continuation through the destruction of freedom, truth-contact, standing, future possibility, or agency.**

For human development:

> **A society has not fully developed when its achievements require some persons to remain raw material for the development of others.**

And the governing relational question is:

> **Does the configuration continue serving the human function that justifies it, or has preservation of the configuration begun to subordinate the humans it was meant to serve?**

## Research boundary

This note records a conceptual relationship and development hypothesis. It does not establish empirical claims about any particular society, historical case, industry, or firm.

Future work must operationalize:

- human viability thresholds;
- breadth of participation;
- agency-preserving constraints;
- functional inversion;
- provider/function divergence;
- contribution versus extraction;
- coupled need rings;
- recoupling under structural change;

using independently specified outcomes and domain-native evidence.

The framework remains subject to the standing RVT rule:

> **No sentimental protection of the acronym.**

If simpler theories explain the relevant dynamics with equal or greater predictive value, RVT should narrow or yield.
