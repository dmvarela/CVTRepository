# RVT Center of Gravity: Relations That Reproduce or Consume Their Own Viability

**Status:** Foundational concept note  
**Date:** 2026-09-10  
**Program:** Relation Viability Theory (RVT)  
**Historical label:** Coherence Viability Theory (CVT)

## Core claim

Relation Viability Theory studies **how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability**.

Its central question is not whether a relation is coherent, harmonious, stable, or merely persistent. It is whether ongoing interaction preserves, regenerates, repairs, consumes, or destroys the capacities from which viable relation must continue to arise.

A compact formulation is:

> **RVT asks whether a relation is growing the constituents and capacities from which healthy relation can continue, or quietly consuming them while appearing to succeed.**

## Why relation, not coherence

The program's prior name, **Coherence Viability Theory**, created an unnecessary definitional burden because coherence is not the theory's principal object of study.

A relation may be highly coherent and still be coercive, extractive, destructive, or agency-consuming. A viable relation may include disagreement, correction, asymmetry, conflict, adaptation, and temporary instability.

Therefore:

```text
coherence ≠ viability
agreement ≠ viability
persistence ≠ viability
stability ≠ regenerative durability
```

Coherence may remain a domain-specific variable or observable. It is no longer the primitive concept named by the theory.

## Maintaining a relation versus a relation maintaining itself

A central RVT diagnostic is:

> **How do we distinguish a relation being maintained from a relation maintaining itself?**

A relation may appear stable because coercion, hidden reserve consumption, compensation, or continuous external effort holds it in shape. This is **maintenance dependence**, not necessarily cultivated relational capacity.

Healthy maintenance is not zero maintenance. The relevant question is whether maintenance preserves, develops, or restores the capacities that make future viable relation possible, rather than merely substituting for their erosion.

## Regenerative and extractive trajectories

Two relations can produce the same observable outcome at time `t` while changing future viability in opposite directions.

Schematically:

```math
Y_A(t)=Y_B(t)
```

while:

```math
\Delta C_A > 0,
\qquad
\Delta C_B < 0,
```

where `C` denotes constitutive relational capacities such as trust, agency, legitimacy, restorative reserve, identity-preserving organization, receptivity, recoverability, or other domain-specific conditions.

Relation A may be regenerative:

```text
interaction
    → capacity / trust / agency / reserve
    → wider future relational possibility
```

Relation B may be extractive:

```text
interaction
    → proximate output
    → capacity depletion / agency loss / reserve consumption
    → narrower future relational possibility
```

Both may look successful now. Only one is reproducing the conditions from which viable relation can continue.

## Relation viability as changing topography

Let `x_i` denote the state of participant or process `i`, and `L` the viability landscape within which interaction occurs:

```math
\dot{x}_i=f_i(x_i,x_{-i},L).
```

RVT emphasizes that the landscape need not be fixed:

```math
\dot{L}=g(L,x_1,\ldots,x_n).
```

Participants move through the landscape, but repeated interaction can also rewrite it.

Thus history is not merely a path **over** a landscape. Interaction may deepen, flatten, fragment, repair, redirect, or capture the landscape itself.

A compact RVT intuition is:

> **The relation writes the landscape on which its next act will occur.**

## The future-viability question

RVT is concerned not only with present viability but with what present interaction does to the **future viability landscape**.

Two systems may both be viable now:

```math
V_A(t)=V_B(t)=1,
```

while one cultivates future viability,

```math
\frac{d\mathcal V_A^{future}}{dt}>0,
```

and the other consumes it,

```math
\frac{d\mathcal V_B^{future}}{dt}<0.
```

This links multiple research threads that might otherwise look unrelated:

- poisoned wells;
- cultivation versus extraction;
- restorative reserve;
- trust decay and trust repair;
- relational backcasting;
- non-annihilating correction;
- viable delegation and appealability;
- development basins;
- reproductive capability;
- basin capture and recoverability;
- preservation of agency under constraint;
- maintenance dependence;
- path dependence and hysteresis.

Their common structural problem is that **present outputs can be produced either by reproducing or by consuming the conditions of future relation**.

## Durability is not mere persistence

RVT must not identify durability with duration.

Harmful relations can persist for long periods. Coercive institutions, exploitative organizations, abusive relations, and captured systems may reproduce themselves while degrading the viable participation of their constituents.

A healthy durable bond therefore means something more demanding:

> **the capacity of a relation to continue, transform, correct, recover, or where appropriate separate without systematically destroying the constitutive viability of its participants and relational field.**

Accordingly:

```text
continuation ≠ viability
compliance ≠ agency
persistence ≠ health
stability ≠ regenerative durability
```

## Role of time

Time is essential because regenerative and extractive relations may be observationally similar at one moment.

A relation can produce cooperation, stability, output, or apparent harmony while consuming trust, reserve, legitimacy, or agency beneath the visible outcome. Through time, their trajectories separate.

The relevant question is not only:

> What outcome exists now?

but:

> What did producing this outcome do to the conditions from which the next outcome must be generated?

## Intellectual placement

RVT is situated within the broader **relational-viability tradition** rather than claiming relational viability as a new category.

```text
RELATIONAL VIABILITY
Broad inherited problem-space and literature tradition

    RELATION VIABILITY THEORY (RVT)
    Dynamics and geometry of relation viability under transformation:
    how viability is preserved, regenerated, consumed, damaged,
    repaired, routed, and carried through time

        FTLτA
        Non-compensatory normative framework for agency-bearing relations
```

The terminological proximity is deliberate but requires care. **Relation Viability Theory** is the name of this research program; **relational viability** is a broader inherited scholarly problem-space.

## Falsification and scholarly boundary

The existence of a coherent research question does not establish the truth, novelty, or universality of RVT's candidate mechanisms.

RVT must still demonstrate explanatory, predictive, or design value beyond established relational viability, viability theory, resilience, autopoiesis, dynamical systems, control theory, and domain-native approaches.

The project therefore retains the rule:

> **No sentimental protection of the acronym.**

RVT survives only to the extent that it contributes a clearer, testable account of relational transformation, landscape deformation, regenerative versus extractive dynamics, path dependence, recoverability, or future viability.

## Center-of-gravity formulation

The current best concise statement is:

> **RVT studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

Expanded:

> **RVT studies whether relational processes reproduce, restore, or deepen the constituents required for healthy durable relation, or instead consume those constituents while maintaining an apparently successful state.**

This is the current center of gravity of the active research program.
