# Paper F — Capability Graduation and Productive Supersession

## Working manuscript

**Title:** *From Protection to Productive Supersession: Capability Graduation in a Changing Production Network*  
**Author:** Daniel Varela Arévalo  
**Main source:** `main.tex`  
**Status:** first full working draft; not submission-ready.

## Core question

When does temporary support convert an initially nonviable productive relation into durable unsupported capability, and what happens to that capability when the original productive configuration later exits?

The paper studies:

`activation -> capability accumulation -> graduation -> generativity -> supersession / de-accretion`

## Current formal spine

1. **Pure-complementarity activation gate** using a monotone activation operator. This is a restricted formation device, not a general equilibrium production-network model.
2. **Capability law**
   
   `q_(t+1) = (1-delta) q_t + lambda`.
3. **Graduation feasibility:** for `0 < delta < 1` and `q_0 < q*`, finite graduation is impossible when `lambda/delta <= q*` and possible when `lambda/delta > q*`.
4. **Minimum support duration** in closed form when graduation is feasible.
5. **Network-conditioned comparative static:** a productive environment that raises capability conversion and/or lowers the unsupported threshold cannot lengthen the minimum support horizon in the constant-environment comparison.
6. **Unsupported dependency closure:** removes false graduation inherited from neighbors that remain nonviable after extraordinary support is withdrawn.
7. **De-accretion trigger:** exit of a sufficiently important buyer can push a supplier below minimum viable demand.
8. **Graduation-frontier contraction:** loss of productive complements can shrink the set of relations that are locally graduable.
9. **Productive supersession:** configuration-specific capability may die while recombinable capability transfers into unsupported successor relations.

## Contribution boundary

The manuscript does **not** claim a general theory of industrial policy or development. Major neighboring mechanisms belong to established literatures: Big Push / coordination, Hirschman linkages, learning-by-doing and infant-industry theory, East Asian export discipline, evolutionary economics / creative destruction, economic complexity, GVC upgrading, and production networks.

The intended seam is narrower: connect temporary support to an explicit capability state, evaluate graduation against support-free dependencies, and make the fate of capability after exit part of the developmental diagnosis.

## Diagnostic cases in the draft

- **South Korean footwear:** used to show why sectoral decline alone cannot establish either failure or successful supersession; actual capability-transfer channels must be traced.
- **FATE / Pampa Energía synthetic rubber, Argentina (2026):** used as a candidate observed de-accretion edge: loss of a major downstream buyer contributed to loss of viability in an upstream synthetic-rubber line. This is not presented as proof that FATE should have been protected or as a complete welfare evaluation.

## Deliberately outside Paper F

The following are currently reserved for later work rather than added to F:

- strategic capability / national-security option value;
- domestic capability versus trusted international embeddedness;
- rare-earth and coercive-dependence problems;
- oil/mining-led structural transformation and Vaca Muerta;
- coordination latency and opportunity-conversion capacity;
- Canada / Argentina strategic infrastructure comparison.

Those questions are candidates for Paper G.

## Immediate stress tests

1. Verify the unsupported-dependency-closure operator against counterexamples with multiple fixed points.
2. Stress-test the de-accretion result under substitution and alternative buyers.
3. Decide whether the graduation frontier adds enough analytical value to keep in the paper.
4. Conduct a deeper literature audit on capability transfer, redeployment, industry life cycles, and evolutionary economics before making novelty claims around supersession.
5. Replace contemporary-media citations in the FATE case with primary/company/government documentation where available.
6. Compile and inspect the LaTeX manuscript; then record build status and source hash before registering it as canonical.
7. Run a hostile referee pass before expanding the model further.

## Strong working claims

> **Time under protection is not learning.**

> **Industrial death is not capability death.**

> **Industrial survival is not developmental success.**

> **Development succeeds when productive capability can outlive the productive configurations that created it.**
