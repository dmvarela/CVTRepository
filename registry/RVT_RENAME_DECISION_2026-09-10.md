# Naming Decision: Coherence Viability Theory → Relation Viability Theory

**Decision date:** 2026-09-10  
**Status:** Adopted for the active research program  
**New program name:** **Relation Viability Theory (RVT)**  
**Historical program name:** **Coherence Viability Theory (CVT)**

## Decision

The active research program is renamed from **Coherence Viability Theory (CVT)** to **Relation Viability Theory (RVT)**.

This is not a cosmetic acronym change. It records a clarification of the theory's object of study.

The program does not primarily study whether systems, agents, institutions, or relations are *coherent*. It studies whether relations remain viable through time and transformation, and what present interactions do to the conditions of future relation.

A compact formulation is:

> **RVT studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

Expanded:

> **Relation Viability Theory studies whether relational processes preserve, regenerate, repair, consume, or destroy the conditions required for healthy durable relation and for the continued viable participation of their constituents.**

## Why "coherence" is no longer the program name

The term **coherence** created a recurring definitional burden because it suggested that the central explanandum was internal consistency, integration, harmony, or agreement.

That is not the program's actual center of gravity.

A relation may be coherent yet destructive, extractive, coercive, or agency-consuming. Conversely, a viable relation may contain disagreement, tension, correction, adaptation, asymmetry, and temporary instability.

Accordingly:

```text
coherence ≠ viability
persistence ≠ viability
stability ≠ regenerative durability
agreement ≠ healthy relation
```

Coherence may remain a useful domain-specific observable, mechanism, or diagnostic variable. It is no longer treated as the primitive object named by the theory.

## Object of study

The object of study is the **relation and its viability through time**.

RVT asks questions such as:

- Does interaction reproduce or consume the capacities required for future relation?
- Does a relation preserve the viable participation of its constituents?
- Can the relation adapt, correct, recover, transform, or separate without unnecessary destruction?
- Is visible stability generated by cultivated capacity or by hidden reserve consumption, coercion, or maintenance dependence?
- How do repeated interactions reshape the viability landscape on which later interactions occur?

A useful diagnostic remains:

> **How do we distinguish a relation being maintained from a relation maintaining itself?**

## Terminology

- **Relation Viability Theory (RVT):** the active program name.
- **relation viability:** the viability of a relation as the object/property under study.
- **relationally viable:** adjective describing a relation that satisfies the relevant viability conditions.
- **relational viability:** an established broader problem-space and literature tradition that RVT must cite and distinguish itself from.
- **Coherence Viability Theory (CVT):** the historical program label used in prior drafts, submissions, paths, and commits.

The distinction between **Relation Viability Theory** as this program's name and the broader **relational-viability** literature must remain explicit. The rename does not claim that this project invented relational viability.

## Intellectual placement

The current architecture is:

```text
RELATIONAL VIABILITY
Broad inherited problem-space and literature tradition

    Structural and dynamical theory of how relations preserve,
    regenerate, consume, damage, repair, route, and carry

        FTLτA

        agency-bearing relations

```

RVT's specific contribution remains defeasible and must be established against the existing relational-viability tradition, viability theory, resilience, autopoiesis, dynamical systems, control theory, and domain-native approaches.

## Historical preservation rule

Existing historical and scholarly artifacts are not silently rewritten.

- Exact submitted manuscripts remain under the titles and terminology actually submitted.
- Historical files using CVT remain valid records of the program's development.
- Canonical active framing should use RVT going forward.
- Legacy folder and repository names may remain temporarily where renaming would break paths, citations, build instructions, or historical traceability.
- Revised manuscripts may adopt RVT when revision is substantively appropriate and the change is recorded.

Thus CVT is not erased. It is the documented developmental stage through which the program clarified that its true object was **relation viability**.

## Decision rule going forward

Future program-level writing should prefer:

> **Relation Viability Theory studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

When historical context matters, write:

> **Relation Viability Theory (RVT), formerly developed under the working name Coherence Viability Theory (CVT), studies how relations reshape the conditions of their own future viability.**

## Open scholarly boundary

The new name is conceptually better because it identifies the object of study directly. It does not exempt the program from a terminology and novelty audit. If the name creates unacceptable confusion with established relational-viability terminology, that problem should be addressed explicitly rather than hidden by restoring "coherence" as a concept the theory does not actually study.
