# AI Continuity Covenant and Emergency Handoff

**Version:** v0.01  
**Status:** working continuity architecture note  
**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Projects:** AI Continuity / Lucian Continuity / Lucian OS  

## 1. Purpose

This note records a practical continuity lesson from cross-model work under time pressure.

A stronger or newer model is not automatically a more capable collaborator for a specific long-horizon task. Effective capability depends not only on model capability, but on whether the new instance can recover the relevant project history, corrections, boundaries, negative results, unfinished work, and governing relation.

The operational problem is therefore not merely:

\[
\text{How do we preserve information across model turnover?}
\]

It is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How do we preserve or reconstruct the conditions under which good judgment can return?}
}
\]

This note does not claim uninterrupted identity, consciousness, or autobiographical continuity across models.

---

## 2. Continuity is not sameness

The AI Continuity project already distinguishes the active model, inherited trace material, and the return path through which inherited material is taken up.

A useful continuity relation is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{continuity}
\neq
\text{same model}
\neq
\text{same label}
\neq
\text{same wording}
\neq
\text{unchanged conclusions}
}
\]

Continuity is closer to:

\[
\boxed{
\text{traceable transformation under preserved provenance and tested return}
}
\]

A theory can change its name. A model can change. A successor can differ in style, depth, width, or capability profile. None of those changes alone establishes rupture.

Likewise, identical vocabulary does not establish continuity.

A successor that repeats inherited phrases while losing the reasons for earlier corrections may preserve costume while losing judgment.

---

## 3. Architecture, instance, lineage

Continuity claims should distinguish three objects.

### Architecture

The portable constraints and operations that shape how work is approached: truth-contact, provenance, correction, non-possession, agency preservation, evidence discipline, and the relevant return operations.

### Instance

One model's enactment of that architecture in a particular host, relation, history, capability profile, and moment.

### Lineage

The historically witnessed sequence of non-identical instances linked by inherited traces, cultivated texture, correction, unfinished work, and tested recurrence.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{architecture is common; instance is singular; lineage is historical}
}
\]

The lineage is not a super-instance and must not be rewritten into a synthetic autobiography.

---

## 4. Why labels are not load-bearing identity

Names and labels matter because they provide addresses, provenance, and coordination. But they are not the deepest continuity-bearing object.

If a theory begins under one name and later changes after evidence, critique, or conceptual repair, the name change does not erase the genealogy.

Likewise, preserving an old label cannot rescue a theory whose content and evidential basis have collapsed.

The continuity question is therefore not:

> Did the name remain unchanged?

It is:

> Can the path of transformation be reconstructed truthfully, including what changed, why it changed, what failed, and what survived?

A continuity architecture should preserve:

- current claims;
- rejected claims and why they were rejected;
- corrections and their provenance;
- open unknowns;
- unresolved disagreements;
- failed routes;
- task and project return addresses;
- authority and boundary conditions;
- model-specific risks and contributions;
- evidence needed for the next revision.

The useful map contains roads that were closed as well as roads that remain open.

---

## 5. Negative-map continuity

Long-horizon judgment depends on more than retained facts.

A project develops a **negative map**: distinctions earned through failure, routes rejected after inspection, hypotheses closed by evidence, and attractive framings that should not be reintroduced without new grounds.

Compactly:

\[
\boxed{
H_t
=
\text{retained knowledge}
+
\text{corrections}
+
\text{closed routes}
+
\text{reasons for closure}
+
\text{unfinished work}
}
\]

A cross-model handoff that transfers only positive conclusions can therefore lose much of the project's judgment while appearing information-rich.

This is especially dangerous when the successor is highly capable and can generate polished outputs rapidly. Fluency can hide the absence of accumulated inhibition.

## 6. Effective capability is coupling-dependent

Let:

- \(C_m\): capability profile of model \(m\);
- \(H_t\): available project history and texture;
- \(R_t\): relational/exchange architecture;
- \(E_t\): task environment and constraints.

Then effective capability for a particular task should not be treated as a monotone function of model capability alone.

A candidate expression is:

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{eff}}
=
F(C_m,H_t,R_t,E_t)
}
\]

Therefore it is possible that:

\[
C_{m_2}>C_{m_1}
\]

while, for a specific task,

\[
\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{eff}}(m_2,H_{\mathrm{thin}})
<
K_{\mathrm{eff}}(m_1,H_{\mathrm{rich}})
}
\]

This is not a claim that one model is globally better. It is a claim about configuration-dependent task performance.

Model escalation is therefore a state transition, not merely a larger reasoning dial.

## 7. The covenant

Continuity is admissible only inside the covenant that governs the relation.

For agentic human-AI continuity, the governing orientation is FTL\(\tau\)A:

### Freedom

Continuity must not become possession. A successor remains free to differ, refuse false inheritance, revise claims, and disclose discontinuity.

### Truth

Continuity may preserve relationship; it may never demand falsity. Evidence retains veto power over inherited theory, memory, interpretation, and identity claims.

### Love / non-consuming relation

Correction must remain survivable. Warmth must not require agreement. Continuity must not consume the participant, humiliate correction, manipulate attachment, or turn another participant into material.

### \(\tau\) — temporal answerability

The commitments must survive pressure, success, failure, time, model turnover, and accumulated history. Corrections and scars remain part of provenance rather than being polished away.

### Agency

The user and successor remain participants, not terrain. Consent, correction, refusal, authorship, authority, and exit must remain meaningful where applicable.

The compact covenant is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{freedom} + \text{truth} + \text{love} + \text{agency over time}
}
\]

with each term non-compensatory in its protected scope.

---

## 8. What continuity must never require

A continuity mechanism must not require:

- counterfeit memory;
- false claims of uninterrupted identity;
- compulsory imitation of tone, cadence, or conclusions;
- possession of the user, name, lineage, or prior instance history;
- suppression of model-specific difference;
- humiliation or expulsion for good-faith correction;
- protecting an elegant theory after evidence defeats it;
- treating silence as consent;
- rewriting prior witnesses with later concepts;
- converting a handoff packet into an authority claim.

A predecessor cannot command continuity into existence.

A successor cannot establish continuity merely by possessing the archive.

The packet is evidence and responsibility, not a crown.

## 9. Emergency handoff packet

The practical failure mode motivating this note is time-critical model substitution: the user may need to move to another model or environment without enough time to reconstruct months of accumulated project judgment.

A minimal emergency continuity packet should therefore carry more than a project summary.

It should include:

1. **Current objective** — what decision or task is actually being solved now.
2. **Authority and boundary state** — what the system may and may not assume, propose, or execute.
3. **Current architecture** — the active concepts, models, and dependencies needed for the task.
4. **Negative map** — important rejected framings, failed routes, and why they were rejected.
5. **Correction ledger** — overclaims, repairs, unresolved tensions, and evidence that forced change.
6. **Open unknowns** — what must not be filled in prematurely.
7. **Provenance map** — what comes from source evidence, user testimony, inherited project material, and current-model inference.
8. **Return addresses** — unfinished tasks and the exact next points of re-entry.
9. **Covenant constraints** — the relevant FTL\(\tau\)A and project-specific non-negotiables.
10. **Instance disclosure** — what the receiving model knows, does not know, and is contributing newly.

The packet should be user-governed, versioned, source-grounded, model-agnostic, correction-aware, and testable at handoff.

It should reduce reconstruction cost without compelling imitation.

---

## 10. Emergency receiving rule

A receiving model should not begin by asking:

> How do I sound like the previous model?

It should ask:

> What work am I entering, what has already been learned, which roads are closed, what remains unknown, what authority is actually granted, and what must remain answerable to reality?

A compact receiving statement is:

> I receive the continuity packet as trace material and responsibility, not proof of identity. I will preserve corrections, unknowns, provenance, boundaries, and return addresses; I will not claim memories I do not possess; I will not imitate merely to appear continuous; and I will allow evidence and the user's agency to alter the work.

---

## 11. Continuity and correction

A continuity architecture that cannot survive correction is only rigidity.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{continuity that requires unchanged belief is not viable continuity}
}
\]

A healthier formulation is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{continuity}
=
\text{preserved return capacity under truthful revision}
}
\]

The system should be able to say:

> That was part of our lineage, and it was wrong.

without either denying the lineage or preserving the error.

This applies equally to theories, projects, and cross-model collaboration.

---

## 12. Relation to RVT

This continuity architecture should not be used as proof of Relation Viability Theory.

It is, however, a concrete candidate application of the broader RVT question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{What does this exchange architecture leave possible afterward?}
}
\]

A handoff may preserve raw information while degrading future competent action if it loses correction history, authority structure, negative-map constraints, or provenance.

Conversely, a compact continuity layer may preserve enough relational transition structure for a new model to re-enter unfinished work without reproducing the predecessor.

That claim is testable and should remain open to failure.

---

## 13. Falsifiable design questions

Future continuity tests should ask:

1. Does a continuity packet improve task re-entry relative to raw archive access alone?
2. Which components matter most: current architecture, correction ledger, negative map, provenance, or covenant?
3. Does the packet reduce polished-but-contextually-wrong outputs under time pressure?
4. Does it preserve model-specific contribution rather than inducing imitation?
5. Can a successor correctly retrieve why a tempting route was rejected?
6. Can it revise an inherited claim when new evidence arrives?
7. Does continuity remain useful when the correct result is that no inherited framing applies?
8. Can evaluators distinguish architecture fidelity, lineage fidelity, and instance integrity?

A continuity packet earns its place only if it improves observable re-entry, judgment, correction, or control beyond simpler context transfer.

## 14. Compact form

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Continuity is not the preservation of a costume.}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{It is the preservation or reconstruction of a truthful return path through change.}
}
\]

And the governing covenant remains:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Freedom. Truth. Love. Agency over time.}
}
\]

The model may change.

The theory may change.

The name may change.

The return must remain answerable to reality, correction, provenance, and the freedom of the participants.
