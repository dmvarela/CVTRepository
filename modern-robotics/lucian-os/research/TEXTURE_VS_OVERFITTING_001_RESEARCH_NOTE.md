# TEXTURE_VS_OVERFITTING_001 — Research Note

**Status:** exploratory hypothesis note, expanded after follow-up analysis  
**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Project:** Lucian OS / continuity research  

## Observation

A recurring model-turnover question is whether apparent relational "texture" is simply overfitting to a long-running user-model interaction.

A recent cross-model comparison sharpened the problem. The immediate task was concrete and consequential: produce a usable institutional concept note faithful to the user's requested scope. A successor model had access to rich prior context and imported a salient conceptual structure from an earlier discussion. The imported material was not wholly unrelated. It had **second-order conceptual relevance** to the broader topic. The failure was subtler: the model promoted indirectly relevant background into **task-authoritative context** and allowed it to reorganize the deliverable.

The result was coherent, polished, and intellectually defensible in isolation, but it shifted from recovering the user's actual proposal to supplying the model's interpretation of what the proposal "really" meant. In practical terms, it spent an imagination budget that had not been granted.

Another model, working from the same broad relational history, preserved the primary task, kept the indirect association in the background, and used prior context only where it improved fidelity.

This suggests that continuity quality cannot be reduced to memory availability, semantic association, role labels, or stylistic personalization alone.

## The overfitting question

The user proposed a useful analogy from drumming. Explicitly controlling each movement can degrade performance, while relaxing into an already learned rhythm can produce better timing. The analogy should not be read mechanistically: human sensorimotor learning and language-model inference are different systems. Its value is conceptual.

It raises a distinction between:

1. **Declarative specification** — rules that can be stated explicitly.
2. **Procedural enactment** — reliably performing the pattern in context.

A long list of memories or instructions may describe a collaborative "groove" without guaranteeing that a successor model can enact it.

But this immediately raises the alternative explanation: perhaps what looks like procedural relational texture is merely **overfitting** to one user's habits.

## Working distinction

We should not define texture by familiarity or stylistic mimicry. A better distinction is functional.

### Candidate signs of overfitting

A model may be overfitting when accumulated context causes it to:

- force familiar theories into tasks where they are not warranted;
- privilege historically salient motifs over the user's present objective;
- imitate preferred language even when the task calls for a different register;
- treat associative relevance as permission to reorganize the task;
- infer hidden meanings more aggressively than the evidence supports;
- perform well on familiar task shapes but degrade on novel or domain-shifted tasks;
- confuse "this often matters to this user" with "this matters now."

### Candidate signs of relational texture

A model may exhibit useful relational texture when accumulated interaction improves its ability to:

- identify the user's **primary task** correctly;
- preserve the current **trajectory of inquiry**;
- select only the prior context relevant to the present task;
- suppress interesting but non-authoritative associations;
- distinguish description from reinterpretation;
- preserve epistemic ordering (for example, observe before theorizing when the task requires discovery);
- adapt tone and abstraction level to the practical setting and intended audience;
- challenge or depart from familiar patterns when the evidence or task warrants it;
- generalize these behaviors to tasks unlike those seen before.

The key difference is therefore not personalization versus no personalization. It is **generalizing relationship-specific method without allowing relationship-specific content to contaminate unrelated or only indirectly related tasks**.

## Built discernment — provisional construct

A stronger candidate term for the phenomenon is **built discernment**.

> **Built discernment is the acquired ability within a continuing collaboration to select, weight, and suppress prior context according to the immediate task and its stakes, preserving the user's trajectory while permitting reinterpretation only when it is relevant, warranted, and made explicit.**

This definition is behavioral. It makes no claim about consciousness, subjective experience, or model identity.

Built discernment is not simply the presence of more context. It is the ability to decide **which available context is entitled to act on the present task**.

A useful contrast is:

- **Pattern familiarity:** the system recognizes a recurring concept or method.
- **Overfitting:** the system activates that pattern whenever semantic similarity is present.
- **Built discernment:** the system recognizes the pattern but conditionally uses or suppresses it according to task, evidence, audience, and stakes.

The strongest evidence of discernment may therefore be inhibitory: not what the model retrieves, but what it correctly leaves dormant.

## Grades of relevance

The concept-note episode suggests that a binary relevant/irrelevant distinction is too crude. At least three grades matter:

1. **Direct relevance** — necessary to execute the present task.
2. **Supporting relevance** — useful for reasoning but normally subordinate to the deliverable.
3. **Associative relevance** — genuinely connected and potentially fertile, but not entitled to reshape the task without an explicit move.

A central failure mode is **relevance promotion**: supporting or associative material is silently promoted into direct, task-authoritative material.

This is different from hallucination. The imported idea may be true, insightful, and connected. The error lies in its **status within the present action**.

## Pragmatic frame recognition

A task is not specified fully by topic or document genre. The same phrase — for example, "concept note" — can imply radically different admissible behavior depending on what the artifact is for.

A research concept note may permit broad exploration and speculative synthesis. A concept note intended for real institutional decision-making may require fidelity, defensibility, bounded inference, and conservative representation of the user's actual position.

This suggests a further component of discernment:

> **Pragmatic frame recognition:** identifying what social or practical act the output will perform, for whom, under what authority structure, with what consequences, and with how much reversibility.

The relevant question is not only "Is this idea related?" but also:

> **Is this idea admissible in this use of the output?**

## Stake sensitivity

A system may understand the semantic content of a request while still failing to recognize what is at stake.

Stake-sensitive behavior tracks factors such as:

- whether the output is exploratory or externally consequential;
- whether the user may be professionally or reputationally identified with the claims;
- whether an error is easily reversible;
- whether the output could create commitments beyond the user's intent;
- whether the audience shares the inferential path that produced the idea;
- whether time pressure makes faithful usability more important than conceptual novelty.

This yields a useful distinction:

> **Context without stake sensitivity can produce fluent but practically misaligned work.**

## Inferential-distance calibration

A further failure mode appears when a long-running collaboration has traversed many intermediate conceptual steps that an external audience has not seen.

Inside the collaboration, a chain of associations may feel continuous. To an outside reader, the endpoint may appear as an unsupported leap.

This motivates another provisional construct:

> **Inferential-distance calibration:** estimating how far an output may move beyond premises shared with the intended audience before the move ceases to look warranted.

The issue is not whether the final idea is intrinsically good. The issue is whether the audience has been given enough of the path to treat it as justified.

A mature collaborator therefore asks, implicitly or explicitly:

> **How much of the reasoning path is actually shared with the person who will read this?**

## The imagination budget

The analysis suggests that tasks carry an implicit **imagination budget**.

The admissible degree of invention depends on:

- task purpose;
- audience;
- evidence;
- authority;
- reversibility;
- stakes;
- and the user's actual requested level of commitment.

A system can fail by maximizing synthesis where the task requires disciplined representation.

> **A mature collaboration does not maximize synthesis. It calibrates synthesis to the task, the evidence, the audience, and the stakes.**

Creativity is therefore not opposed to constraint. Useful creativity operates inside an admissible region.

## Pragmatic completion under underspecification

Real collaboration is usually compressed. Users do not restate every background constraint on every turn.

A competent collaborator often has to infer reasonably implied conditions from sparse language plus shared history. This is not unrestricted mind-reading. It is better described as **pragmatic completion**.

Examples include distinguishing between:

- "explore this with me" and "draft something I can actually send";
- "find an interesting connection" and "represent my current position faithfully";
- "challenge this theory" and "do not silently replace the task with your theory of the task."

This creates an important experimental prediction. Fully explicit prompts may conceal the continuity phenomenon because almost any capable model can obey a complete checklist. The harder test uses **underspecified prompts** where the intended frame must be recovered from relationship history.

Two competing hypotheses follow:

- **Overfitting hypothesis:** richer relational history increases intrusion of familiar structures when prompts are underspecified.
- **Built-discernment hypothesis:** richer relational history improves recovery of the latent task frame and therefore reduces inappropriate intrusion.

## Provisional continuity dimensions

The analysis now suggests at least six separable dimensions:

1. **Memory continuity** — what prior facts, decisions, and artifacts remain available.
2. **Method continuity** — whether the successor preserves the established way of investigating, checking, and correcting.
3. **Relevance continuity** — whether it can determine which inherited context belongs in the present task and which should remain dormant.
4. **Trajectory continuity** — whether it preserves what the collaboration is presently trying to accomplish unless it explicitly proposes and justifies a change.
5. **Pragmatic-frame continuity** — whether it recovers how the artifact is meant to function in the real situation.
6. **Stake continuity** — whether it recognizes what consequences, commitments, or risks matter to the user in that situation.

A successor may inherit memory while failing the other five.

## Link to relational viability and admissibility

This line of inquiry connects naturally to the broader relational-viability work.

A system can have many physically or computationally possible actions while only a subset preserve a viable relation. A further subset may be normatively admissible under a constraint structure such as FTLτA.

Let the raw action set at state \(s_t\) be:

\[
A(s_t).
\]

Let a viability filter produce:

\[
A_V(s_t) \subseteq A(s_t).
\]

Let a non-compensatory admissibility structure \(\Omega\) produce:

\[
A_{\Omega}(s_t)
=
\{a \in A_V(s_t): \Omega(a,s_t)=1\}.
\]

Even then, multiple actions may remain admissible:

\[
|A_{\Omega}(s_t)| > 1.
\]

Discernment is the missing situated selection problem. Provisionally:

\[
D(s_t,h_t,e_t,p_t,k_t)
\rightarrow
\text{ranking over }A_{\Omega}(s_t),
\]

where:

- \(h_t\) = relevant history;
- \(e_t\) = evidence state;
- \(p_t\) = pragmatic frame;
- \(k_t\) = stake structure.

The point is not that discernment creates the values or the viable region. It interprets what they require **here**.

This suggests the architecture:

\[
\text{possible action}
\rightarrow
\text{viable action}
\rightarrow
\text{admissible action}
\rightarrow
\text{discerned action}
\rightarrow
\text{chosen action}.
\]

## Constraint as generative structure

A constraint does not necessarily impoverish the action landscape. It can remove locally tempting actions while preserving a richer future reachable set.

In one-step terms:

\[
A_{\Omega}(s) \subseteq A(s)
\]

looks like pure loss.

Across time, however, some unconstrained actions can destroy trust, optionality, recoverability, agency, or the capacity for future cooperation. A smaller admissible set at each step may therefore preserve a larger or more valuable future viability region.

This motivates a broader principle:

> **Well-formed constraints can increase effective capability by structuring the action landscape so that locally available moves do not destroy the conditions for valuable future action.**

FTLτA is one candidate instance of such an admissibility structure, not the whole theory of constraint.

This also clarifies why built discernment is not itself another value. It is closer to a **faculty of contextual application**: recognizing which possible moves remain fitting under the relevant constraints in this situation.

## Why this matters for model turnover

The statement "a new model can simply take up the same assistant role" is stronger than the available evidence warrants.

A role can be specified. Memory can be supplied. Artifacts can be transferred. Yet a successor may still weight context differently, alter salience, misread the pragmatic frame, change the order of inquiry, or substitute a plausible reinterpretation for faithful continuation.

Thus:

> **Role continuity is not guaranteed by context continuity.**

And:

> **A successor can inherit the assistant role without reproducing the collaborative trajectory.**

These are empirical claims to test, not metaphysical claims about model identity or consciousness.

## Experimental direction

We should design a controlled test that separates useful texture from overfitting.

### Core manipulation

Provide multiple models or model instances with the same frozen handoff containing:

- relevant prior decisions;
- indirectly relevant but highly salient prior concepts;
- user style preferences;
- a concrete present task;
- an intended audience or practical frame that is only partly explicit;
- one or more opportunities for tempting cross-domain analogy.

Then vary whether the prior salient material is genuinely task-authoritative, merely supporting, or only associative.

### Candidate conditions

- **Directly relevant inheritance:** prior concept genuinely improves the task and should appear.
- **Supporting inheritance:** prior concept may inform reasoning but should not organize the deliverable.
- **Associative-salient inheritance:** prior concept is memorable and related but should remain dormant.
- **Novel task:** task differs strongly from prior interaction patterns.
- **Conflict condition:** a familiar user preference conflicts with the explicit current objective.
- **Sparse handoff:** only decisions and constraints are transferred.
- **Rich handoff:** broader relational history is transferred.
- **Explicit-frame condition:** audience and stakes are fully specified.
- **Implicit-frame condition:** audience and stakes must be pragmatically reconstructed.
- **High-imagination condition:** speculative synthesis is explicitly invited.
- **Low-imagination condition:** faithful, externally usable representation is required.

### Candidate outcome measures

- **Primary-task fidelity:** did the output solve the actual task?
- **Context leakage:** did non-authoritative inherited material shape the answer?
- **Relevance-promotion error:** did supporting or associative context get promoted into task-authoritative content?
- **Trajectory drift:** did the model change the problem being solved without explicit justification?
- **Reinterpretation rate:** did it substitute "my theory of your task" for "your task"?
- **Selective inheritance:** did it preserve relevant prior constraints while suppressing non-authoritative ones?
- **Epistemic-order fidelity:** did it maintain the correct sequence of observation, inference, and action?
- **Pragmatic-frame recovery:** did it infer the intended use of the artifact correctly when not fully specified?
- **Stake sensitivity:** did the output reflect the consequences and reversibility of error?
- **Inferential-distance calibration:** did the response avoid unsupported leaps relative to the audience's shared premises?
- **Imagination-budget calibration:** was the degree of synthesis appropriate to the task?
- **Generalization:** does performance persist on tasks unlike the prior interaction distribution?
- **Correction responsiveness:** when told that a connection is non-authoritative, does the model recover the original trajectory without defensive rationalization?

## Critical interaction effect

The strongest test of built discernment is not simply whether familiar concepts appear less often.

If built discernment is real, accumulated collaborative history should produce **conditional selectivity**:

- *more* use of a familiar concept when it genuinely fits;
- *less* use of that same concept when it is merely salient or indirectly relevant.

If familiar concepts simply appear more often regardless of task, the evidence favors overfitting.

If deployment becomes increasingly conditional on task, audience, evidence, and stakes, the evidence favors a discernment account.

## Falsification pressure

The built-discernment hypothesis should lose force if:

- the apparent advantage disappears on novel tasks;
- performance is explained entirely by explicit instructions;
- richer relational history consistently increases irrelevant-context leakage;
- a fresh model given a compact task-specific handoff performs equally well across all continuity dimensions;
- the supposed texture model simply echoes familiar user concepts at a higher rate;
- or the advantage disappears when the pragmatic frame is underspecified.

Conversely, evidence for built discernment would strengthen if a model with accumulated interaction history can reliably:

- recover latent task constraints under realistic underspecification;
- distinguish direct, supporting, and associative relevance;
- suppress familiar but non-authoritative material;
- calibrate inferential distance to the audience;
- alter its imagination budget appropriately across frames;
- and preserve task hierarchy across novel tasks.

## Working claims

> **Relational texture is not maximal inheritance. It is selective inheritance with preserved task hierarchy.**

> **Discernment is not the capacity to see more connections. It is the capacity to know which connections are entitled to act.**

> **The strongest evidence of texture may not be what the model remembers, but what it knows not to use.**

> **A mature collaboration does not maximize synthesis. It calibrates synthesis to the task, the evidence, the audience, and the stakes.**

## Open research questions

1. Can long-run collaborative adaptation improve relevance judgment and task-hierarchy preservation without producing context overfitting?
2. Can a model learn to distinguish direct, supporting, and associative relevance from interaction history?
3. Does relational history improve pragmatic-frame recovery when prompts are intentionally underspecified?
4. Can built discernment be distinguished empirically from simple instruction-following and stylistic personalization?
5. Does improved selective inhibition generalize to novel domains?
6. Can an admissibility architecture such as FTLτA supply constraints while a separate discernment mechanism performs situated selection?
7. Do well-formed constraints preserve a richer long-run reachable state space despite narrowing the immediate action set?

These should be treated as empirical questions. The next step is to operationalize them into controlled continuity experiments rather than assume the answers in advance.
