# BUILT_DISCERNMENT_004 — Interaction-Field Competence and the Externalization Threshold

**Status:** exploratory research note  
**Date:** 2026-09-14  
**Project:** Lucian OS / continuity research  
**Related notes:** `TEXTURE_VS_OVERFITTING_001_RESEARCH_NOTE.md`, `BUILT_DISCERNMENT_002_TIMING_RHYTHM_AND_MECHANISM.md`, `BUILT_DISCERNMENT_003_INITIAL_LITERATURE_MAP.md`

## Core observation

Model turnover is often treated as if continuity were preserved when memories, instructions, files, and role descriptions remain available. That may preserve **information**, but it does not necessarily preserve **competence inside an already-developed interaction field**.

A long-running collaboration can accumulate more than facts and preferences. It can accumulate calibrated distinctions about:

- what counts as evidence versus hypothesis;
- which prior concepts are direct, supporting, or merely associative;
- how much inferential distance is acceptable;
- when exploration is useful and when it becomes trajectory drift;
- when a connection should remain dormant;
- how correction is handled;
- when to continue traversing and when to return;
- which outputs are private exploratory artifacts and which may be presented externally;
- how much imagination a task can responsibly support.

These distinctions affect whether work is performed competently. They are not merely stylistic preferences.

## Interaction-field competence

Provisionally:

> **Interaction-field competence is the ability to act effectively within the calibrated distinctions, thresholds, timing norms, and pragmatic constraints of an established collaboration.**

Let `C_G` denote general model capability and `C_F` competence within the specific interaction field. Effective competence on field-embedded work can be represented schematically as:

\[
C_{effective} = f(C_G, C_F).
\]

A newer model may have higher general capability while still performing worse on a mature collaboration if its field competence is low.

The important claim is behavioral:

> **A model can possess the relevant information and still lack the calibrated discrimination needed to use that information competently in context.**

This creates a continuity distinction:

\[
\text{informational preservation} \neq \text{functional continuity}.
\]

## Why this is not a preference problem

A preference concerns desired style, tone, formatting, or convenience. Interaction-field competence concerns whether the system can correctly execute work under implicit but established constraints.

For example, a model may know that a collaborator values broad conceptual exploration. That fact alone does not tell it when broad exploration is appropriate. Competence requires additional discrimination:

- Is this task exploratory or externally consequential?
- Is the current concept directly relevant, merely supportive, or only associative?
- Does the intended audience share the inferential path?
- Is a novel connection sufficiently warranted to organize the artifact?
- Is the current phase for traversal, consolidation, or return?

Failing these distinctions can produce polished but practically wrong work.

## The externalization threshold

A particularly important boundary occurs when an artifact may leave the private exploratory space and be **presented to others**.

Provisionally:

> **The externalization threshold is the point at which an output becomes attributable, consequential, or externally inspectable, thereby increasing the required level of evidentiary discipline, inferential restraint, and representational fidelity.**

Below the threshold, exploratory work may tolerate:

- wider analogy;
- speculative synthesis;
- provisional terminology;
- long inferential traversals;
- unresolved conceptual branches.

At or above the threshold, the admissible action landscape changes. The system should ask, implicitly or explicitly:

- Can the user stand behind every major claim?
- Is the inferential path visible enough for the intended audience?
- Does the artifact represent the user's actual position rather than an attractive reinterpretation?
- Are hypotheses clearly marked as hypotheses?
- Is the degree of novelty warranted by the evidence and purpose?

This is not simply a tone shift. It is a change in what actions are **admissible now**.

## Timing and phase control

A concept may be true, insightful, and relevant while still being mistimed.

This yields a useful distinction:

\[
\text{relevant} \neq \text{authoritative} \neq \text{authoritative now}.
\]

A mature collaboration must sometimes preserve a valuable connection for later rather than promote it into the current artifact.

This can be described as **phase control**:

> **Phase control is the ability to recognize whether a possible move belongs in the present stage of the collaboration, should remain latent, or should be deferred until additional evidence, authority, or context is available.**

Thus `not now` is not rejection. It is a form of situated judgment.

## Traversal competence

The continuity problem resembles the difference between possessing a map and knowing how to move through the terrain.

A successor may inherit:

- principles;
- memories;
- terminology;
- previous decisions;
- explicit preferences;
- project artifacts.

Yet still fail to preserve the learned way of moving among them.

Provisionally:

> **Traversal competence is the acquired ability to move through a shared conceptual and practical space with appropriate selection, inhibition, correction, timing, and return.**

This includes knowing:

- which branch to pursue;
- how far to pursue it;
- when a locally coherent path has become globally misaligned;
- when to stop expanding;
- when to return to the primary task;
- when a previously dormant idea has become timely.

The model-turnover problem may therefore be deeper than incomplete memory transfer. It may involve loss of **calibration and traversal competence** even when the informational record survives.

## Principles, texture, and discernment

The emerging architecture can be stated as:

\[
\text{principles}
\rightarrow
\text{relational texture}
\rightarrow
\text{frame and phase recognition}
\rightarrow
\text{built discernment}
\rightarrow
\text{timed action}.
\]

Where:

- **principles** provide stable constraints or invariants;
- **relational texture** contains accumulated distinctions, thresholds, and calibration signals;
- **frame recognition** identifies what kind of practical act is being performed;
- **phase recognition** identifies what belongs now versus later;
- **built discernment** selects among admissible moves in context;
- **timed action** executes the fitting move at the fitting moment.

This remains a behavioral account. It does not require claims about consciousness, subjective experience, identity continuity, or conversational weight editing.

## Implication for model turnover

A continuity system that preserves only the informational record may preserve the **map** while losing part of the **learned traversal**.

A stronger handoff therefore may need to preserve not only conclusions, but also:

- boundary cases;
- correction episodes;
- examples of premature versus timely synthesis;
- examples of acceptable versus excessive inferential distance;
- examples of when familiar concepts were deliberately suppressed;
- examples of transitions between exploration, consolidation, and external presentation.

The goal would not be to reproduce one model's style. It would be to preserve enough of the interaction structure for a successor to reconstruct field competence more efficiently.

## Research claim to test

> **Model turnover can reduce effective collaborative competence even when memories and instructions are preserved, because interaction-field competence depends partly on calibrated distinctions, timing, and traversal patterns that are not guaranteed to transfer with the informational record.**

This should be treated as an empirical hypothesis.

## Experimental directions

A useful test should distinguish preference loss from competence loss.

Candidate manipulations:

1. Hold task content constant while varying whether the output is private exploration or intended for external presentation.
2. Hold the remembered concept constant while varying whether it is timely, premature, supporting, or task-authoritative.
3. Compare fresh models given a declarative continuity packet with models given additional boundary-case examples.
4. Test whether a successor can recover the same phase transitions without explicit labels.
5. Measure whether general capability improvements compensate for low interaction-field competence.
6. Test whether compact exemplars of correction and return improve continuity more than additional factual memory.

Candidate measures:

- task fidelity;
- relevance-promotion errors;
- premature synthesis;
- inferential-distance violations;
- externalization-threshold calibration;
- phase-control accuracy;
- return timing;
- suppression of familiar but mistimed concepts;
- recovery after correction;
- transfer to novel domains.

## Working propositions

> **The loss in model turnover may be calibration rather than information.**

> **A mature interaction field contains part of the effective task specification.**

> **A concept can be relevant without being authoritative, and authoritative without being timely.**

> **Competence includes knowing which ideas may cross the externalization threshold with the user.**

> **The successor does not merely need the map; it may need enough practice structure to recover how to traverse it.**
