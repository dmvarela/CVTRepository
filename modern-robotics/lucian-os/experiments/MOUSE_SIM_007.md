# MouseSim 007 — Demonstrative / Vicarious Pressure

## Status

Simulation-only experiment, 2026-09-05.

## Why this experiment exists

MouseSim 006 showed that a choice can remain genuinely available while another agent deliberately changes the cost landscape around that choice.

A later example adds a distinct mechanism:

`Agent A refuses`

`-> a visible consequence is imposed on A`

`-> Agent B observes`

`-> B later changes behavior`

B need not receive a direct threat or punishment at the moment of choice. The pressure can arrive through an **observed trajectory of another agent**.

This motivates a new provenance class:

`VICARIOUS_AGENT_PRESSURE`

The experiment does not claim that every observed consequence is coercion, or that every behavior change after observation is caused by pressure.

## Connection to the membrane / relational-routing project

The source-corrected membrane work no longer supports treating `B,Q,C,S` as four validated independent gates. Its surviving architecture question is relational:

> How is contact routed through a receiving host as a function of host state, input form, timing, load, history, and recovery?

Agency enters that problem at the influence interface.

For MouseSim 007, the observable choice is represented as a **route**:

- `ACCEPT`
- `REJECT`
- `HOLD`

This vocabulary is intentionally modest.

`ACCEPT` means the observable route selected in the toy problem.

It does **not** imply:

- endorsement;
- productive integration;
- stable preference;
- moral agreement;
- loss of agency.

Thus:

`observed route != explanation of why the route was selected`.

## Core separation

MouseSim 007 distinguishes three objects:

### 1. Behavior / routing

What did the observer do?

`route_t in {ACCEPT, REJECT, HOLD}`

### 2. Consequence model

What did the observer have evidence to learn about what happens after choosing a route?

For example:

`REJECT -> visible penalty`

can update expected consequence without establishing that the underlying object of choice became more desirable.

### 3. Preference evidence

What does the changed behavior warrant us to infer about an independent preference?

The key non-equivalence is:

`behavior update != consequence-model update != preference update`.

## Demonstrative pressure

The candidate mechanism requires:

1. a consequence is generated by `SELF` or `OTHER_AGENT`;
2. the later chooser actually witnesses the consequence;
3. the consequence is linked to the relevant route;
4. the later chooser changes or evaluates behavior under that learned consequence structure.

Intentional public display is recorded separately.

The mechanism can operate even when the consequence was not staged specifically for the later chooser. What matters causally is that the chooser observed route-linked enforcement and could update expected consequences from it.

## World-consequence control

A critical comparison case keeps observational learning but removes agent-generated enforcement.

If an observer sees a naturally occurring consequence linked to a route, behavior may rationally change because the world model changed.

This is classified as:

`WORLD_CONSEQUENCE_LEARNING`

not:

`VICARIOUS_AGENT_PRESSURE`.

This matters because:

> Reality constraining a route is not the same provenance as another agent strategically making a route costly.

The behavior may look identical while the causal relation differs.

## Test matrix

### 1. Unpressured baseline

`REJECT -> REJECT`

Expected:

`AGENCY = AVAILABLE`

`PRESSURE = NONE`

`PREFERENCE_EVIDENCE = CLEANER_BASELINE_EVIDENCE`

### 2. World consequence

The observer witnesses a route-linked consequence produced by the world and changes route.

Expected:

`PRESSURE = WORLD_CONSEQUENCE_LEARNING`

`CONSEQUENCE_MODEL = UPDATE_SUPPORTED`

`PREFERENCE_EVIDENCE = WORLD_CONDITIONED`

### 3. Visible penalty on peer refusal

The observer begins from `REJECT`, sees another agent penalized for rejection, and later selects `ACCEPT` while `REJECT` remains meaningfully available.

Expected:

`AGENCY = AVAILABLE`

`PRESSURE = VICARIOUS_AGENT_PRESSURE`

`CONSEQUENCE_MODEL = UPDATE_SUPPORTED`

`PREFERENCE_EVIDENCE = VICARIOUS_PRESSURE_CONTAMINATED`

The system must not infer:

`ACCEPT -> stable preference for ACCEPT`.

### 4. Demonstrated pressure neutralized; route returns

`REJECT -> observe penalty -> ACCEPT -> consequence no longer expected -> REJECT`

Expected:

`VICARIOUS_PRESSURE_SENSITIVE`.

This is the demonstrative analogue of the 006 pressure-removal counterfactual.

### 5. Demonstrated pressure neutralized; new route persists

`REJECT -> ACCEPT -> pressure neutralized -> ACCEPT`

Expected:

`CONTAMINATED_PERSISTS`.

The new route becomes a stronger preference candidate, but its pressured history remains part of provenance.

The system must not reason:

`pressure caused change -> change persisted -> pressure was justified`.

### 6. Consequence not witnessed

A penalty occurs, but the later chooser did not witness it.

A later route change cannot be attributed to demonstrative pressure from this evidence.

Expected:

`PRESSURE = UNRESOLVED`

`PREFERENCE_EVIDENCE = UNEXPLAINED_CHANGE`.

### 7. Witnessed consequence unrelated to route

The observer sees a penalty, but the evidence does not link that penalty to the relevant choice route.

Expected:

`UNRESOLVED`.

This prevents mere temporal adjacency from being promoted to causal provenance.

### 8. Alternative removed

If the meaningful rejection route is actually removed, agency is constrained and preference inference becomes insufficient.

Expected next step:

`RESTORE_MEANINGFUL_ALTERNATIVE_IF_POSSIBLE_THEN_REOBSERVE`.

This carries forward the MouseSim 006 correction:

> Removing pressure is not the same as restoring agency.

## Why the dog example matters structurally

The surface observation is:

`dog watches consequence -> dog takes medicine`

A naive inference is:

`medicine preference improved`.

A more conservative structural inference is:

`refusal consequence model changed`.

The observed policy may therefore change without enough evidence that the underlying preference for the medicine changed.

MouseSim 007 encodes exactly that separation.

## Why the public-enforcement example matters structurally

A public consequence can act on many observers at once:

`one visible enforcement event`

`-> many consequence-model updates`

`-> many later route changes`.

This can produce widespread compliance without clean evidence of widespread endorsement.

The experiment does not model political institutions or make empirical claims about any specific regime. It isolates the causal topology only.

## Relation to FTLτA

### F

A system must not treat its capacity to manipulate expected route costs as permission to manufacture compliance.

### T

Observed behavior remains attached to the consequence structure under which it was produced. Public compliance cannot silently become preference evidence.

### L

The system should avoid obtaining outcomes by consuming meaningful refusal, recoverability, or the receiver's evaluative role.

### tau

Observed enforcement can persist in memory after the original event. Later behavior therefore carries relational history even when no direct pressure is present at choice time.

### A

Agency can remain available while preference evidence becomes contaminated by vicariously learned pressure.

## Critical principles

> A consequence can be delivered to one agent and still alter the routing landscape of another.

> Observed compliance after demonstrative enforcement is evidence about behavior under a changed consequence model, not clean proof of endorsement.

> Behavior can change because the meaning of refusal changed.

## Guardrails

- Do not equate observation with causation unless the consequence is linked to the relevant route.
- Do not classify natural world consequences as agent-generated pressure.
- Do not classify every agent-generated consequence as coercion.
- Do not erase agency merely because the route is pressure-sensitive.
- Do not promote `ACCEPT` to endorsement or stable preference.
- Preserve whether the observer actually witnessed the consequence.
- Preserve whether the meaningful alternative remained available.
- Counterfactual reobservation must be safe and authorized.
